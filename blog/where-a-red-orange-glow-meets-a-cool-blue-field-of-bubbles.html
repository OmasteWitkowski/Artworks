Where a Red-Orange Glow Meets a Cool Blue Field of Bubbles

Ember and Tide is a glowing, glass-like abstract that splits warm from cool along a thin rainbow seam.

Start on the left. A bold red-orange band curves down the frame beside patches of rough, mottled amber, and it looks lit from behind.

Now follow the eye to the center. The color turns cool: a hazy blue field drawn with fine curved lines and scattered with tiny round dots like bubbles. Between the two runs a thin seam of green, yellow and blue.

What the eye finds when it stays a while

I'm not going to hand you a backstory for this piece. What it offers is already in the image, and it rewards looking.

At first it reads as three zones: warm red and orange at left, cool blue in the center, and a golden-amber panel at right. Stay longer and the surface changes from zone to zone. The blue center is smooth and glassy, with fine parallel ridges. The amber at left is rough. The panel at right carries a faint square grid.

Long arcs sweep from upper center down and to the right, and thin vertical streaks of green and blue pull your eye across the frame. Sharp, bright edges appear where the bands meet. Nothing here is a recognizable object, so the pleasure is color, light and line.

What you'll see in it

  • A red-orange band curving down the left side. It gives the image a warm, glowing anchor you can read from across the room.
  • A cool blue field of fine curved lines in the center. It sets a calm, hazy counterweight to the heat beside it.
  • Tiny bubble-like dots scattered through the blue. They give close viewers a reason to step nearer.
  • A thin seam of green, yellow and blue between warm and cool. It works like a small rainbow edge where the two sides meet.
  • Rough, mottled amber at left against a smooth glassy center. The change in surface adds depth and variety.
  • Long arcs and vertical streaks. They carry the eye down and across the frame instead of letting it settle in one spot.

The wall it's made for

This is a horizontal piece, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, bed, console or mantel, or in a hallway or office that needs a vivid focal point. The three color zones read from a distance, while the fine lines and bubbles reward a closer look.

The warm-against-cool palette pairs well with neutral walls and with rooms that already use blue or amber. The mood is energetic and vibrant. It's there for the color and light, and it needs no story to earn its place.
