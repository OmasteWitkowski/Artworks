A Rose That Looks Cast in Molten Gold

Ember Rose in Molten Gold is a single rose seen from above, its petals raised and glowing like hammered metal against a pitted, tile-like grid.

Look at the rose first. It fills most of the wide frame, seen from directly above, with its petals curling inward to a dark center.

Then look at the petals. They don't read as soft. They look raised, sculpted and hammered, with bright gold ridges and deep rust-red hollows, as if the flower had been formed from molten metal.

What the eye finds when it stays a while

The first pull is the spiral. The petals wind inward and draw your eye to the dark center. Once you reach it, smoother folds carry you back out to the right and upper right, where a curled form presses in at the corner.

The second discovery is the surface. The left and lower areas are speckled with tiny bubble-like rings and fine cracks, which give the image a pitted, granular feel. Behind the rose, faint block-like divisions break the background into rough tile-like sections.

The third is the light. Metallic-looking highlights run along the petal edges, and the shadows deepen into reddish brown. The whole rose looks lit from within.

I'd rather you find all of this yourself than be told what it means. It is a rose that looks like gold, and that alone is a good reason to want it.

What you'll see in this piece

  • Petals that look hammered and embossed, with crisp raised edges. They give the rose a sculpted, almost tactile presence that a soft floral rarely has.
  • Bright metallic highlights against deep rust-red hollows. The rose looks lit from within and holds its own across a room.
  • Molten gold, burnt orange and deep rust red, with amber and pale cream-yellow accents. One warm family of color runs from glow to shadow.
  • A pitted surface of tiny bubble rings and fine cracks. It adds grain and depth when you step in close.
  • A faint tile-like grid behind the flower. It gives the rose an almost architectural frame.
  • A top-down spiral that pulls the eye to the center. Smoother folds then release it toward the upper right.

Where it belongs

This is a horizontal piece, with the rose large and slightly left of center. That suits a wide wall above a sofa, bed, console or fireplace, or a dining or entry wall that needs a rich color anchor.

The saturated gold and rust work well against deep neutrals and dark woods. Against cool walls, the gold glows even more.

  • This is for you if you want a floral with weight and intensity, not a soft, conventional bloom.
  • This is for you if a warm, high-contrast focal piece is what your wide wall needs.
  • This is for you if you like texture you can almost feel, such as embossed ridges, pitted surfaces and metallic highlights.
  • This is for you if the look of the piece is reason enough. It needs no other justification.
