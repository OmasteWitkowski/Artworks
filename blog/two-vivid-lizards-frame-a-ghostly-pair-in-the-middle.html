Two Vivid Lizards Frame a Ghostly Pair in the Middle

Mirror Crawlers is a digital lizard triptych: two saturated mirror-image lizards filled with kaleidoscopic crimson, teal and gold, with a pale, see-through pair crossing between them.

Picture a white wall with three tall panels across it. In the left and right panels, a lizard seen from above crawls upward. Its feet are splayed and clawed, and its long tail ends in a tight spiral. The two lizards are exact mirror images, and both glow in deep crimson, teal green and golden yellow.

Then your eye reaches the middle. Two pale, see-through lizards cross there in an X, and their tails curl around each other at the bottom. After the saturated outer panels, the faded center works like a quiet pause.

What your eye finds the longer you look

The first thing you notice is the silhouette. The edges are crisp and smooth, so each lizard reads clearly as a shape from across a room.

Step closer and the inside of each body opens up. Swirling, marbled patterns repeat and reflect, like a kaleidoscope set inside the outline. Each lizard is a clear shape and also a field of detail.

Then the rhythm takes over. The S-curved bodies and spiral tails make the figures seem to crawl upward and dance. It is strong, soft, strong: two vivid lizards on the outside and a faded, overlapping pair in between. That is why it is easy to remember.

  • Thin navy lines between the panels. They keep the three parts distinct and tie the colors together.

Where it belongs

This is a horizontal, three-panel piece, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk, or along a hallway. The flat white ground and strong color blocks help it read from across the room. The dense pattern rewards you when you walk up to it.

The mood is playful, lively and decorative. The bold color has a warm energy, and it works best where you want the art to be the accent, such as a room with white or neutral walls.
