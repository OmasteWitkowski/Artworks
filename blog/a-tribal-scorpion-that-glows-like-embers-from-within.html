A Tribal Scorpion That Glows Like Embers From Within

Ember Sting is a symmetrical red scorpion with a smoky, marbled fill, cut into clean shapes by sweeping white lines on a plain white square.

Look at the scorpion head-on. Its tail sweeps up and over the body to a sharp stinger, and two big claws spread wide beneath it. A fan of jointed legs fills the lower half of the square.

Now look inside the shapes. The red is not flat. It swirls and mottles like smoke or marbled stone, and it seems to glow from within. Around it, everything else is perfectly clean white.

What Your Eye Finds, Step by Step

First you see the silhouette. It is a bold tribal-tattoo shape with tapering, pointed tips and a perfectly symmetrical layout. The wide claws form a broad base, and the tail rises above them.

Then you see the white cut-lines. Curved and tapering, they separate the claws, the tail segments and the body. The anatomy is drawn with negative space instead of dark outlines, so the creature reads instantly.

Then the fill pulls you in. Deep crimson shifts to dark maroon, bright scarlet and touches of burnt orange, like embers breathing under the surface. The white lines and background stay completely smooth, so the red looks even more alive.

Finally, the S-curve of the tail leads your eye from the body up to the stinger. With the spread claws, it gives the scorpion a coiled, ready-to-strike energy. That is why I called it Ember Sting.

What You Get to Look At

  • Curved white cut-lines carve the claws, tail segments and legs. The anatomy is drawn in negative space, so the shapes stay crisp and graphic.
  • A mottled, smoke-like red fill with soft veining. It gives a flat graphic design the depth of marbled stone.

  • Perfect bilateral symmetry on a square field with generous white margin. The piece feels balanced and confident, and it holds the wall without clutter.

Where It Belongs

Ember Sting works as a single-motif statement piece. The centered scorpion and wide white margin let it hang alone as the focal point of a wall. The square format also suits balanced, symmetrical arrangements, so it pairs well with a matching piece.

The red and white palette and the fierce, dangerous mood suit a den, office, studio or entry, anywhere you want a bold image with some heat in it.
