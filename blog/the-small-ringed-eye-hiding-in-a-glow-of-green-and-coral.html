The Library · Omaste Witkowski

The small ringed eye hiding in a glow of green and coral

Moss and Ember is a horizontal abstract close-up of smooth, translucent folds in olive green and coral, with a small bubble-like pocket at its center.

Look at the piece for a moment and the first thing you'll notice is the glow. Smooth, rounded fields of olive and moss green sit in the upper left and lower right. Between them, a sweeping ribbon of coral and salmon seems lit from behind.

Then your eye finds the small ringed pocket just right of center. It looks like a bubble or a tiny eye, and it keeps pulling you back. That's the piece.

What the eye finds when it stays a while

I'll let the image tell its own story. At first it reads as color: green against coral, cool against warm. The warmth runs orange through the pink forms, and the green turns cooler toward the edges of the frame.

Stay longer and the shapes start to move. Sweeping diagonal curves carry your eye from the lower left toward the upper right. The folded forms seem to press and flow against one another, with thin dark lines tracing where they meet.

The small ringed detail is where that movement pauses. Once you have rested there, you start to see the finer things: tiny speckles scattered over the green, and hairline creases crossing the glassy surface.

What you'll see in Moss and Ember

  • Olive and moss green against coral and salmon, with touches of amber and burnt sienna. The pairing is warm and cool at once, so it feels rich without being loud.
  • A soft glow that looks backlit, warm orange through the pink and cooler green at the edges. It gives the piece depth, like light passing through the shapes.
  • One diagonal coral band dividing two green fields. It gives the horizontal format a clear sweep and a sense of direction.
  • A small ringed pocket just right of center. It is the focal point, and it gives your eye a place to rest.
  • Smooth, glassy surfaces with a gentle gradient sheen. Fine speckles and hairline creases reward a closer look.

Where it belongs

The broad green fields and single diagonal band make this a color-led statement piece. The horizontal shape suits the wall above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The earthy palette sits comfortably beside warm wood, neutral linen and plants.

The mood is calm, warm and quietly organic. It needs no explanation, and you can want it simply because the color and light are beautiful.

  • This is for you if you love green and coral together and want a pairing that is less common in calm abstracts.
