Look Longer: Faces and Wings in a Mirrored Violet Glow

Veiled Diamond is a symmetrical digital abstract in indigo, violet and magenta, with a pink-lilac diamond glowing at its centre.

Start in the middle. A softly glowing diamond, pale pink and lilac, burns against a near-black indigo ground. Translucent ribbons of magenta, violet and royal blue fold outward from it like layered veils or petals, so the whole piece looks backlit, like glass or stained fabric.

Then notice that every curve has a twin. The image mirrors left to right and top to bottom, so the rippling movement never tips into chaos. It stays calm, balanced and a little hypnotic.

What the eye finds when it stays

I'll be straightforward: the story of this piece is what happens when you look at it. At first you see colour and glow. Then the structure shows itself: two matching motifs at the sides, funnel-like shapes at the top and bottom, and small spiral curls near the left and right edges.

Stay a little longer and the side forms begin to suggest stylized eyes, faces or wings in profile. Nothing is literally depicted. That impression is yours, and it is the reason the piece rewards a second and third look.

Thin outlines trace each fold, pointed tendrils flick sideways, and coral and pale aqua glints catch at the edges. A dark navy border holds it all in, so the glow reads as something lit from within.

What you'll see in Veiled Diamond

  • Strict four-way mirror symmetry around a bright central diamond. It gives the piece a calm, balanced pulse that suits a long look.
  • A pink and lilac glow against near-black navy. It makes the centre the focal point of the wall, with the layered shapes looking backlit.
  • Deep indigo, electric violet and magenta, with royal blue, soft pink, and small coral and aqua accents. It is rich, saturated colour, with small bright notes to catch the eye.
  • Smooth, glossy, silky gradients, with fine outlines tracing the folds and a faint mottled swirl inside the larger magenta areas. These add detail to find up close.
  • Side forms that can read as stylized eyes, faces or wings. They give the piece something to discover over time, without telling you what to see.

Where it belongs

This is a horizontal piece with a dark, saturated border, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The central glow pulls the eye there, so it can anchor the space.

The dark ground gives strong contrast against pale walls. In moody, jewel-toned rooms it blends in and deepens the palette. If you simply love rich purple and blue, that is reason enough.
