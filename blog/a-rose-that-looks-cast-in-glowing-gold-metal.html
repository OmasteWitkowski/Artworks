A Rose That Looks Cast in Glowing Gold Metal

Ember Rose is a single head-on rose that looks embossed in gold and copper, set in a grid of tiled panels.

Look at the rose first. It is seen straight on, with its petals curling inward toward a dark center. The edges of the petals shine as if lit from within.

Now look at what surrounds it. Square panels form a grid behind the flower, and some are dotted with tiny bubble-like rings. This is not the soft bouquet most rose pictures give you. It is a rose with weight, heat and a metallic glow.

What the eye finds, the longer you look

I'm Omaste Witkowski, and I'd rather point you to what is actually in the image than tell you a story that isn't. So here is what happens when you stand in front of Ember Rose.

From across the room you see the glow. Molten gold and burnt orange fill the petals, and bright cream highlights trace each raised ridge. The recesses drop into dark red-brown shadow, so the rose looks cast in gold or copper metal.

Step closer and the surface changes. The smooth, bright ridges sit against fine speckled areas of bubble-like rings in the left and lower panels, with thin cracks in the lower left. It reads as hammered, aged metal mixed with glass.

Then the movement takes over. The petals spiral toward the dark center and pull your eye in, while soft folds sweep out to the right and upper right with a slow, liquid feel. The piece is listed as digital art, and the metal look is how it appears to the eye.

What you'll see in Ember Rose

  • A single rose seen head-on, with petals curling inward. It gives you one clear focal point that reads from across a room.
  • Bright, raised petal ridges that look embossed in gold. They give the flower a sculptural, gilded presence instead of a soft, painted one.
  • A palette of molten gold, burnt orange and deep rust red, with dark brown shadow and pale cream highlights. Together they make a warm, glowing, luxurious color mood.
  • A grid of square, tile-like panels behind the rose. It gives the flower a structured, almost architectural setting.
  • Fine bubble-like rings and thin cracks beside the smooth metallic ridges. They give the surface an aged, hammered quality that rewards close viewing.
  • A lobed shape in the upper right and bubbled panels on the left. They balance the rose, which sits slightly left of center.

Where this rose belongs

Ember Rose is a horizontal piece, and the rose fills most of the width of the frame. That makes it a natural fit for a wide wall, such as above a sofa, sideboard, bed or fireplace. The strong contrast and saturated warm color hold up at a distance.

Its gold, burnt orange and rust palette brings heat and a strong focal point to a living room, dining room, library or entry. The gilded, antique look suits a space that wants some richness. The warm glow is reason enough to want it.
