A Fire Lit From Within, Held by Cool Blue Ovals

Ember Emblem is a vertical, four-way mirrored digital abstract: a glowing orange, red and gold core inside concentric navy and violet ovals.

Start at the center. A small diamond-like junction sits at dead center, and orange, red and gold shapes unfold from it like something lit from within. They are mirrored left to right and top to bottom, so every angle repeats.

Around that heat are cool, concentric ovals of blue and violet, and a deep navy ground. I made Ember Emblem for the way the fire and the cool sit against each other. It is bold, and it is easy to read from the far side of a room.

What your eye finds when you stay with it

I can't tell you a story about where this came from, and I'd rather not invent one. What I can tell you is what happens when you look for a while. Your eye goes to the glowing core first. Then it follows the red arms out toward the side edges and the pointed yellow forms that anchor the top and bottom.

Then the dark background starts to move. It is full of soft, smoky, marbled swirls that drift slowly around the ovals. Against them, the central shapes are crisp, with angular edges and notched, geometric cutouts. The rigid symmetry never turns cold, because the smoke keeps softening it.

Small flickers of turquoise run along the vertical axis. They are easy to miss at first and then hard to stop seeing. A thin copper-orange border holds the whole design like a frame around an emblem.

What you'll see in Ember Emblem

  • A core of saturated orange, crimson and golden yellow. It looks lit from within and pulls the eye to the center first.
  • Deep navy and violet surrounding the core. The cool ground makes the warm shapes stand out sharply, so the piece reads from a distance.
  • Strict four-way mirror symmetry in a vertical format. It gives the image a calm, ceremonial order, like an emblem or mandala.
  • Crisp, notched, angular edges set against smoky, wispy blue swirls. The hard and soft textures keep the surface worth a closer look.
  • Small turquoise accents along the central axis. They add a quiet flicker of cool color inside all that heat.
  • A thin copper-orange border and concentric ovals. Together they contain the design and give it a finished, framed-in-itself look.

Where it belongs

The vertical format and strong central focal point suit an entry, a stairwell, a narrow wall, or the space above a console or sofa. The warm-against-cool contrast carries well from a distance, so the piece holds its own on pale or neutral walls. The dark blue ground also settles comfortably into moodier rooms.

It is energetic, radiant and bold. It is not trying to be quiet, and it is simply a strong piece of color and pattern to enjoy.
