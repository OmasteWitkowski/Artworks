Light Through Molten Glass: One Flare in a Field of Spirals

Ember Current is a horizontal abstract in burnt orange and amber, where glossy ribbons and tiny spirals rush toward a cream flare near the upper right.

Start with the color. Burnt orange and deep amber flow across the whole frame in glossy ribbons, with olive green and dark brown pooling in the shadows between them. It looks like light passing through molten glass, or oil swirled on water.

Then find the flare. Near the upper right, a pale burst of cream and yellow gives all that motion somewhere to land. I made this piece to be felt from across the room first, then explored up close.

What your eye finds when you step closer

I can't give you a backstory for this piece, and I won't invent one. What I can give you is what's actually in the image, so here is where I'd point you.

On the left, long glossy streaks sweep down and slightly to the right, dense and dark. Move toward the center and the curves begin to rush toward a bright spiral point near the top. On the right, the forms open into larger loops, and the whole picture gets lighter and airier.

Look closer still and the surface fills with small spirals, fine bright edges and tiny bubble-like circles layered over the broad color. Small flecks of red-orange, violet, magenta and electric blue flicker inside the warm palette like sparks. There is no horizon and no single subject, so your eye is free to wander.

Where it belongs on your wall

Ember Current is a horizontal, color-dominant piece. It suits a wall where you want a warm, glowing focal point, such as above a sofa, a console, a bed or a dining table, or in an office.

The dark brown and olive keep the orange grounded, so it adds energy and glow without overwhelming a room. The dense left side balances the open, brighter right. Warm color and motion are reason enough to want it.
