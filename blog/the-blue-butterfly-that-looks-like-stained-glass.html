The Blue Butterfly That Looks Like Stained Glass

Cobalt Jewel Wings is a stylized, perfectly mirrored butterfly with marbled blue panels, pale pink veins and purple-spotted borders on pure white.

Start with the blue. It is a deep, saturated cobalt, and it fills the inner wings of a single butterfly seen from directly above. The wings are open flat, the body sits dead center, and everything around it is clean white.

Then look at what surrounds the blue. Pale pink lines divide it into rounded cells, and the outer borders turn blush pink and beige. That contrast is what I want you to notice first.

What the eye finds when it stays a little longer

From across the room, this is a bold blue butterfly that you recognize in a glance. The strict left-right symmetry makes it feel steady and calm, and the white space around it keeps it uncluttered.

Come closer and the blue changes. The cells look mottled and marbled, like cloudy glass or stone, with a soft glow inside each panel. The pale veins branch outward from the darker shading near the body and lead your eye across the wings in a gentle radiating rhythm.

The borders reward a slow look too. They are smooth, with a faint pearly sheen, and scattered with small purple and indigo oval spots. A few olive-green touches sit near the top. The slim teal body and two thin curved antennae finish the figure.

Most butterfly art is naturalistic or soft and muted. This one is graphic, vivid and simplified, but it is still clearly a butterfly.

What you will see in this butterfly

  • Saturated cobalt-blue wing cells: a strong color that carries across a room and gives a white wall a clear focal point.
  • Pale pink vein lines between the cells: they give the blue a stained-glass look and draw your eye outward from the center.
  • Blush pink and beige borders with purple and indigo spots: a soft, dotted frame that balances the intensity of the blue.
  • Mottled, marbled texture in the blue panels: a cloudy-glass depth that rewards close viewing.
  • Strict bilateral symmetry on a plain white ground: a calm, balanced look with no visual clutter.
  • Thin curved antennae and a teal body: small details that make the simplified, graphic form read as a living butterfly.

Where this butterfly belongs

The horizontal format and the generous white space suit a wall above a console, sofa, bed or desk. The white ground melts into a white or neutral room, so the blue and pink feel like a deliberate accent rather than a competing pattern.

The mood is light, cheerful and calm. The symmetry gives the eye somewhere steady to rest, and the strong blue keeps the piece lively from a distance.
