The Glowing Eye Hiding Inside a Current of Electric Blue

Ember Eye in Electric Blue is a near-abstract macro photograph: a ruffled, jewel-like center inside a lime-yellow ring, swept by a golden band.

Start at the center, just right of middle. A bright yellow-green ring circles a ruffled, crumpled mass of reddish-brown and gold, and it looks like an eye.

Around it, translucent layers of cobalt and turquoise ripple in fine curved lines. From the left, a broad golden-orange band streams in and pulls your attention toward that glowing center.

What Your Eye Finds, One Look at a Time

The first thing you see is the oval. Its rim glows lime and yellow, as if light is passing through it from behind, and the ridges catch white-blue highlights against deeper blue shadows.

Then you notice the center is not smooth. It is rough and coral-like, and magenta, rose and plum tones surface inside the reddish-brown. Against the glassy ripples around it, the contrast of textures is striking.

Then the lines take over. Fine concentric curves spiral around the oval and lead you inward, and a few bright looping strokes curl at the right edge. Small clusters of bubble-like dots sit at the upper right and lower left.

I'm leaving one question open on purpose. The scale and identity of this form are ambiguous. It could be something tiny or something vast, a hidden miniature world, and you get to decide what you are looking at.

Where It Belongs

This is a horizontal piece, so it suits the wide walls: above a sofa, a console, a bed or a mantel. The saturated blues give it presence, and the warm gold and orange sweep adds some heat to a cool palette.

It works best in a room that wants one confident, colorful focal point rather than a gallery of small things. The mood is vivid, energetic and a little mesmerizing.
