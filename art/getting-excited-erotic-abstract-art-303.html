The Color That Looks Lit From Behind

Abstract Glass Macro · horizontal

Slow Current is a horizontal abstract in olive yellow, magenta and amber, glossy layers that seem to glow from inside.

You know the wall. It's the one over the sofa, the bed or the console, where the paint is settled, the furniture is right, and the space still feels unfinished. Everything you've found for it looks like everything else.

Now picture a wide image of warm, glossy color that looks lit from behind. A large olive-yellow shape drifts across the middle. A curved magenta-red ribbon sweeps along below it. The colors seem to glow from inside the layers.

First you see the big shapes. The olive-yellow form has a pointed right end and crosses the image on a diagonal. The magenta ribbon curves along the lower left. Cream and pale lavender surround them, and amber orange sits in the corners.

Then the light. Thin bright lines trace the edge of every shape, and the tones shift softly from warm to cool across the frame. That is what makes it look like colored glass with a lamp behind it.

Stay a minute and the small things appear. Tiny round bubbles sit in the cream and magenta areas. A fine, sandy grain gathers at the upper center and along the right side. Some people see them at once. Others find them later.

There's no horizon and no single thing to look at. Your eye follows the red curve, crosses the yellow form and comes to rest in the pale areas on the right. Whether you see glass, liquid or a landscape of color is yours to decide. The only test I trust is what you feel standing in front of it.

This is a horizontal image, so it suits a wide, specific wall: over a sofa, a bed, a console or a dining wall. The shapes are big and simple, and the diagonal flow gives a long wall something to do.

The warm corners and olive-magenta center sit well in a room with neutral, wood or earthy tones that wants one strong color moment. The pale cream and lavender areas keep it from feeling heavy.
