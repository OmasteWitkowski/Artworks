A Blue Glass Disc That Almost Disappears Into the Sky

Abstract Glass Macro · vertical

Cobalt Lookout is a vertical photograph of a frosted blue glass disc, lit from behind and balanced on a weathered wooden rail.

You know the wall. It might be the one in the entry, beside the desk, or at the turn of the stairs. The furniture is where you want it and the paint is settled, but that wall still looks unfinished every time you walk past.

I made Cobalt Lookout for a wall like that. It has one clear focal point and one strong colour. A round blue glass disc glows against a blue sky, and the eye goes to it right away.

The first thing you see is the blue. A round, frosted cobalt disc stands on edge, lit from behind so it glows evenly. The sky around it is a deep, clean blue, so close in colour that the glass looks like a piece of the day set into the frame.

Then you look closer. A single clear bubble sits at the centre of the disc. Low in the glass there is a faint white shape, a soft cloud reflected or showing through. The longer you stay, the more of these small things turn up.

The disc rests on a rough wooden rail. The sun catches every crack, knot and line of grain in the weathered wood. Beyond the fence, hills and golden-green trees fall away into the distance.

Near the top, in small serif type across the empty sky, is a line from Winston Churchill about looking ahead. It is part of the design. It is quiet enough that you can take it in or ignore it.

The vertical format and a single strong subject suit an eye-level spot where the quotation can be read: an entry, a desk wall, a reading corner or a stairwell. It is one piece with one focal point, so it does not need other art around it to make sense.

The saturated blue looks best against white, grey or warm wood surroundings. There the disc stands out and the honey tones of the rail pick up the wood in the room.
