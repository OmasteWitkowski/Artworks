Two Golden Meanders and the Navy Channel Between Them

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Golden Meander is a horizontal marbled abstract in which glowing gold ribbons wind across a deep navy ground, with flashes of teal, crimson and cobalt inside the folds.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and the empty space above it still looks unfinished every time you walk past. Maybe the room is deep blue, charcoal or a warm neutral that is waiting for one strong note of color.

This is the piece I would put there. Ribbons of golden yellow, amber and ochre twist into two large, winding shapes against a flat navy field. The gold looks molten, as if light were running along the folds.

From across the room you see two big golden masses with a dark navy channel snaking between them. One fills the left and lower part of the picture. The other rises from the top center and curls around to the right.

Come closer and fine contour lines appear inside every fold. They repeat and repeat, like marbling, wood grain or the lines on a topographic map. The edges ripple, and the whorls and eddies lead the eye along the channels, so there is always another turn to follow.

Then the color surprises you. Pockets of teal, turquoise, crimson and cobalt blue glow inside the gold, mostly toward the lower right and center. Against the dark ground they shimmer like oil on water.

I'm not going to tell you what it means. The warmth and the movement are reason enough to want it, and what you find in it is yours.

The horizontal format and strong contrast make it a natural focal piece above a sofa, bed, console or mantel. It works especially well in rooms with deep blue, charcoal or warm neutral tones, where the gold can glow against the darker surroundings.

It suits a room that wants warm color and energy. The mood is rich and lively, so it fits a space where you want the wall to be noticed.
