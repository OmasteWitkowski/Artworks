Where does this blue tunnel lead? To one tiny star.

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Midnight Star Tunnel is a symmetrical blue abstract in which nested ellipses and rectangles draw your eye to a small star cluster at the center.

You have a wall that has looked bare since the new sofa went in. Or the old picture over the bed has started to look small in the room. You know the color you want. You don't know what to hang there.

Picture a deep navy, close to black, with bright cobalt ribbons that look lit from behind. Then picture your eye traveling inward, ring after ring, until it lands on one small four-pointed star. That is this piece.

At first it reads as a bold blue pattern. Dark navy bands frame the image, and three nested ellipses sit inside them. A smaller rectangle, a second ellipse and the star come after those, all drawn toward one point at the middle.

Behind the geometry, glossy cobalt swirls ripple like liquid or flame. They mirror each other exactly, left to right. The swirls flow outward and upward while the rings pull your eye back to the center. The two movements work against each other, and that is why you keep looking.

Look into the star itself. It holds a tiny cluster of star forms, with pale periwinkle flecks at its core. You don't see them from across the room. You find them when you step closer.

The blues and the star can suggest night sky, water or energy. I'll leave that reading to you. What you feel in front of it is the point.

This is a horizontal piece for a wall with room to breathe: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The near-black navy bands and strong contrast give it presence from across the room. It reads as calm, deep and a little cosmic.

It is a bold blue with a focal point, which sets it apart from blue abstracts that simply spread color across the surface.
