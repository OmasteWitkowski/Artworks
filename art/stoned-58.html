A small orange curl pulls your eye out of a wall of burgundy

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Curl is a glossy horizontal abstract in burgundy, crimson and burnt orange, with a cool blue ribbon and room to breathe.

You've got the sofa you wanted and the paint is done. The wall above it is still bare, or it holds something small that got lost the day the bigger sofa arrived.

I made Ember Curl for a wall like that. It is wide and dense with colour, and it doesn't fade into the background. Burgundy, crimson and burnt orange crowd across the canvas, and the pale space on the right lets them breathe.

The first thing you meet is the weight: a dark, inky mass at lower left and a large crimson form at right. They look wet and glossy, almost sculptural. A bright peach-white glow runs along the edge of the dark shape, as if a window were just out of sight.

Then the colour opens up. An orange sphere rises at the top. A mustard-yellow diagonal crosses the middle, and a ribbon of blue and lilac runs along the bottom. The cool blue gives all that red and orange something to push against.

Look closer and the shapes seem to fold into each other. Sometimes they read as petals, though nothing here is a flower or anything else you could name. The left edge is a band of olive and bronze marbling that ripples like veined stone and frames the glossy forms beside it.

At upper right, one orange tendril curls into a small spiral and leads your eye out of the frame. I think of it as the playful note in an otherwise rich piece. What the whole thing means is up to you.

Ember Curl is a horizontal piece, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a sideboard. The dark lower-left mass and the big crimson form give a long wall a strong anchor, and the pale space on the right keeps it from feeling heavy.

The palette is warm and dense, with burgundy, orange and mustard, and a cool blue to balance them. It will sit well in a room that already has some warmth in wood, leather, brass or textiles, and it can also bring warmth to a room that has none. The mood is rich and a little playful.
