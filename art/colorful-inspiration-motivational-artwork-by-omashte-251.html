The Word INSPIRE, Taken Apart Letter by Letter

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

A symmetrical digital abstract where INSPIRE drops down a glowing centre column, framed by mirrored ribbons in cobalt, crimson and yellow.

You know the wall. It might be over the sofa, above a console, or behind a desk. The furniture is settled and the paint is chosen, and the wall still looks like it is waiting for something. Not another pale quote print. Something with colour and a centre of gravity.

This is a piece for that wall. A tall olive-green and orange column runs down the middle, and the word INSPIRE sits at its top in crimson capitals. On either side, cobalt, crimson and yellow shapes loop and fold toward it.

First you read the word. INSPIRE is written once in full at the top of the column, in crimson serif capitals. It is easy to read and it is not hidden.

Then you see it taken apart. Below the full word, the letters step down one at a time: a small yellow N, a large red S, a large green P, a small yellow R, a large red E. Thin horizontal lines and blue rectangles stand in for the letter I and sit between them. The word is not laid over the design. It is the spine of it.

Then you notice everything around it. The curving shapes on each side could be folded ribbons or stylized figures. The left half mirrors the right, and the top echoes the bottom. Two small arrow-like points at the middle left and right pinch the composition at its waist and draw your eye back to the centre.

I have left the meaning open. Some people will see a word. Others will see ribbons, figures or a pattern. Your response is the only test that counts.

It is horizontal, so it suits a wide wall: over a sofa, a console or a desk, or along a hallway. It also works in a studio or office that could use a focal point. The strict symmetry means it centres easily, and the dark blues and reds hold their own against bright yellow and chartreuse.

The mood is upbeat and energetic. That comes from the word and from the colour, and what it means to you is yours to decide.

Picture it centred over your sofa or desk, with INSPIRE at the top of the column and the ribbons looping out on both sides. The wall that has bothered you for months finally has something to look at.
