Two amber eyes, or a peacock feather? You decide.

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · vertical

Amber Gaze is a tall, mirrored abstract in jewel tones: two glowing amber ovals with violet-blue hollows, split by one thin dark line.

You know the wall. It might be the one beside the doorway, the stretch in the entry, or the narrow strip the sofa never quite reaches. Everything around it is settled, and that one spot keeps looking back at you.

Picture a tall piece there with a thin dark line down its center. On either side of the line sits a large golden-amber oval with a deep violet-blue hollow at its heart. You see it from across the room before you see anything else.

The first thing is the symmetry. The two halves match, and the dark line between them makes the image read like a mirrored pair. Then the color arrives: glowing amber and yellow against darker brown and violet centers, with bands of emerald, teal and cobalt wrapping around the ovals.

Stay a little longer and the ovals start to look like something. Some people see a pair of watchful eyes. Others see a ceremonial mask, or the marking on a peacock feather. None of these is a fixed subject. They are impressions, and I would rather you keep whatever you see.

Then the surface shows itself. The edges are fine and feathery, almost flame-like, and they pull toward the center seam. The color looks like melted, marbled liquid, with a smooth, glossy finish. It is listed as digital art, and the cyan and blue edges look backlit.

This piece is about color and pattern, and that is enough. If it stops you, that is a good enough reason to want it.

The vertical format and strict symmetry suit an entry, a narrow wall or the space beside a doorway. The color is bold enough to be seen from across a room, so it holds its place even at a distance.

The dark center against the bright perimeter makes it a focal point. It fits best in a room that can handle saturated color and where you want one piece to stand out.

Measure the wall first. Then tape paper or painter's tape to it at the size you are considering and stand back. The listing lets you choose the size, and the 30-day money-back guarantee is there if it does not work.

Screens vary, and I can't promise an exact match. The palette is amber, emerald, cobalt and violet with bright cyan edges, and the print should keep that jewel-tone feel. If it does not suit your room, the 30-day guarantee applies.

Your response is the only test that counts. There is no good or bad art, and you do not need training to know whether something holds your eye. If these glowing ovals do, that is reason enough.

Picture it on that wall: two amber ovals, a dark seam between them, and a ripple of emerald and cobalt at the edges. It stays interesting, and you may keep seeing something different in it.

When you are ready, go to the listing, choose your size and finish, and see how it looks in your space.
