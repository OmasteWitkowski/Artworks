A Blue Disc Wrapped in Orange, Like an Eclipse of Liquid

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Cobalt Eclipse is a digital abstract: a glowing blue disc ringed in orange ribbons that drifts into cool teal and green.

You bought the sofa. The paint is settled. And the wall above it still looks bare, or the old picture there looks small now. You have known for a while that it needs one thing, and that the thing is not another pale print that matches everything and says nothing.

Picture a deep cobalt-blue disc filling the left side of that wall, wrapped in curling orange and amber ribbons, with a dark crescent arching over it like an eclipse. To the right, the colour cools into teal, aqua and emerald green. This is Cobalt Eclipse.

I'll tell you what I see, and leave the rest to you. From across the room it reads as an eclipse made of marbled liquid: a blue disc with a dark brown crescent over it and a ring of orange around it. The blue and the orange are opposites, so the left side hits hard.

Come closer and it opens up. Round, bubble-like orbs with spiral centres sit inside the disc, and spirals curl outward from them. The surfaces are smooth and veined like marble. Translucent layers overlap, so the colour looks lit from within, the way stained glass does when light comes through it.

Then your eye travels right. The shapes loosen and break into softer teal and green patterns, and the energy settles. Fine speckle along the top edge and in parts of the teal field adds a bubbly grain. I won't tell you what it means. I think a piece like this is felt, and what you feel in front of it is yours.

It is listed as digital art. Beyond that, the image is what I have to show you, and I'd rather you judge it by looking.

This is a horizontal piece with a heavy, saturated left side, so it suits a wall that needs one strong colour moment: above a sofa, a bed or a console, or at the end of a hallway. The cooler right half keeps the whole thing balanced instead of shouting.

It works best where you want the art to lead and the room to follow. If your space is mostly neutral, this gives it a centre. If it already has blue, orange, teal or green in it, those colours have a place to echo.

Measure the wall first, and the furniture beneath it. Then open the listing and compare the sizes offered. The piece is horizontal, so it suits a wide space. I can't promise a maximum size here, so check the sizes shown on the listing.

Screens differ, so no one can promise an exact match. The colours to expect are cobalt blue, burnt orange, teal and emerald green, set against deep brown-black. If it isn't right in your room, Fine Art America's 30-day money-back guarantee covers you.

Your own response is the only test that counts. If the blue and orange stop you, or you keep coming back to the glow, that is reason enough. You don't need to know what it means or how it was made to want it on your wall.

Imagine walking into the room and seeing that blue disc glowing on the wall, the orange curling around it, and the teal and green cooling off to the right. Imagine a guest asking where you found it.
