A cream spiral caught in blue and red light

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Luminous Curl is a glossy, horizontal abstract in periwinkle blue, brick red and plum, crossed by glowing cream-white swirls.

You know the wall. Maybe the new sofa made it look bare. Maybe the old picture looks small in a bigger room. You've measured it and walked past it for months, and nothing you've found has been right.

Now picture a bright cream-white spiral curling inward at the lower right of that wall, with periwinkle blue on its left and deep red and dark plum above. That is Luminous Curl.

I'll tell you what I see in it, and you can decide what you see.

First it's the light. The cream-white highlights burn out to near white and glow pale yellow at their edges, the way light catches a wet or glassy surface. Next to the dark, saturated color around them, they look lit from inside.

Then the eye starts to travel. There's no horizon and no single place to rest. It follows the fine, feathery streaks that run through the pale shapes like strands of hair. It circles the spiral at lower right and the hooked white shape at upper right. It slips across thin curved lines that divide the frame into cell-like segments. Tiny bubble-like dots are scattered across the red.

Step back and you get color and glow. Come close and you get texture and detail. I'd rather you stand in front of it than read about it. Your own response is the only test that counts.

Luminous Curl is horizontal and densely filled, so it suits a wide wall: over a sofa, a bed or a console, in an entry, in an office or in a studio. It has no single focal point, so it reads from across the room. It also holds up when you walk up close.

The dark plum and red give the wall weight. The cream highlights bring in light. It's a bold, energetic piece, not a quiet one.
