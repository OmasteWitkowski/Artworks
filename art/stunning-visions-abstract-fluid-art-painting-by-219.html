One red petal glowing in a melting orange and violet swirl

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Petal is a wide horizontal abstract where burnt orange meets periwinkle violet, with a single glossy red shape at the meeting point.

Your sofa is in. The paint is settled. And the wall above it still looks like it is waiting for something. A small picture would float up there, and another pale landscape would only match the room, not belong to it.

This is the piece I would put in front of that wall. Orange and violet pour into each other across a wide horizontal frame, and one small red shape glows just left of center. It is the first thing you see, and then your eye starts to travel.

I would rather you stand in front of this piece than read about it, but here is what is there. The left side is warm: burnt orange, mustard yellow, coral. The center and right open into a broad field of lavender and periwinkle. The red form sits right where the two meet.

Look closer at that red shape. It glows as if lit from within, with magenta and violet inside it, and thin pale aqua lines trace its edge like reflections on a wet surface. It suggests a petal or a flower bud, but it never becomes one. What it is, you decide.

Then the ribbons take over. A large S-shaped orange band sweeps through the right side, curling lines frame a pale violet oval, and spirals gather in the upper right corner. Fine ripple lines run through every color field, like wood grain or topographic lines.

I believe art is felt, not seen. If the colors stop you, that is reason enough to want it.

The wide horizontal format suits the wall above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The warm left side and cool right side make it a color-led statement in a room of neutral furnishings. In a room that already has orange or violet in it, it can pick those colors up.

The mood is lively and warm, with a dreamy, flowing quality. It is playful without being busy, because the red shape gives it a clear center.
