A Cobalt Sky Big Enough to Fill a Long Blank Wall

Pacific Northwest Landscapes · panoramic horizontal

Cobalt Stillness is a panoramic photograph of a glassy lake under towering white clouds, with sunlit evergreens on the right bank.

You know the wall. It's the long one over the sofa, or the bed, or the console in the hall. The furniture is settled and the paint is right, but the wall has looked bare for months. Maybe the old picture now looks small, or you haven't found anything that fits that much width.

Now picture a sky across it. A dense cobalt blue crowded with bright white clouds, above a lake so still it holds them in its reflection. That is Cobalt Stillness, and the sky is the reason it exists.

I don't have a tale to tell you about this one, and I won't make one up. What I can tell you is what happens when you look. The horizon sits low, so the sky takes about two-thirds of the picture. The clouds are bright white on top with shadowed gray undersides. They sweep diagonally from the upper left toward the right, while the water below stays perfectly still.

Then your eye starts to travel. The shoreline curves from the rounded gray and brown stones at the bottom edge out to a stand of tall evergreens on the far bank. Low, warm sun catches their trunks and the strip of dry tan shore beneath them. On the left there is open water and a dark, low ridge, which gives the whole picture room to breathe.

Come closer and there is more. You can see fine needles on the evergreens and tall grasses at the lower right, and the rough scatter of cobbles in the foreground. Some people see a summer afternoon by the water. Others simply enjoy that much blue. Either is a good reason to hang it.

This is a statement piece for a long horizontal wall: over a sofa, a bed, a console or a long hallway. The very wide format fills that width, and the low horizon lets the blue work as the main colour in the room. In a neutral room, that blue is the colour.

The mood is open and calm-looking, a quiet stretch of water under a big sky. The evergreen mass on the right balances the open lake on the left. The picture gives the eye somewhere to go without crowding the room.
