An Eye of Cobalt Blue Inside a Ring of Amber Light

Abstract Glass Macro · horizontal

Cobalt Eye in Amber is an abstract glass close-up: a tilted blue oval, a glowing amber band and a bubble-flecked teal field, ready to print for your wall.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall still looks bare every time you walk past it. You have probably been waiting for something that holds the whole room together without shouting over it.

Picture a tilted oval of deep cobalt blue, cropped off the left edge, with a hazy pale center and a ring of glowing amber around it. It looks back at you like an eye. That is the piece I'm asking you to consider.

From across a room, you see the colour first. Blue against amber, with a dark brown-black rim holding them apart. The light seems to come from behind, the way it does when you hold something translucent up to a window.

Step closer and the rings start to separate. A pale, cloudy center streaked with soft ripples. A cobalt ring around it. An amber band crossed by faint hairline cracks. Then the rim, where the colour falls into shadow.

To the right, the picture changes. A teal and green field is dotted with tiny bubbles and droplet-like specks, with fine streaks sweeping toward the edge. Sharp focus sits on the left and center, and the right side softens into blur.

It reads as glass to me, from the translucency, the bubbles and the sheen. I'd rather you spend time with what is in the frame than with a story I haven't told. What it means is yours to decide.

The horizontal format suits a wall that needs one bold statement: over a sofa, a console or a desk. The saturated blue and amber carry across a room, and the bubbles, cracks and streaks keep paying off when you are standing close.

The colour pairs well with neutral or dark surroundings, where the blue and amber can be the loudest thing in the room. It is vivid and absorbing rather than quiet.

Imagine walking into the room and seeing that cobalt eye ringed in amber where the bare wall used to be. The blue and amber are lit from within, and the bubbles and fine cracks reward a closer look.
