A Red Glow Between Dark Timbers, Under a Swirling Sky

Travel / Specialty / Projects · horizontal

Ember Timbers is a digital, painterly image of a small half-timbered building with red-orange panels and a tall brick chimney, printed to order in the size and finish you choose.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall above it still looks unfinished. Nothing you have found so far looks like it belongs to you.

Now picture a small building with red-orange panels glowing between dark timber beams, and a tall brick chimney breaking the skyline above it. The sky behind it is gray-blue and rippled like oil paint. This is Ember Timbers.

I have nothing to tell you here about where this building stands or how old it is, and I would rather say so than make something up. What I can tell you is what is in the image, because it rewards a longer look.

The first thing you see is the colour: saturated red-orange panels framed by dark brown and weathered grey beams, over a lawn of grass green. Then the eye climbs. The steep clay-tile roof leads up to two gables, and the ribbed brick chimney carries on past them into the sky.

Then the sky itself shows up. It ripples and swirls with a brush-like texture, while the building below stays still and solid. Come closer and the tiles, the bricks and the wood grain are all finely detailed. The calm of the building and the movement of the sky sit side by side in the same frame.

What it means is yours to decide. It has a rustic, nostalgic, storybook feel, with intense colour under a quiet cloudy sky, and that is as much as I will claim for it.

Ember Timbers is a horizontal piece, so it suits the wide spaces that tend to bother people: above a sofa, over a mantel or console, in an entry, along a hallway or in a study. The building is centred and fills most of the frame, so the colour does the work from across the room.

It sits easily in rustic, cabin, lodge or traditional rooms. It can also be the one bold accent in a neutral room, where the red-orange gives the eye somewhere to land.

I believe there is no good or bad art, and that everyone can be an artist. Your own response is the only test. If this building holds your eye, that is reason enough. If it does not, it is not your piece, and that is fine too.

Picture the empty wall above your sofa with this on it. The red-orange panels glow against the dark beams, the chimney reaches up into the swirling sky, and the green below grounds it all. When a guest asks where you found it, you will have something to say.
