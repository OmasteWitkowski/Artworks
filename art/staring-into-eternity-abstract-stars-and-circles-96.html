Eleven Eyes or Eleven Moons? Look Closer at This Glowing Star

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Burst is a digital artwork: a jagged, marbled orange star ringed by eleven small eye-like shapes, glowing inside a dark oval on black.

You have a wall that is calm, maybe even dark, and it has been quiet for too long. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and the space above it still has nothing that gives the room a center.

Picture a star there, glowing. Its core is hot orange and golden yellow, and the points push out into the dark. It sits in an oval of near-black navy, so it looks lit from behind.

From across the room you see a star burst. Its points are sharp and uneven, some long and some short, so it reads as spontaneous rather than stamped from a template.

Step closer and the color changes character. Inside the star, orange, yellow and deep red-orange flow in curving ribbons and fine wavy streaks, like something poured. Teal, cobalt and pale green show up toward the tips, and a dusty pink-gray sits at the center. Thin dark outlines hold each shape apart and keep the glow sharp against the black.

Then there is the ring. Eleven small round shapes circle the star, and each holds a smaller circle inside it. On one day they look like bubbles. On another they look like eyes, or crescent moons. I leave that to you. Art is felt, and what you see in them is yours.

Ember Burst is horizontal, so it suits a wall that is wider than it is tall: over a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The near-black ground lets the warm star read as the brightest thing in the room, especially against calm or dark walls.

The mood is warm, energetic and a little playful, like a radiant sun or a firework with a cosmic edge. It is a graphic, decorative piece. It does not need a deeper reason to earn its place. Wanting it because it stops you is reason enough.
