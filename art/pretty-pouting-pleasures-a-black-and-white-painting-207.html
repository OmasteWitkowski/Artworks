A Lily Made of Spirals, in Pure Black and White

Botanical & Flowers · horizontal

Spiral Lily in Black and White is a large close-up lily in charcoal, gray and soft white, with a small swirling curl on every petal, bud and background shape.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is done, and that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished. Whatever you've held up to it so far has looked either too quiet or too much like everyone else's.

This is the image I'd hold up to it. A large lily fills the frame, its long pointed petals fanning out from the center and curling at the tips. The petals are pale and bright, and the ground behind them is nearly black.

From across the room, you see a lily. Dark speckles and wavy marks run along the inner petals, and round buds and berry-like shapes gather toward the right. It reads as a true botanical subject, in strict black and white.

Step closer and the surface changes. Small swirling curls cover every petal, bud and background shape, like thick, twisted paint. The petal edges end in tight scrolled ruffles. The flower stops being only a flower and becomes a rolling pattern of spirals.

I'm not going to hand you a made-up backstory about where this lily grew. What I can tell you is what's there on the surface, and it is worth a longer look. The piece is listed as digital art, a monochrome floral with swirl texture.

Look closer and the light does much of the work. Petals are bright at the center and along their upper edges, then fall into deeper gray where they curl and overlap. The swirls turn outward with the petals, so the whole flower seems to move slowly.

The format is horizontal, with the flower centered and petals reaching toward the edges. That suits a wall that needs one strong statement: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a mantel. The strong contrast is dramatic and a little playful.

It sits easily in rooms built on neutral, black, white or natural tones. Because it is strictly monochrome, it adds drama through light and texture, not through color.
