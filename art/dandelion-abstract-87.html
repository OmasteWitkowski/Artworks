A seed head that looks lit from within, on near-black

Botanical & Flowers · square

Quiet Starburst is a single seed head drawn in glowing white strands, a digitally processed botanical image in a square frame.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished. Maybe the old art looks small next to the new room. Maybe you have been waiting for something that isn't just another colourful flower.

This is what I would hang there. One round seed head sits in the middle of a near-black square, and every strand of it glows as if there were a small light inside.

At first glance it is a starburst. Long, slender, pointed filaments fan out in every direction, soft white at the tips and shading to pale grey-lavender. Then you see what it is: a seed head, most likely a dandelion or a close relative, with a thin stem faintly visible at the bottom.

Look closer and the longest strands curve and cross in the middle, forming a loose X. The burst starts to look like it is turning, very slowly. In the center the green is pale and woolly, almost sage, and it holds the whole sphere in place.

Between the strands there are faint periwinkle shadows. The tips blur into soft halos, so the strands look both silky and sharp. It sits somewhere between a nature photograph and a drawing made of light. It is a digitally processed botanical image, and I would rather you look at it than read more about it.

The mood comes from contrast alone: bright strands, dark ground. It is quiet and still, with a little energy moving outward. What it means to you is yours to decide.

This is a single focal piece. The square format and centered form suit a wall where you want one luminous image to stand out in a quiet room. The very dark ground reads well on light or mid-tone walls, and it holds its own in a dim room.
