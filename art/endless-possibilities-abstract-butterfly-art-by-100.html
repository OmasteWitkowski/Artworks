A Butterfly That Looks Lit From Behind, Like a Glass Window

Butterflies, Birds & Wildlife · horizontal

Glowing Orange Wings is a stained-glass-style digital illustration of one large orange butterfly, cut into jewel-colored cells by thick black lines.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished. Maybe you have been looking at it for months.

This is the piece I would hold up to it. A large orange butterfly sits in the middle of a white panel inside a thick black border, wings spread wide and seen from above. The first thing you notice is the color. It looks like light coming through glass.

From across the room, you see a butterfly: orange, symmetrical, and almost filling the frame. Black lines divide its wings and body into cells of orange, sky blue, leaf green, yellow and coral pink, with purple along the upper edges. It reads as a stained-glass window, though it is an illustration and not glass.

Come closer and each cell has its own small pattern. The color ripples and swirls like marbled paper or liquid glass. In the body and along the upper wing edges, the swirls mirror each other across the center, a little like a kaleidoscope.

There is a softer detail too. The white panel has uneven, torn-looking edges that take the strictness out of the black frame. Green vines curl in from the corners, and the antennae curl up into the top border. The design is still and balanced, but those curves keep a little motion in it.

I can't tell you where this butterfly flies. What I can say is that it is bright, playful and full of color, and that it is all there in the image.

The horizontal shape suits a wall that is wider than it is tall: above a sofa, a console, a bed or a desk. The black and white framing lets it hold its own in a bright room, and the orange gives a warm accent without taking over.
