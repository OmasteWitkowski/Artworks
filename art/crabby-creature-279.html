An orange flower that burns, and a face you may miss

Botanical & Flowers · horizontal

Ember Bloom is a mirrored orange flower whose petals melt into flame-like ribbons, with a dark green mask waiting low in the center.

You have one wall that has bothered you for months. The paint is settled, the sofa or bed is where you want it, and the wall above it is still bare. You don't want something polite. You want one piece with some heat in it.

This is the piece I would hold up to that wall. An orange flower opens at the center, mirrored exactly left to right, with two pointed petals rising in a V like horns or wings. Its throat glows yellow, as if lit from within.

The first thing you see is a flower. Saturated orange, dark-tipped stamens at the middle, deep green below and a soft blue-gray haze above. It reads at once from across the room.

Then the edges start to move. Petals and leaves stretch into wavy ribbons of orange, amber and green, and they flow outward and downward like flame, or like grass in wind. Thin dark outlines and yellow highlights give the whole thing a stained-glass glow.

Look at the lower center and you may find a dark green opening that reads as a mouth, with the shapes around it forming a mask. Some people see a face right away. Others see only the flower, and that is fine. I would rather leave that to you.

Stay a little longer and the finer detail appears: veining across the petals, fine filaments in the center, and a faint marbled ripple in the background at the top. What it means is felt, and it is yours to decide.

The image is horizontal, which suits the space over a sofa, a bed or a long console. Its center is strong and its sides are symmetrical, so it sits squarely on a wall and looks settled there.

The deep green lower half and the saturated orange center give it real presence in a room where the paint and furniture are quiet. You don't need a reason beyond being drawn to it. That is reason enough.

You don't need to know anything about art. If the orange stops you, or you keep wondering about the face in the lower center, that is the test. Your own response is the only one that counts.
