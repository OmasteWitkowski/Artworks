A lupine made entirely of spirals, for your narrow wall

Botanical & Flowers · vertical

Spiral Lupine is a tall blue and violet flower spike in which every petal, leaf and background shape curls into a small swirl.

You know the wall. It might be the narrow stretch in the entry, the run beside the stairs, or the space above a console table. The room around it is finished, and that one spot keeps looking empty.

A tall wall like that wants something tall. This is a single lupine spike, standing upright in the middle of the frame and rising almost to the top. The blossoms are cobalt blue and orchid violet, stacked in rows and tapering to a tip of small buds.

At first glance it is clearly a lupine, and you could name it from across the room. Then the spirals show up. Each petal curls. The leaves at the lower left curl in. The soft green and sky-blue bands behind the flower are scattered with little circles and swirls. The whole flower looks like it is made of ribbons of blue and purple.

That curling is why I think this piece sits between a flower portrait and an abstract. You get a flower you recognise, but it is not a botanical record and it is not a photograph. It is a lupine drawn in curls, with the colour turned up.

The light is soft and the background is bright and even, with no hard shadows. Pale yellow-green and pink touches on the petal edges and bud tips make the blossoms look lit from within. Spirals repeat at every scale, from the smallest bud to the circles behind, so the image seems to turn slowly. The upward taper of the spike lifts your eye with it.

The vertical shape and centered spike suit an entryway, a stairwell, the space above a console, or any narrow wall. The cobalt and violet are strong enough to hold a wall on their own. The green and sky-blue bands pair easily with white, soft gray or green rooms.

The mood is cheerful and playful, like a garden in bright light. If you want to hang two side by side, the upright shape lets them sit together as a pair.
