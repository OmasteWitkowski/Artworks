Find the Face Hidden in This Molten Yellow Mirror

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · vertical

Molten Sun Mask is a tall, mirrored abstract in golden yellow, amber and cream, with a face-like pattern waiting at its center.

You know the wall. It's beside the door, or at the top of the stairs, or over the console in the entry. The furniture is settled and the paint is chosen, and that one tall space still looks unfinished every time you walk past it.

Now picture it filled with glowing yellow. Amber and cream swirls curl up and down a tall frame, with cobalt blue, red and emerald green at the edges. In the middle, something looks back at you.

The first thing you notice is the light. The yellows and whites glow at the center and along the edges, as if the picture were lit from behind. The shapes are pointed and curled, a bit like leaves and a bit like flames, and every edge is crisp.

Then you notice the symmetry. The left side mirrors the right exactly, and the top nearly mirrors the bottom. A seam runs down the middle and a line runs across it, so the whole image is organized like a cross, or like a kaleidoscope.

Where those lines meet, two dark purple almond shapes sit on either side of a pale green-gold center. It reads as a face, or a mask. I can't promise you'll see the same one twice. The eye resolves it differently each time you look, and that's part of why I like standing in front of it.

Look closer still and you'll find small dark purple and maroon areas that give the brightness some depth, and a fine mottled grain that breaks up the smooth, glossy surfaces.

This is a tall, portrait-format piece, so it suits the narrow, vertical spaces that are hard to fill: beside a doorway, in an entry, along a stairwell, or above a console table.

Its strong central symmetry and bright color make it a focal piece. In a neutral or dark room, the yellow and orange become the one thing the eye goes to first.
