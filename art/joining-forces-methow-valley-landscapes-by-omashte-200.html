A River Bend That Walks Your Eye Into the Pines

Pacific Northwest Landscapes · horizontal

"Slate Water, Green Wall" is a landscape photograph with a soft painterly finish: a shallow river, a pale gravel bar and a wall of tall green trees.

You have the sofa. The paint is settled. And the wide wall above it still looks like something is missing. You know what you don't want, which is another print that looks like everyone else's.

This is the picture I'd offer for that wall. A shallow river comes in from the lower left, breaks into small white rapids over the stones, and curves away toward a thick line of tall green trees. It is an easy picture to look at for a long time.

Most people start at the bottom left, on the pale gravel bar with its few fallen logs. From there the river takes over. The ripples carry you along the S-curve, past the white of the rapids, and out toward the trees.

The trees are the opposite of the water. The water ripples and sparkles, and the evergreens and leafy trees stand upright and still. That contrast is what keeps the picture from going flat.

The light is soft and even, under a hazy blue sky with thin streaky clouds. There are no harsh shadows. The right bank has rounded rocks, green shrubs and dry golden grass, which add a warm note to all the green and blue.

It is a photograph with a soft, painterly texture over the surface. That finish keeps the scene gentle rather than sharp. I would rather you stand in front of it than read about it, so trust whatever your eye does first.

This is a horizontal landscape, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The river runs diagonally across the picture and carries that width through the space.

The muted greens and blues and the even light read as calm, which suits living rooms, bedrooms, offices and waiting rooms with a natural palette. If your room is already settled, the gold grass and pale gravel give it a little warmth without competing with your furniture.
