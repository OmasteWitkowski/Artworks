A Taurus glyph cut from fire and set in a window of white

Spiritual, Mandala & Symbolic · vertical

Taurus Ember Window is a vertical digital piece: the Taurus zodiac glyph filled with orange swirl, inside a backlit-looking white oval and a mirrored border.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished. Anything small gets lost on it, and anything generic feels like it came with the house.

This is the piece I'd ask you to picture there. A tall white oval, lit as if from behind, holds a single symbol in burning orange. It is bold enough to hold a wall on its own.

From across the room you see the shape: a circle with a crescent on top, like a pair of horns. That is the Taurus glyph, centered in a tall white oval.

Come closer and the glyph changes. It isn't a flat orange fill. It is made of the same marbled, flame-like swirl that runs around the border, with small rose-like spirals and curls repeating through both. The symbol looks cut out of the pattern and set against the white.

Then the light. A soft yellow-orange glow runs along the inside edge of the border, right where it meets the oval. The oval starts to look like a window with bright light behind it.

The pattern circles the edges while the oval and the glyph stay perfectly still. That mix of motion and calm is what keeps pulling me back for a longer look.

The image is vertical, centered and symmetrical, so it suits a wall where one bold image should do the work: above a sofa or a bed, at the end of a hallway, or on a narrow stretch beside a doorway. The orange border brings strong color. The white oval keeps it from feeling crowded.

It works especially well against a white or light wall, where the orange stands out. It can also warm a calmer room with a single strong accent.
