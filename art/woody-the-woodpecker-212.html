The Glowing Orange Drop That Could Be Petal or Molten Glass

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Drop is a large, translucent orange-red teardrop with one thin crease, set against emerald green and dusty mauve-pink.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished. You have probably been looking at it for months.

It may need one warm shape that holds the room together. Ember Drop is that: a single glowing orange-red form, tapering to a soft point, in a horizontal frame.

First comes the glow. A large, smooth orange-red form hangs from the top edge and narrows to a point near the bottom center. It looks lit from within, or from behind, with darker red-brown shadow near the top and along its right edge.

Then the crease. One thin, slightly curved line runs down the right side of the form. Faint, fine lines show near the top and along that crease, so the smooth surface rewards a longer look.

Then everything around it. Dusty mauve-pink, pale olive-cream and bright green blur softly into one another. The green sweeps upward on the right, and rounded pale shapes on the left add a slow curve the other way.

I won't tell you what the form is, because the image doesn't settle it. It could be a petal, a drop of molten glass, or something else. You get to decide, and nothing here asks you to decode a message.

Ember Drop is a horizontal piece, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The one dominant form and soft edges give the wall a warm focal point without crowding the room.

The orange-red and green give it presence, and the silky gradients keep it restful. It reads warm, quiet and a little dramatic.
