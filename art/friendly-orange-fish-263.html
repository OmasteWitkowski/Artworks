One Lit Ember of Orange Inside Sweeping Green Curves

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember in the Green is a horizontal abstract print: a glowing orange-amber teardrop held between olive and leaf-green arcs.

You know the wall. It is the one over the sofa or the bed, and the room around it is finished. The furniture is where you want it and the paint is settled, but that stretch of wall still looks unfinished every time you walk in.

Now picture something wide and uncluttered there. Most of it is soft olive and leaf green, with a pale cream channel running through the middle. Just above center is one glossy orange-amber teardrop, and it is the first thing you see.

The orange comes first. It leans on a diagonal, and small bright glints sit on its left tip and its right edge. Those glints make it look wet and lit, like an ember held inside the green.

Then your eye moves out. A large olive curve sweeps across the top, and a smaller green curve runs along the bottom edge. Between them is a pale cream-gray channel shaped like a slow S. A thin bright-green sliver slips out from beneath the orange and points back toward the lower left, so the eye keeps travelling instead of settling in one spot.

The surfaces look smooth and almost glassy. A few tiny droplets or bubbles show near the top of the orange shape and along the bottom. The background is a milky blur with nothing in it to compete with the colour.

I would rather you stand in front of it than have me tell you what it is. It reads as abstract first. Some people see liquid, some see a leaf, and some just like the colour. That is reason enough.

The horizontal format is made for a wide wall: over a sofa, a bed, a console or a dining sideboard. The image is minimal and uncluttered, so it can work as a single statement piece without crowding the room.

The palette is olive, leaf green, burnt orange and cream. It sits easily with neutral or earthy furnishings, and the orange can pick up an accent you already have, such as a cushion, a lamp or a piece of pottery.
