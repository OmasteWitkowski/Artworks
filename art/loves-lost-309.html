White shapes floating on molten colour: antlers or stars?

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Cream Constellation is a horizontal marbled abstract in orange, cobalt blue and deep red, with glossy white shapes that could be antlers or stars.

You have a room that is finished. The sofa is right, the paint is right, and one wall still looks like nobody has decided anything about it. In a room of quiet colours, that wall can be the one place that holds all the heat.

This is the piece I would put there. Orange, cobalt blue, deep red and magenta flow across it like marbled liquid. Then your eye finds the white shapes, glossy and calligraphic, sitting on top of all that colour as if light were catching a wet surface.

The colour comes first. It is bright and saturated: a blue mass at lower left, a band running from purple into orange through the centre, and a large red curve at right. A soft sage-green corner at upper left gives the eye a cool place to rest, and a cream and pale pink glow sits low in the centre between the blue and the red.

Then the white shapes. They are spread across the upper half, the largest near the top centre. They could be antlers. They could be stars. Small dark green stars run along the left edge, which makes me lean toward stars, but I would rather you decide than tell you.

Then you look closer, and the surface keeps going. Feathered wisps curl across the upper left, small spirals gather at the upper right, and colour drifts and blends down through the middle. Nothing here is fixed in a single reading. Art is felt, and your response is the only test it needs.

It is a horizontal piece, so it suits the wall over a sofa, a bed, a console or a sideboard. In a room with neutral paint and furniture, it can supply the colour all at once. The orange, blue and red are bold enough to anchor the palette of the whole room.

The mood is lively and warm, a little playful and dreamlike. It is not a quiet piece, and it is not trying to be.
