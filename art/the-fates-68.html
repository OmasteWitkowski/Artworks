The red flower that turns into a face

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Mask is a perfectly mirrored red-orange floral abstract that reads as a glowing flower and, a moment later, as a round-eyed mask.

You have a wall that has bothered you for months. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and the space above it still looks unfinished. Everything you have found so far looks like everything else.

Picture this in that spot. A bright red-orange center glows against dark brown edges, with green leaves arching over the top and golden-yellow florets at the sides. It is a flower first. Then you notice two round shapes stacked above it, and the flower is looking back at you.

I can't tell you a story about where this was made, because I would rather say only what is true of the image. What I can tell you is what happens when you stand in front of it.

The composition is mirrored exactly, left to right. That symmetry is what turns the red petals into a central figure with paired, eye-like circles. It reads as a flower and as a mask or creature at the same moment, and I think that is the reason it holds your attention.

Rippling lines swirl through every layer, like glass or liquid, and curl into spirals at the top corners. The eye travels outward along those echoing curves and then comes back to the bright center. The image seems to pulse.

It can look playful or mysterious. Which one it is for you is up to you. I believe art is felt, not seen, and your response is the only test that counts.

This is a horizontal piece, which suits the wide wall above a sofa or bed, or the space over a console or desk. Its saturated red-orange center against dark brown edges gives it the weight to be the focal point of the wall rather than a quiet accent.

It suits a room that welcomes bold colour and pattern, or a neutral room that needs one warm, confident note. It is ornamental and a little psychedelic, so it works well in an eclectic space.
