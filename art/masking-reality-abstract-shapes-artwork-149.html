A Winged Mask Made of Color, Glowing on Black

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Crest is a mirrored, mask-like winged figure in orange, red and cobalt blue, set on a flat black panel and framed in a painterly border.

You have a wall that can take something strong. Maybe it's over a new sofa, a bed or a console, in a room that is neutral or dark and has been waiting for one piece to hold it together.

Ember Crest is built for that wall. One mirrored figure sits in the middle of a black panel, with horns or wings curling up and out on both sides. The color gathers in the center and glows. It reads from across the room, and it still has something to show you when you step closer.

The first thing you see is the glow. An orange and red core sits in the middle of the black, and the colors around it look lit from behind, like stained glass. There is no cast shadow and no light source in the picture. The color itself is the light.

Then the shape comes into focus. A face-like form sits on a central vertical axis, with a red mouth-like shape. Blue cross-shaped arms reach below it and out to the sides. The curls on both sides spiral inward and pull your eye back to the center, while the tips sweep outward and give the whole figure lift.

Up close, the fills are marbled and swirled, like blended oil or watercolor pigment, with magenta pink, turquoise, yellow and lavender moving through them. The black behind them is perfectly flat and smooth, which is why the color stands out so much.

Is it a mask, a face, a winged crest or just a pattern? I leave that to you. Your own response is the only test that counts.

The format is horizontal, and the figure is centered and symmetrical. That suits the wide wall behind a sofa, a console, a bed or a desk, where a centered piece looks settled.

The strong contrast works best in a neutral or dark room, where the black panel and the glowing color can be the one bold thing. This is not a quiet piece, and it is not meant to be.
