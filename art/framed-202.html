One wobbly orange ribbon frames the blue. Then you spot the eyes.

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Orange Ribbon Frame is a horizontal abstract of glossy cobalt and teal swirls, held inside a loose frame of saturated orange.

You have a wall that has bothered you for a while. Maybe the new sofa made it look bare. Maybe the old picture now looks small in a bigger room. The furniture and paint are settled, and the wall is the one thing still waiting.

Picture a horizontal piece there with one bold idea: a wavy orange ribbon running along the top, down the left side and across the bottom, holding two cool panels of cobalt and teal. It is not another quiet blue abstract. The frame gives it a clear shape, so it reads from across the room.

I'd start where your eye starts, with the orange. It is a saturated orange-gold, heaviest at the top right and along the lower edge. It wobbles like a rectangle drawn freehand, and that looseness keeps the frame from feeling stiff.

Inside it sit a dark blue square on the left and a larger panel of teal and blue ripples on the right. Thin bands of cobalt, emerald and navy run side by side like marbled paper or poured paint. The edges between colors are crisp, and pale highlights along the swirls give everything a wet, reflective sheen.

Then the motion shows up. Ribbons fold and curl into small whirlpool spirals at the top center, along the left edge and in the teal panel. A bright green curl sits near the top.

In the right panel, the swirled shapes may look like a face with two spiral eyes. That is a chance resemblance, not something I set out to draw. Some people see it right away and some never do. It is a small, playful extra for anyone who looks a little longer.

The horizontal format suits a wall that runs wide: above a sofa, a console or a bed, or behind a desk. The blues and teals settle comfortably into modern rooms. The orange brings in a lively accent that can anchor the space.

The mood is energetic, playful and bold. If a room feels a little too careful, this piece loosens it up.

Imagine walking into the room and seeing that orange ribbon frame the blue across the way. Imagine a guest asking where it came from, and noticing the little spirals when they step closer.
