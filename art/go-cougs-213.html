Face, mask or crest? Look at the red diamond first

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Crimson Emblem is a mirrored red and white digital abstract, glossy forms swirling out from one small red diamond.

The sofa is in. The paint is settled. And one wall still looks unfinished, the way it has for months. Whatever hangs there needs to hold its own, and most of what you find online looks like everything else.

Now picture a horizontal piece in saturated red and glossy white, mirrored exactly left to right. Two pale ovals sit either side of a small red diamond. Curving white lines frame them, and crimson shapes crown the top. It stops you before you know what you're looking at.

I'll tell you what I see when I stand in front of this one. The eye goes to the red diamond in the middle first. It's small, and everything else is arranged around it.

From there the eye travels outward along the curves. Because the two halves mirror each other, the movement feels steady and ceremonial, not chaotic. The forms look wet and polished, like melted glass or liquid metal. Fine white lines trace their edges like thin reflections.

Then the other details appear. The reds deepen to near-black in the folds. Burnt orange and copper brown warm the edges. Silvery grey spreads along the bottom. A thin white outline runs near the borders like a rounded frame.

Some people see a face. Some see a mask or a crest. Nothing here is literally depicted, so I'd rather leave that to you.

The horizontal format and the centred symmetry suit a wall that needs one anchor: over a sofa, a bed, a console or a fireplace, or at the end of a hallway. The red and white contrast holds up against neutral walls and gives a quiet room one dramatic point of emphasis.

It's bold, ornate and a little ceremonial. That's how it looks to me, not a meaning I'm handing you.
