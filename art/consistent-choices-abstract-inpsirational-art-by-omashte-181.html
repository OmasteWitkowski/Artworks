Small Choices, Framed in Glowing Amber and Gold

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

A dark red quote on a pale butter-yellow oval, held inside a mirrored border of amber, gold and orange: a digital piece called Radical Difference.

Picture the wall you look at most: across from your desk, beside the front door, down the hallway you walk a dozen times a day. Maybe it has been bare for a while, or it holds something you stopped seeing long ago.

Now put a pale yellow oval there, lit like glass with the light behind it. Three lines of dark red type sit inside it, and an amber-and-gold border wraps around them. That is Radical Difference.

The words come first, and you can read them from across the room: Small, Smart Choices + Consistency + Time = RADICAL DIFFERENCE, credited on the piece to Darren Hardy. It is set like an equation, so the sentence reads as a sum that adds up.

Then the border pulls you in. Overlapping oval and bean-shaped segments repeat evenly on every side, mirrored, with pale yellow ovals at the corners. Along the edges, the orange ovals hold smooth, flowing swirls, darker at the centre. They look like polished amber or stained glass, and they give the frame real depth.

Come closer and the yellow areas turn out to carry a fine, mosaic-like grain with a slight painterly feel. Deep red-maroon runs around the outer edge and holds the whole glow in place. This is a digital piece, and what I care about is what it does when you stand in front of it: the border keeps your eye moving in an even loop and sends it back to the words.

The horizontal format and the centred, symmetrical layout suit a wall you face head-on: above or across from a desk, in a home office, in an entryway, along a hallway. The warm yellows and ambers add colour to a neutral room, and the deep red edge holds up against the surrounding wall.

Because the text reads from across a room, it works at a medium viewing distance, not just up close. It is also a decorative piece in its own right, so the wall still looks finished if you stop noticing the words.
