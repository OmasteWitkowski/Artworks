Three Glowing Shapes That Look Like Backlit Glass

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Amber Drift is a horizontal abstract in which a golden-amber form, a red circle and a cobalt-blue dome float in soft pink, lavender and tan swirls.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and the wide space above it still looks bare. Anything small would look lost there, and anything generic would look like it came with the house.

Picture this in that space: a heavy golden-amber shape filling the center and right, a red circle sitting above it, and a bright blue dome pushing up from the bottom edge. Each shape has a thin rim of light, so they look like backlit glass floating in soft swirls. It is bold color, but it does not feel loud.

From across the room, you see three big shapes: amber, red and blue. They balance each other, and they crop off every edge of the frame, so the image feels larger than the picture itself.

Step closer and the surface changes. Small whirlpool curls and pointed, flame-like tendrils sit where the colors meet. A fine, even texture runs across the whole piece, and the pink, lavender and tan backgrounds marble into each other.

A pale lilac arc sweeps down the right side and carries your eye around the amber form. The tan side on the left is quieter, so the eye has somewhere calm to rest before it goes back to the color.

I make work to be looked into, not just glanced at. I won't tell you what this one means. Whatever you feel standing in front of it is the part that counts.

Amber Drift is a horizontal piece with a strong center of gravity, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a dining buffet. The warm amber, crimson and cobalt, softened by orchid pink, lavender and dusty tan, give a room one clear focal point.

It suits a room that welcomes confident color. The soft swirls and glowing edges keep that color from feeling harsh, so the mood is warm, lively and a little dreamy.
