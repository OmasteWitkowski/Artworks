Every Tulip Here Wears Its Red and Cream Differently

Botanical & Flowers · horizontal

Crimson Flames is a close-up photograph of red and white flamed tulips, set against dark mulch and purple-striped leaves.

The sofa is new, the paint is settled, and one wall still looks bare. You have probably scrolled past a lot of flower pictures that all look the same: one colour, one tidy bouquet, nothing to stop on.

This one might stop you. A cluster of red and white tulips leans in every direction, and no two blooms are marked quite alike.

At first you see red and white. Look a little longer and the petals separate into individuals. On one tulip the crimson climbs high from a cream base. On another the cream takes over and the red only edges the petals. The red is feathered like flames, and each petal ends in a point that flares outward.

The ground does a lot of the work. Dark mulch, dry brown leaves and fine twigs sit under the satiny petals, so the colour seems to glow. The soft, even light leaves no harsh shadows. The sharpest focus falls on the central and upper blooms, and the background melts away behind them.

Then the smaller things appear. Broad green leaves with purple-toned stripes sweep across the frame on diagonals. Small periwinkle-blue flowers sit at the upper left. A blurred pink bud touches the right edge. A few blooms are open enough to show golden yellow centres, and others are still closing.

I won't tell you what it means. It is a bright, lively spring garden, and what you feel in front of it is yours.

The horizontal shape suits the wall above a console, sofa or dining table, and it can hang over a bed. The saturated red gives it presence even at a modest size, so it can hold a wall without being enormous.
