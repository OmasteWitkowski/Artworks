A Face Hides in These Mirrored Yellow Flowers

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Sunmask is a glowing, symmetrical abstract in which blurred yellow flowers merge into a mask-like face on a dark green field.

You bought the sofa. The paint is done. And the wall above it still looks like nobody has decided anything yet.

What that wall needs is one thing that holds the room: a bright centre, a dark surround, something you notice from the doorway and still find new details in a month later. Sunmask is my answer to that wall.

From across the room, you see a glow. A saturated yellow centre sits in a dark green-black field, and it looks lit from within.

Come closer and the glow turns into flowers. They are blurred and streaked with motion at the heart, but at the edges the petals and leaves keep sharper outlines. Small clusters of yellow and pink blossoms repeat on the left and right, mirrored exactly around a central line.

Then the centre shifts. The mirrored shapes form a triangular nose, dark eye-like hollows and a wide lower curve, and a mask looks back at you. Look longer and it can be a totem, a creature, or a garden again. I don't fix it as one thing. What you see in it is yours.

Around the face, concentric arcs and fine looping light trails ripple outward, and the yellow forms flare upward like flames. Teal wave-like bands run across the bottom.

Sunmask is a horizontal piece, so it suits the wide, low wall above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The dark surround lets the yellow centre act as the one bright focal point in the room.

It is vivid, playful and a little otherworldly. It will sit most naturally in an eclectic or contemporary room that can take a strong dose of colour.
