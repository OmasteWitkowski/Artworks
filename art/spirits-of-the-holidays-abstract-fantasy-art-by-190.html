A Red Snowflake That Glows Like Stained Glass

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Snowflake in Red and Gold is a large, symmetrical crimson snowflake on clean white, filled with swirling red, orange and amber gold.

You have a wall that has looked bare for months. It might be above the mantel, the console or the entry table. The furniture is settled and the paint is right, but the wall isn't finished. When the holidays put a date on the room, you notice it again.

Now picture one red shape on that wall, a snowflake, but not the icy blue kind. This one is deep crimson, with gold running through it like light behind glass, set in plenty of clean white.

From across the room, you see the silhouette first. It has six arms, feathered with smaller side branches, and each main arm ends in a small rounded knob. It is flat, centered and perfectly mirrored left to right and top to bottom, a little wider than it is tall.

Come closer and the red stops being a solid color. It is full of swirling, marbled bands of crimson, burnt orange and amber gold, with traces of maroon, purple and magenta. The gold streaks look luminous against the darker red. There is no cast light or shadow here, so the glow comes from the color itself.

Closer still, the surface opens into fine curved bands and tiny speckles, a stained-glass, kaleidoscopic effect. The shape holds still while the pattern inside it flows outward from the small oval medallion at the center. That is why I say look closer.

The horizontal format suits a wall above a mantel, a console, a sofa or an entry table. Because the snowflake is wider than it is tall, it fills a wide wall better than a narrow one.

The red and gold read as festive, and the white space keeps it clean and graphic. It can be the single statement piece in a winter-leaning living space, and it is also simply a bold image for anyone who likes a snowflake with some fire in it.
