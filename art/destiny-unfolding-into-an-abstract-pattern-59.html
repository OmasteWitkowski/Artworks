A dark navy cross, four corners of ember-colored swirls

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Quadrants is a mirrored abstract in orange, gold and cyan that glows against a deep navy center, inside a wavy turquoise border.

You have a wall you've been looking at for months. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and the one thing missing is something with enough presence to hold the room. Or the art you hung is now too small for the space.

Picture this one there. A deep navy cross sits in the middle, and orange, gold and yellow swirls curl around bright cyan orbs in all four corners. From across the room it reads as one strong blue-and-orange contrast.

I'll tell you what I see, because the image does the talking. The center is a dark navy cross with pointed, orange-edged peaks at the top and bottom. It works like a lantern: the darkest part of the picture, with everything bright gathered around it.

The glow comes from that contrast. The cyan and yellow orbs and the cream highlights in the gold look backlit, a bit like stained glass with light behind it. There are no cast shadows, only luminous color.

The mirror symmetry is exact, so the four corner clusters match. The shapes curl outward from the center, your eye travels around the corners, and it settles back in the dark middle.

Then there's the border. Step closer and the wavy turquoise-green frame is full of thin, marbled swirls that you miss from across the room. The piece is listed as digital art, a mirrored abstract composition.

This is a horizontal piece, so it suits the wide spaces: above a sofa, a console or a bed, or at the end of an entry. The strict symmetry gives a wall a clear center, and the blue-and-orange palette brings a burst of color to a living room, office or hallway.

It is vivid and energetic, with an ordered feel from the symmetry. If your room is quiet and neutral, this can be the one piece that carries the color.
