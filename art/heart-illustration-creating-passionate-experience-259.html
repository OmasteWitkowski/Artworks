This Heart Is Built Like a Window of Red, Mustard and Olive

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Heart of Scrolls is a single large, mirror-symmetrical heart filled with scrolls, crescents and a green four-lobed center, outlined in thin gold lines on a cream ground.

You know the wall. It's over the bed, the sofa or the console, and it has bothered you for months. The furniture is settled and the paint is done, but the space above it still feels unfinished.

Picture one centered image there: a large heart in warm orange-red, mustard and olive, resting on soft cream. From a distance it is a clear, friendly shape. Step closer and it stops being a simple symbol.

Start at the center line. Everything on the left appears again on the right, as a mirror image. At the heart's core sits a green four-lobed shape, like a small cross. Above it, paired red scrolls and mustard crescents curl outward. Orange-red ribbons run down the middle, and dark blue-purple wedges sit at the sides.

Every shape is outlined in thin gold-tan lines, the way leading holds the glass in a church window. Inside those lines the color glows, with glossy gradients and deeper crimson and navy-violet for depth. It reads less like a flat icon and more like a lit window.

Look closer still. The green and yellow areas carry a faint mosaic-like grain and a marbled swirl. Behind the heart, the cream ground is patterned with a delicate network of rounded, lace-like cells in pale peach. The scrolls sweep outward and always come back to the middle.

I'm happy to tell you what I see in it. What it means to you is yours to decide.

The image is horizontal, with a generous margin of cream around the heart. That margin reads as quiet space, so the piece works well alone on one wall: over a bed, above a console, over a sofa or at the end of a hallway.

The warm reds and mustard pick up wood tones. The olive and cream sit comfortably with natural textiles, and deeper navy accents echo the dark wedges at the sides of the heart.
