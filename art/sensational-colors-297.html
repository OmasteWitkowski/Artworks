Where Hot Orange Meets a Cool, Glossy Blue Current

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Heat Meets Water is a wide, horizontal swirl abstract in cobalt, teal, mustard and fiery orange, with thin bright lines that make every curl look like molten glass.

You know the wall. It might be the long one over the sofa, or the one behind the bed. The furniture is settled and the paint is done, and the wall still looks like it is waiting for something.

This is the picture I would hold up to that wall. Deep blue and teal sweep in from the lower left, a hot red-orange flares in from the edge, and a yellow-olive field ripples across the top. Bold colour and motion like this can stop you from across the room.

There is no subject to work out. It is loops, spirals and teardrop-shaped eddies, and the whole frame is moving. A broad blue current climbs diagonally from the lower left and curls into a large spiral just right of centre.

What holds me is where the orange meets the blue. Thin, bright lines trace every curve, and glossy highlights run along the edges, so the shapes look like polished glass or wet metal. The glow is strongest right where the two colours collide.

Under all that smoothness there is a fine, woven, canvas-like grain. Glossy and grainy sit side by side, and your eye keeps moving between them.

Come close and the small eddies start to show. Step back and it reads as one current. I would rather you stand in front of it than read about it. What it feels like is yours to decide.

This is a wide, horizontal picture with edge-to-edge colour, so I picture it on a long wall: over a sofa, a bed, a console or a sideboard. It is built to carry the colour of a room, not to sit quietly in the corner of it.

Because it is so saturated, it works best as the one bold note on a wall rather than one of many. I especially like the idea of it in a calm, neutral room that needs a single burst of heat and colour.
