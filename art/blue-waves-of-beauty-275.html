Blue ribbons that look like liquid glass caught mid-pour

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · panoramic vertical

Deep Current is a tall digital abstract in cobalt, royal blue and ultramarine, with glossy white-blue highlights running down every fold.

You know the wall. It might be the stairwell, the hallway, or the slim stretch beside a doorway. The paint is done and the furniture is settled, and that one tall space still looks like nothing has been decided.

Now picture a long drop of deep blue there. Ribbons of cobalt and ultramarine twist and fold from top to bottom, edged in pale blue and white, and they look wet. It is a tall piece for a tall wall.

From across the room, Deep Current reads as colour and motion. Cobalt, royal blue and ultramarine fill the frame edge to edge, and dark navy settles into the gaps between the folds. There is no single focal point. Your eye moves up and down the length of the piece along dark channels and bright ridges.

Step closer and it changes. Pale-blue and white highlights trace the edge of every curve, and the surface looks smooth, glassy and polished, like light on liquid or molten glass. Small eddies and curls gather at the upper right, the left middle and the bottom.

Closer still, there are fine rippled lines and contour-like edges layered over one another. I would rather you find them yourself than have me list them. That is what I mean by look closer.

Some people will see water, some glass, some a current pulling through. It is felt, and yours to interpret.

The shape does most of the work. It is tall and narrow, so it suits a stairwell, a hallway, a slim wall beside a doorway, or the space above a console or headboard. These are places where a wide landscape would feel wrong and a small frame would feel lost.

The deep blue holds its own against pale walls and warm wood. The mood is cool, energetic and fluid. It is rich and immersive, and the colour and motion are reason enough to want it.
