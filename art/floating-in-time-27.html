One Still Pink Flower at the Eye of a Spinning World

Botanical & Flowers · horizontal

Eye of the Swirl, Pink Flower is a digitally altered flower photograph: a crisp bloom with crimson stamens, circled by a whirlpool of orange, gold and green.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall still looks bare every time you walk past it. You've pictured something there. You just haven't found the piece that sits right.

Picture this one in that space: a single pink flower, seen from above, in the middle of a wide frame. Around it, orange and green streak into a spiral that pulls your eye straight to its red center.

At first glance it's a flower, pink and ruffled, lit brightly. Then you notice the background. The grass behind it has been twisted into a spiral, so the whole surrounding world seems to be turning while the bloom does not move.

This is a photograph of a flower with the background digitally altered, and that contrast is what the piece is about. The petals are papery and finely veined, with lilac shadows tucked between the folds. Everything around them is smeared into streaky, brush-like color.

Follow the concentric bands inward and they all end in the same place: a small cluster of deep crimson stamens. A thin blade of green grass curves along the upper left, following the spiral around.

I'd rather you stand in front of it than read about it. It's energetic and a little playful, and it doesn't ask you to take it seriously.

The format is horizontal, with one strong centered focal point and saturated warm color. That suits a wall over a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk, where you want one lively image to anchor the space.
