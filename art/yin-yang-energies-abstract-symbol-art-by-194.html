A white yin-yang cut into a glassy swirl of indigo and gold

Spiritual, Mandala & Symbolic · vertical

Indigo Veil Yin Yang is a tall oval of mirrored, silky color around a clean cream-white symbol, available as a print on Fine Art America.

You know the wall. It might be the narrow stretch by the front door, the space above a dresser, or the quiet corner of the bedroom. The furniture is settled and the paint is chosen, and that one tall, slim space still looks unfinished.

This is the piece I'd hold up to it. A tall cream oval, a clean white yin-yang, and around it a dense field of indigo, red-violet and gold that looks like folded silk or liquid glass.

At first glance you see the symbol: a cream S-curve and a patterned half, divided by one flowing line. Then you notice that each half holds a small dot of the opposite tone. A white oval sits in the patterned half and a deep indigo oval sits in the white half.

Then the pattern pulls you in. Fine ripples and lace-like folds spread outward from the center in mirrored waves, like a kaleidoscope. A few touches of crimson glow near the middle. The top is dark indigo moving to glowing red-violet, and toward the bottom it softens into sand, amber and cream.

The white half stays flat and empty, and that is the point of the piece. All that dense, glossy color needs somewhere quiet to rest, and the white half gives it that.

I find that most yin-yang art is either flat black and white or loosely decorative. Here the symbol is crisp and the surface around it is rich. You can read it as balance and duality, or you can simply enjoy the color and the sheen. Either response is a good one.

The tall vertical oval is made for the wall that wide landscapes can't use: a narrow stretch, the space above a console or dresser, an entryway, a bedroom, a study, or a quiet corner. The cream ground is clean and the oval fills most of the frame, so one piece can hold the wall by itself.
