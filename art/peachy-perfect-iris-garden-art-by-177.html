An iris that glows like an ember against the dark

Botanical & Flowers · vertical

Ember Ruffles is a single orange bearded iris, ruffled apricot above, veined rust and cream below, lit against a deep green-brown ground.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall above it still looks unfinished. Maybe the old picture now looks small in the room. Maybe you have walked past the same bare space for months.

Picture a single orange iris there, seen straight on and filling the frame. It is lit like an ember against a dark, softly clouded ground, and it is the only thing your eye has to settle on.

I won't dress this up with a made-up backstory. The piece is the story, and it rewards a slow look. I made it as a digital painterly floral portrait, and what I want you to notice is how much is going on inside one flower.

Start at the top. The apricot and burnt-orange petals crinkle, curl and fold over one another, swirling upward and outward. Then let your eye drop to the broad lower petal. It is smoother, streaked with fine rust and cream veins that fan out from a small orange-red beard at the center. The cream at the heart of that petal is the brightest point in the picture.

Two smaller petals reach out to either side, with golden-yellow near their tips, and a bud shows at the bottom edge. A faint pale halo lifts the whole bloom off the shadow. The ruffled part and the veined part are so different that they feel like two moods in one flower, and that contrast is what I keep coming back to.

I believe art is felt, not seen. You don't need to know anything about irises to know whether this one stops you.

This is a vertical piece with one centered subject that fills most of the frame. It works best as the single focal point on a wall, over a sofa, a bed, a sideboard or in an entry, not as one among many in a crowded gallery wall.

The colors are burnt orange, apricot and rust against deep olive and charcoal brown. They sit comfortably with warm wood, deep greens and muted neutrals, and suit rooms where you want something richer and quieter than a pastel floral. The mood is warm, dramatic and a little intimate.

You don't need to be. I believe there is no good or bad art, and your own response is the only test that counts. If this iris stops you, that is reason enough. If it doesn't, keep looking.

Picture that blank wall again, this time with a single orange iris glowing in the dark. The ruffled petals catch your eye as you walk in, and the veined center is the thing you notice when you stop.
