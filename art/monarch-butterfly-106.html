A Glowing Green Spine Between Two Mirrored Fires

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Totem is a symmetrical abstract in burnt orange, rust red and amber, with a backlit green center that reads as a mask or a standing figure.

You know the wall. It's the one over the sofa, or the bed, or the console, where the furniture is settled and the paint is right, and your eye still slides past it every time you walk in.

This piece is built for that wall. Two large masses of burnt orange and rust red curl inward toward a narrow green channel, and the left and right halves match exactly. It's warm and bright, and it's hard to walk past without looking.

I'm not going to hand you a backstory for this one. What I can tell you is what happens when you stand in front of it.

First you see color: orange, rust and amber folds, glowing along their edges against dark maroon recesses. Then you see the green. It runs down the center like a lit core, with small bulb-like shapes stacked along it.

Then the mirroring clicks, and the swirls start to look like a mask, a face or a standing figure. Nothing is literally depicted. That impression comes from the symmetry, and you can just as easily take it as pure color, line and motion.

Come closer and the surface changes again. Many fine contour lines trace the folds, so it looks smooth, glossy and translucent, like satin or molten glass. It's listed as digital art.

The horizontal format and strong center line suit a wall that needs one confident piece: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a fireplace. Because it's mirrored, it sits easily in a symmetrical arrangement, centered on the furniture below it.

The orange and red bring the warmth. The green gives a cool counterpoint, and it works well alongside olive, brown and neutral rooms. It's bold, energetic and a little otherworldly, so it suits a room that can take a statement.
