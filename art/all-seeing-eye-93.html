The Blue Eye Hiding in a Swirl of Color

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Cobalt Watcher is an abstract of swirling liquid color that turns, as you look, into a giant glowing blue eye.

You know the wall. It's over the sofa, or the console, or the desk. The furniture is settled and the paint is done, and that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished every time you walk in.

Now picture a blue eye on it. At first it is only color, swirling and glossy, then it resolves into a bright cobalt iris with a small dark pupil, looking back at you.

I'll tell you what I see, because it is all I can say for certain. A bright cobalt-blue oval sits at the center like an iris. Just below the middle is a small dark teardrop, like a pupil. Around it run bands of white, rust-orange, amber, brown and yellow, and their scalloped, flame-like edges read as lashes or eyelids.

The image is abstract, and the eye still reads at once. Concentric rings lead you inward to the dark pupil. The blue seems to glow against the near-black navy ring around it, as if the light were coming from inside the color.

Then there is the movement. Ripples spiral in toward the blue, the outer bands flow sideways, and the edges flare outward, so the whole thing seems to turn slowly.

You can take it as pure color and motion. You can take it as a face looking back. I'd rather you decide than tell you what it means.

The composition is horizontal and centered, with the oval filling most of the frame. That makes it a natural fit over a sofa, a console or a desk, or anywhere a wide wall needs a single strong focal point.
