Where Hot Red Meets Cool Blue at a Glowing Edge

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember and Tide is a horizontal abstract close-up in red, cobalt and aqua green, with a pale glowing edge and one small round spot where the eye lands.

You know the wall. It's over the sofa, the bed or the console. The furniture is settled and the paint is done, and for months that stretch of wall has still looked unfinished.

Picture a wide band of saturated red across the top, flecked with scratchy gold and orange. Below it sits a deep field of blue, dusted with pale speckles. Between them runs a thin, glowing edge. That is Ember and Tide.

What holds me is where the warm and cool meet. The red feels heavy and still. The green and blue seem to be folding into one another, and a bright pale edge runs diagonally across the right side, leading your eye from the middle of the picture outward.

A pale green wedge with soft swirls points down just left of centre. Near its tip sits one small round spot, like a bubble, and it gives the eye somewhere to land.

There is no horizon and no object to anchor it, so you decide what you are looking at. Come closer and it keeps opening up: fine speckles, tiny bubbles, faint vein-like lines through the green and blue. Look closer. A few inches of colour hold more than they first let on.

Ember and Tide is a horizontal piece, so it suits a wide wall: over a sofa, a bed or a console. The red is heavy and the blue is deep, and both need some breathing room around them.

Against white, grey or deeper blue walls, the red leads and the blue settles in behind it. If your room is calm and neutral and you want one bold colour moment, this is the kind of piece that does the job.
