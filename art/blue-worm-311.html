One Cobalt Orb, Lit From Behind, for One Bare Wall

Abstract Glass Macro · horizontal

Blue Watcher is a glossy cobalt-blue oval ringed in turquoise and green, with a swirling golden halo around it.

You know the wall. It's over the sofa, the bed or the console, and it has bothered you for months. The furniture is right and the paint is settled, but the wall still looks unfinished. Maybe the new sofa made it worse.

Now picture one deep cobalt-blue oval filling most of that space. It is glossy and dotted with tiny bubbles. Around it are rings of turquoise and green, and beyond them streaks of gold and amber orange sweep around the edge as if the whole thing were slowly turning.

The contrast is what holds me. The centre is dark, dense, saturated blue. The edges are warm, and the yellow looks lit from behind. Light seems to pass through the blue and the translucent rings around it.

Some people see an eye. Others see a polished bead or a jewel. I leave that to you. I won't tell you what it is, because what you see first is the point.

Look closer and the surface changes. The blue is smooth and wet-looking. The rings are finely ridged and streaked. The left side is busier, with thin dark lines crossing blurs of violet and orange. The right side is more open and curved. What you notice first may not be what you notice after a few minutes.

The format is horizontal, and the oval sits comfortably in it. That suits the long wall over a sofa, a bed or a console. The blue is dark and dense, so it holds the wall from across the room.

In a room of neutrals, warm wood or cream, it gives you one strong point of blue to build the space around. It works best where the room can take a single bold statement.
