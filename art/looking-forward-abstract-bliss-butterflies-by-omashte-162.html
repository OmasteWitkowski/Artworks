A Black-and-White Eye Set in a Wing That Glows

Butterflies, Birds & Wildlife · horizontal

Emberwing is a stained glass style butterfly in orange, yellow and crimson, flying alone across a clean white background.

You've got the sofa, the paint and the lamp. The wall is still bare, or it holds something that has stopped meaning anything to you. Maybe it's the wall by the doorway, the one over the desk, or the one in the reading corner.

Picture a single butterfly there, on clean white, with a wing that looks lit from behind. Orange and yellow glow in the middle, a jagged crimson edge frames them, and one black-and-white eye sits at the center. That's Emberwing.

I'll tell you what is in the image, because that's what I can say for certain. The butterfly is in profile, flying to the right. Its large wing is filled with orange and yellow that swirl like marbled glass, with soft ripples inside the color and a few olive-gold patches where it deepens.

The red edge is a different thing. It is flat, smooth and dark, and it ends in jagged points that sweep back like flame. Against it, the orange and yellow look lit from behind. Thin white lines divide the wing into segments, so the whole figure looks cut out and leaded, like a window.

Then there's the eye. A bold black-and-white shape sits in the middle of the wing, and it's where your gaze lands first. I read it as an eye mark. You might see a target or a graphic symbol. Either is fine. Your own response is the only test that counts.

Emberwing is a horizontal piece with one figure and plenty of white space, so it works as a single bold accent. I think of it on a small-to-medium wall: beside a doorway, over a desk or shelf, in an entryway, or in a reading corner. The warm color carries across a room.

It pairs well with neutral walls and dark frames, where the orange and crimson become the one warm note. The mood is bold, fiery and energetic. That comes from what's on the page, and how it feels to you is yours to decide.
