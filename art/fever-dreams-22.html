Orange and Red Ribbons Curling Into Electric Blue

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Tide is a horizontal abstract of glossy orange, amber and deep red folds over rippling blue, set against dark navy.

You know the wall. It's the wide one above the sofa, or the bed, or the fireplace. The furniture is settled and the paint is right, but the wall still looks unfinished, and you've been walking past it for months.

Now picture a large loop of red-orange curling across the top of that wall, folding down into electric blue. The edges fall away into dark navy, so the color seems to glow from inside the frame. That is Ember Tide.

The first thing I see is the loop. A big ribbon of red-orange curls across the upper center, with bright gold along its edges. It's the warmest, brightest part of the image, so that is where the eye lands.

From there the eye follows the curve down into the blue. The lower half is cool and rippling, with touches of magenta, lavender and teal tucked into the currents. Bands of orange and amber cross into the blue on the right, so the warm and cool colors meet rather than sit apart.

Then the dark areas start to matter. Navy and near-black sit between the folds and along the edges, especially at the left and upper right. They are why the bright colors look lit from within. Look closer and the surface has fine feathery ripples, tiny speckles and hairline streaks in the red and the blue below.

Nothing in it is a recognizable object. It reads as liquid in motion, and what it means is up to you.

The format is horizontal, so this suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a fireplace. The dark edges frame the image and ground it, so it anchors the wall instead of floating on it.

The mood is energetic, fiery and dramatic. It works in a room where you want one strong focal point, such as a modern living room, a bedroom or an office wall, and it pairs naturally with navy, deep blue or neutral surroundings.

I can't promise a screen shows every shade exactly, because screens differ. Expect saturated orange, deep red and electric blue against dark navy. If the print isn't what you hoped for, Fine Art America's 30-day money-back guarantee applies.

I believe there is no good or bad art, and your own response is the only test. If this image stopped you and you can picture it on your wall, that's enough. You don't need to know anything about art to trust that.

Picture the wall again, this time finished: red-orange folds curling above the sofa, electric blue rippling below, and dark edges holding it all in. When someone asks where you found it, you can say you picked it because you liked it.
