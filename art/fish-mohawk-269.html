A Green Dome in a Glow of Coral: Flower or Liquid Glass?

Abstract Glass Macro · horizontal

Emerald Lantern is a horizontal abstract in which a dark emerald dome sits among coral and blush-pink shapes that seem lit from behind.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall still feels unfinished. A neutral room can carry a lot of colour, but it needs the right anchor.

This is what I would put there. A dark emerald dome sits at the centre of the frame, and coral, blush pink and cream shapes spread around it and glow as if a light were behind them.

I can only tell you what is in the image, so that is what I will do. From across the room you see the contrast: dense green against warm, glowing colour. Step closer and the surface starts to give itself up.

Thin white highlights run along the edge of the green. Tiny bubbles are scattered across the lower right and upper left. Thick ridges and a slow green drip at the upper right make it look like liquid that has pooled and then set. At the lower left, the warmth cools into violet and blue.

It looks like a flower from one angle and like backlit glass from another, and it never settles fully into either. I would rather you decide what it is than have me tell you. Whatever you see in it is the right answer.

The format is horizontal, so it suits a wide wall: over a sofa, a bed, a long console or a dining buffet. The saturated colour and the edge-to-edge crop are built to hold a big space, so you can choose a generous size without the piece looking lost.

The colours are jewel-like rather than pastel. In a room of whites, greys, wood or linen, the green gives a deep anchor and the coral and pink add warmth.
