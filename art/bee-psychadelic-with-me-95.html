A Bee Caught Mid-Hover Beside a Tall Red Flower

Botanical & Flowers · panoramic vertical

"Quiet Hover, Bee and Red Flower Spike" is a tall, narrow botanical photograph with a swirl-textured background, made for the wall that is too slim for most art.

You know the wall. It might be the hallway, the stairwell, the gap between two windows or the strip beside a door. Everything you have looked at is too wide, too square or too tame for it.

This piece was made in the shape that wall asks for. A single flower spike runs from the top edge to the bottom, and a small bee hovers beside it in the open space on the left.

The first thing you see is colour: a coral-red stem hung with small, rounded, amber-orange buds on thin side stalks. Behind it the background moves from green at the top to tan and soft brown below.

Then the bee. It is yellow and black, caught in mid-flight, facing the stem. The flower is perfectly still, and the bee is the only thing in the picture that seems to be moving.

Stay a little longer and you notice that the buds grow larger and more spread out toward the bottom, and that the stem curves gently as it falls. A deep shadow runs down one side of it. Behind it all, faint swirls and spirals give the background a layered, canvas-like surface.

I would rather you stand in front of it than read about it. Whatever it says to you is yours to decide.

The extreme vertical shape suits narrow wall spaces: a hallway, a stairwell, the space between two windows, or a spot beside a doorway. The flower fills the full height, so the wall is used rather than worked around.

The red and orange give a small space one strong point of colour, and the olive green and tan keep it grounded. A bee and a flower also sit comfortably in a kitchen or a sunroom.

Screens differ, so I can't promise an exact match. The palette is coral red, rust orange and olive green with golden yellow and warm tan. If it isn't what you pictured, the 30-day money-back guarantee applies.

I don't think there is good or bad art, and your own response is the only test. If the bee and the red stem stop you, that is reason enough. If they don't, it is simply not your piece.

Picture the hallway with this at the end of it, or the narrow wall beside the window, with a red flower spike running floor to ceiling and a bee hovering in the space beside it.
