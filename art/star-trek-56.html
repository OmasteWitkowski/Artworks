A Face Appears in the Glow, Then Dissolves Back Into Ribbons

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Mask is a mirror-symmetrical digital abstract: amber, gold and peach ribbons glowing against near-black around a mask-like face that may or may not be there.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is done, and that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished. You've walked past it for months, and nothing you've found has felt right.

Now picture a horizontal piece there, with deep amber orange and golden yellow glowing against a near-black ground. It's the warmest, most focused thing in the room, and your eye goes to it first.

At first it reads as pure pattern: ribbons of amber, peach and gold curling outward in perfect left-right mirror symmetry. Then the middle comes together. Two spiral eyes, a pointed nose and a wide curved band below form a mask, framed by a rounded golden halo.

The face is an optical suggestion, not a depicted subject. It appears when your eye joins the spirals, the point and the band. Look a moment longer and it dissolves back into ribbon and curve. You may see it differently each time you look.

The light is what holds you there. The bright bands look backlit, and thin pale highlights trace the edges of each curve. The whole piece seems to glow from within, like molten glass or a stained-glass window with the sun behind it.

Up close, the surface is smooth and glossy, built from overlapping translucent layers with fine concentric lines. I'd rather you stand in front of it than read about it. What it means is yours to decide.

Ember Mask is horizontal, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a fireplace. The near-black ground and the single central focus keep it readable from across the room.

The mood is warm, dramatic and a little otherworldly. It suits a room that wants one bold point of color rather than something quiet and neutral. It sits especially well where the walls or furniture are already settled and the room wants a strong center.
