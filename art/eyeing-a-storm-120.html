Is it an iris or a whirlpool? Either way, it stops you.

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Jewel Eye Vortex is a cobalt blue abstract, ringed in lime green and wrapped in golden bands, for the wall that needs a center.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished. You have been walking past it for months.

Now picture a deep cobalt oval at the center of it, ringed in bright green and swept around by golden bands. That is Jewel Eye Vortex. It holds the room from across the floor, and it rewards you when you step closer.

The first thing you see is the blue. It is a large oval, dark and saturated, marked with fine rippled lines that look like folds or shell ridges. Around it runs a ring of lime green, and around that, wavy golden-yellow bands that flow like ripples on water.

Then the light gets you. The surfaces look glassy and wet, as if light were passing through them. Bright highlights run along the ridges, and the creases fall away to a near-black navy. Small pinpoint glints catch at the right. At the upper left there is a rougher, flaky brown patch, and it gives all that smoothness something to push against.

The rings pull your eye inward, so it reads as an iris or as a vortex seen from very close. I will leave the choice to you. The image fills the frame to every edge, with no horizon and no empty space, so there is nowhere to rest except the center.

It is a horizontal image, so it sits naturally over a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. Its strong center and saturated color give a wall a focal point, and the colors read from a distance.

It suits a room that can carry bold color and a clear focus, such as a living room, studio, office or entry. Blue and gold are the main colors, so look at what is already near the wall and see if they sit well with it.

You don't need to be. I believe there is no good or bad art, and your own response is the only test that counts. If you keep coming back to the blue center, that is your answer.

Picture the wall again, this time with the blue eye over the sofa and the gold rings catching the light from the window. The next guest who stops in front of it will ask where it came from.
