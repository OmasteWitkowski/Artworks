Red and Yellow Swirls That Glow Like Light Through Glass

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Current is a digital abstract of translucent red, orange and yellow ribbons, set in a deep black mat with one cool teal patch.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall above it still looks bare every time you walk in. You have probably held up a few ideas in your head and none of them felt right.

Picture a wide, horizontal piece there, in saturated red and golden yellow. The colour looks lit from behind, like light passing through translucent liquid, and a deep black border makes it burn. That is Ember Current.

I'll be plain about this one: I'm not going to tell you what it means. What I can tell you is what happens when you stand in front of it.

First you see the heat. Red ribbons twist across the upper corners, and yellow and orange billow through the middle. Cream-white light glows behind them, and thin bright edges on the curls catch like highlights. The shapes read as glass or colored liquid, with overlapping translucent circles that look as if light is moving through them.

Then your eye starts to travel. It follows the curves, gets pulled around the small circular eddies, and drifts down to the one patch of teal-green low on the left. That cool spot is the only counterpoint to all the warmth, and it makes the reds and yellows feel hotter.

Then you notice the frame. The abstract sits in a wide black mat with a fine, faintly woven texture, and around the mat is a soft, blurred halo of the same colors. The mat is quiet and the swirls inside it are glossy and smooth. Nothing else in the image looks like that contrast.

The horizontal format suits wide spaces: above a sofa, a console or a bed. The black mat looks especially at home on neutral, charcoal or dark walls, where the red and gold can be the loudest thing in the room.

The mood is warm, energetic and flowing. It is a piece you choose because the color and motion catch you, and that is reason enough.

Measure the space first. Some people lay paper or painter's tape on the wall to see the footprint. The piece is horizontal, so it suits a wide stretch of wall. You choose from the sizes offered on the listing page, and the 30-day money-back guarantee is there if it doesn't work.

Screens vary, so I can't promise a match. The reds, golds and the deep black may look slightly different depending on your display and your room's light. The 30-day money-back guarantee means you can see it on your own wall and decide.

Your own response is the only test I trust. If the glow and the swirling color make you want to keep looking, that is enough. You don't need to know what it means or have a reason to give a guest.

Picture the wall again, this time with that glow on it: red and gold ribbons curling in a black border, one cool teal patch, and the light of the room catching the colors when you walk in.
