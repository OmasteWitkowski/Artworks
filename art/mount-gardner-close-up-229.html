One Blue Sky, One White Summit, One Bare Wall

Pacific Northwest Landscapes · horizontal

Cobalt Summit is a wide, painterly mountain landscape: snow-white peaks against a saturated blue sky, set between dark evergreen slopes.

You know the wall. It's the one above the sofa, or the bed, or the console, and the room has been finished around it for months. The furniture is right and the paint is right, but the wall still looks bare.

This is the piece I'd hold up to that wall. Snow-covered peaks stand against a deep, cloudless blue, with one jagged summit just left of center. Dark evergreen slopes sit on either side, and the whole scene stretches wide. You'll see the white and the blue from across the room.

I'll tell you what I see in it, because that's the honest story of this piece. The first thing is the contrast. The snow is very white and the sky is very blue, and the dark slopes on both sides make them brighter still.

Then your eye starts to travel. Ridgelines run diagonally down from the peaks to the left and right, and you can follow them like paths. Behind a sunlit tan hillside in the front, the foothills overlap in layers, each one a little darker and farther back. That's where the depth comes from.

Look closer and there's a fine painterly texture over everything. It's smooth in the sky and dense and stippled across the trees. It makes the view read as painted as well as photographic, and a familiar mountain scene starts to feel like a picture of one.

I believe art is felt, not seen, and what you feel here is yours. To me it looks like cold, clear air and a lot of open space.

The wide horizontal format suits a wall that needs width: over a sofa, a bed or a console, or across from a desk. About the top third of the image is open blue sky, so the picture has room to breathe.

The palette is cobalt, snow white, forest green and warm tan. It works in a room that wants one confident splash of color, and it sits easily with natural, neutral or wood tones.
