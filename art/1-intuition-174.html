Glossy amber meets flat blue, stitched with thin red lines

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

A horizontal fluid abstract in which a polished, rippled amber form faces matte slate-blue shapes across a quiet beige field.

You know the wall. It's above the sofa, or the bed, or at the end of the hallway. The furniture is settled and the paint is chosen, and that one space still looks unfinished every time you walk past.

This is the piece I'd ask you to picture there. On the left, a glossy, rippled form glows in olive, bronze, amber and orange. On the right, flat blue shapes reach out like fingers, each traced in thin red. Between them is a wide, quiet field of beige.

Start on the left. The form is layered and polished-looking, with fine contour ripples like poured enamel or wood grain. There is a small speckled patch near its right side, and the outline is rough and rippled. A warm yellow-orange glow sits behind it and deepens to red along the bottom.

Then let your eye cross the beige. The blue shapes are smooth and matte, with no shine at all, and they branch toward the lower right as if something were still moving. The thin red lines run through both sides and tie them together.

Some people see a head or a mask in the left form. Others see melted shapes and nothing more. I'm happy with either. What you see is yours to decide.

The horizontal format suits the long spaces: above a sofa, a bed or a console, or at the end of a hallway. The palette is saturated and warm, so it belongs in a room that can take burnt orange, amber and olive. Rooms with wood tones, neutral walls or touches of blue will give it plenty to work with.

The beige center keeps the oranges and the single blue from crowding the room. The mood is energetic, warm and a little playful.
