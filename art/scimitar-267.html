One crooked line cuts through a wall of glowing blue and amber

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

The Crooked Line is a luminous abstract color study: curved, ribbed bands of cobalt blue, amber and pink, crossed by a single dark, wavering line.

You bring home the new sofa and the wall above it suddenly looks bare. The paint is settled and the furniture is where it belongs. That one wide stretch of wall is still waiting.

Picture something there that glows. Curved, ribbed bands of cobalt blue, amber, orange and pink sweep across the frame in a wide arc, lit so strongly they seem to shine from within. Then a thin dark line wanders straight through them.

The color comes first. Cobalt blue and amber sit side by side, with orange and pink between them and pale lavender-white where the light is strongest. The lower edge glows gold-yellow, like a lit rim. Deep red-brown shadow settles into the lower right corner and gives all that brightness something to push against.

Then the line. One thin, dark, wavering mark runs diagonally from the lower left to the upper right and breaks the smooth sweep of the bands. I keep coming back to it. Without it, this would be a lovely, even pattern. With it, your eye has a path to follow.

Stand closer and the surface changes. Fine parallel striations run along each band like grain, and small gold and copper flecks break up the smooth color. The crop is tight, with no horizon and nothing to tell you how big it is. You decide what you are looking at, and I would rather leave that to you.

This is a horizontal piece, so it suits a wall with room to fill: over a sofa, a bed, a console or a stairwell landing. The arc carries the eye along the width of the wall, and the dark red-brown corners give it weight against pale or neutral paint.

The mood is energetic and vivid, with strong contrast and saturated color. It is a piece you can like on sight, with no explanation needed.

Your own response is the only test that counts. I believe there is no good or bad art, only what you feel in front of it. If the glow and that crooked line stopped you, that is reason enough.

Picture the wall again, with the bands arcing across it in blue and amber and that dark line pulling your eye from one side to the other. When someone asks where you found it, you will have an answer, and it will be a piece you chose by looking.
