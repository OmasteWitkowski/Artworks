Cool teal melts into hot red, with one bubble just off center

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Blue Into Red is a wide, glowing abstract in which beaded teal drifts through an amber band into a deep, veined red.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch above it still looks unfinished. Maybe the old piece looks small now, or maybe you never found the right one.

Picture a wide piece there that starts cool on the left and ends hot on the right. Teal blue speckled with tiny beads of light slowly warms through olive and amber into a deep, saturated red. That is Blue Into Red.

I would rather you stand in front of this piece than read about it, so here is the path your eye is likely to take. It starts on the left, in a cool teal blue that is smooth and soft. Tiny round dots are scattered across it, and small bright glints catch on them like beads of water.

Then there is one slightly larger round bubble, just left of center. Past it, the blue drifts into a tilted band of olive and amber that glows as though light were passing through it. Nothing stops at a hard line. The edges melt into each other.

On the right, the mood changes. The red is deep and saturated, with sharper, vein-like ridges and facets where the blue was smooth. You can begin in the cool and travel to the heat, or go the other way round.

There is no right way to read it. Some people will see water, some a sunset, and some will simply enjoy the colour. That is a perfectly good reason to want it. Your own response is the only test that counts.

This is a wide horizontal piece, shaped for the wall above a sofa, a bed, a console or a long sideboard. It has no literal subject to date it or compete with your furniture. It is simply colour, in three loose zones of blue, amber-green and red.

It works in a neutral room that wants one confident hit of colour. It also suits a room that already has teal, rust, red or warm wood, where it can tie those things together.

You don't need a trained eye. If the colour stops you and you keep wanting to look, that is enough. Your own response is the only test that counts.
