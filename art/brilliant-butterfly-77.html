Butterflies Made Entirely of Spirals, Set Like a Tile

Butterflies, Birds & Wildlife · horizontal

Mirror Wings is a digital artwork of two orange butterflies facing each other in a lilac border, with every surface curled into glossy spirals.

You know the wall. It might be above the console, the dresser, the desk or the bed. The furniture is settled and the paint is chosen, but that one space still looks unfinished, and a plain frame of something pleasant hasn't been enough.

Now picture a bright patch of burnt orange there: two large butterflies facing each other, wings striped in black and edged in yellow, set inside a lilac border. It would look cheerful from across the room, and it would still reward a closer look.

I'll tell you what I see, because the image does the talking. Two butterflies mirror each other on a diagonal, with a ribbed, reddish-brown body running between them. Their wings are orange with black stripes, yellow edges and dark dots. Patches of cobalt blue swirl near the body.

Then you notice that nothing in the picture is flat. The wings, the body and the grey-brown background are all made of curling spirals. The surface looks like thick, wet paint or glazed relief, with a smooth sheen. It is not a realistic butterfly portrait. It is one playful pattern, closer to a decorative tile than a field guide.

The spirals keep moving. They ripple through the background and curl inside the wings, so the pair seems to flutter gently while staying still. The wings are cropped at the edges, which fills the frame and brings the butterflies close. The lilac border, dotted with small yellow and orange flower-like marks, holds it all together like a plaque.

This is a horizontal piece with a framed, plaque-like look. I see it as a cheerful accent over a console, desk or dresser, above a bed, or in a nook, kitchen or sunroom. These are walls that want a bright spot of orange and lilac, not a huge statement.

The mood is playful and warm, with a lively decorative energy. If your room leans neutral, it adds colour. If your room already has warm tones, the orange will pick them up.
