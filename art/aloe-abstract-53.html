Blue Stalks That Glow as if Lit From Within

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Electric Thicket is a horizontal digital art piece: a tangle of cobalt, royal and emerald stalks with small sparks of orange-red running through it.

You bought the sofa. The paint is settled. And the wall above it still looks like nobody lives there yet. Or the old picture has started to look small next to the new room, and you notice it every time you sit down.

This is the piece I would put in front of that wall. It is a wide, edge-to-edge tangle of tall blade-like stalks in cobalt blue, royal blue and emerald green. The color looks as though it comes from inside the stalks, not from one lamp in one corner.

First, the blue. It is deep and saturated, and it has weight. A dark, shadowy mass runs across the top and holds the whole image down, so the glow below it reads even brighter.

Then the stems. Pale, silvery lines sweep diagonally from lower left toward upper right, and one bright stem arcs across the middle. There is no single place to rest. Your eye follows the long strokes, then drops back into the tangle and finds new crossings.

Then the sparks. Small flickers of orange-red, rust and violet run through the cool blues and keep them from going still. Up close the surface looks brushed and feathery, with fine looping lines and a slightly metallic sheen.

It sits between a thicket you could almost walk into and pure color and line. You do not need to decide which. If it stops you, that is reason enough.

The composition is horizontal and runs edge to edge, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The dark band across the top gives it weight, and the deep blues have the presence to hold a room.

The warm accents pick up well against neutral or earthy surroundings. The mood is lively, vivid and a little electric. It also holds up at a moderate size, where the glow and fine linework reward a closer look.
