Look twice: the flower turns into a face

Botanical & Flowers · horizontal

Bloom Masquerade is a mirrored flower abstract in coral, crimson and gold, with a bloom at the center that also reads as a mask.

The sofa is in. The paint is settled. And there is still one wide wall that looks unfinished every time you walk past it.

You don't need something quiet for that wall. You may need one confident focal point, with warm color and enough going on to hold your eye from across the room and again up close. This is the piece I'd put there.

First, the flower. Pale pink and cream petals, a red heart, and a cluster of golden-yellow stamens that seem to glow from within. The stamens are finely detailed and a little granular, and they are the sharpest thing in the picture.

Then your eye gets carried outward. Thick, translucent ribbons of coral and crimson curl away from the bloom in matching pairs, toward the corners. They look smooth, glossy and liquid, and where they overlap the colors layer like stained glass.

Then the symmetry changes what you see. The image is mirrored exactly along a center line, so the flower can start to look like a face, a mask or a winged figure, with the scrolls as plumes or wings. A dark, heart-like plum shape anchors the bottom. That reading is only one of them. You may see something else, and that is the point of a piece like this.

Bloom Masquerade is horizontal, so it suits the wide spots: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a dining buffet. The warm, saturated palette works best where the wall needs one bold statement rather than a cluster of small things.

The deep plum and olive tones pair well with dark or neutral rooms, where the coral and gold can glow. The mirrored layout sits easily in a centered arrangement.
