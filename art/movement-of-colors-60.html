One white spark where two purple curves meet

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Violet Crease is an abstract color study: glowing lilac and mauve forms against deep navy, drawn to a single bright point.

You know the wall. It's the wide one above the sofa or the bed, and it has looked a little bare since the furniture settled in. You've held up a few things in your mind, and nothing has been right yet.

Picture something there that is quiet but not empty. Soft lilac and mauve curves glow against deep navy, and your eye goes straight to one small white point where they meet.

The first thing is the color. A broad lilac-violet ribbon sweeps in from the left, and a pinkish-mauve lobe rises from the center. Behind them, deep navy fills the upper left and right, so dark it is almost black. That darkness is why the purples look lit from behind.

Then the eye follows the curves. They run from the left toward the center and from the upper right down to the bottom, and they all converge on a small crease just left of center. A thin pale wedge of mint and cream sits at the join, and just above it is the white spark. A thin coral-red line runs along the right curve, and pale rims trace the other edges.

Then you lean in. The surfaces are smooth and glassy with a satin sheen. Fine speckles are scattered across the ribbon and the lobe, and hairline creases run through the lower band. The bottom of the image softens into peach and tan, which warms all that cool purple.

I haven't attached a story to this piece, and there is nothing in it to decode. It is color, light and form, and I think that is reason enough to want it.

The format is horizontal, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The large dark navy fields and simple structure keep the image clear and uncluttered, which helps when you go bigger.

The palette is cool, lilac, mauve and violet over navy, with a little warmth from the peach-tan base. It can anchor a quiet, modern room without fighting what is already in it.
