A White Web of Lines Over Color Lit From Behind

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · square

Ember Window is a square geometric abstract: hundreds of angular cells of orange, gold, lime and blue, with a warm star at the center.

The sofa is right. The paint is right. The wall above it still looks like it's waiting for something.

This is the piece I'd put there. From across the room you see a warm orange and red star glowing at the center of a square. Walk closer and it turns into something more detailed.

From a distance, Ember Window reads as light. The center is the warmest and brightest part, orange and red, and the blues and greens gather toward the edges and corners. It looks lit from behind, the way a stained glass window does.

Come closer and the light turns out to be color held in cells. A dense web of thin white lines divides the square into hundreds of triangles and fan shapes. Many of the lines carry a faint warm orange outline, which is part of why the whole surface seems to glow.

Closer still, there are small nested triangles that seem to spin inward, and wedges that fan out from many points. Under the sharp white lines the color is soft and marbled. The pattern is precise and the color is loose, and each of them is more interesting for having the other beside it.

I can't tell you what it will mean to you. That part is yours. What I can say is what the piece does: it pulls the eye in to the center, lets it rest in the cooler edges, and sends it back out across the pattern again.

Ember Window is made for a plain or neutral wall that needs one strong focal point: over a sofa, a bed, a console, or at the end of a hallway. The warm center holds attention from across the room, and the fine linework gives a closer look something to do.

Its mood is energetic, warm and lively, a kind of kaleidoscopic motion. It suits a modern living room, a studio or an office that wants color and energy from a precise geometric pattern. And if you simply love how it looks, that is reason enough.
