Raspberries Glowing in a Bowl of Liquid-Looking Metal

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · vertical

Raspberry Whirl is a painterly digital still life: a small heap of pink-red berries in a mirror-bright bowl, rippled into spirals.

You know the wall. Maybe it's in the kitchen, the dining area or beside the bar. The paint is chosen and the furniture is where you want it, but that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished.

Now picture a round, dark bowl hanging there, with a small heap of raspberries glowing in the bottom of it. The metal around them has been bent into spirals, so it looks less like a bowl and more like something poured.

The raspberries come first. They are the brightest, most saturated thing in the image, a pink-red that stands out against the dark inside of the bowl. You know what you're looking at straight away: fruit in a bowl, seen from above.

Then the bowl takes over. Its rim glows copper-orange. Inside, the reflections run steel blue and purple on the right, with warm tan at the upper left. Behind it, beige at the top left gives way to deep blue-black.

Then, if you look closer, you notice the spirals. Small whorls repeat in the background curves, in the reflections across the bowl and on the soft, plump surfaces of the berries. The curves loop outward and bring your eye back to the center.

I don't need to tell you what it means. It is a simple fruit still life with a playful, slightly dreamlike twist, and what you feel in front of it is yours.

This is a vertical, high-contrast image with a centered round form, so it works as the focal piece on a single wall. The kitchen, a dining area, a bar or an entry all suit it. The dark palette of charcoal, plum and steel blue holds up from across a room, and the raspberry gives it one bold accent.

The mood is playful, vivid and a little whimsical. Liking it for how it looks is reason enough.
