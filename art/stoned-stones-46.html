Pebbles That Look Like They Lie Under Moving Water

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Liquid Stones is a painterly stone abstract in slate blue, burnt sienna and amber, where a plain bed of pebbles ripples and swirls like glossy, marbled paint.

You know the wall. It's the wide one above the sofa, or the bed, or the console in the hallway. The furniture is settled and the paint is chosen, and that stretch of wall still looks unfinished every time you walk past.

Picture a horizontal piece there, filled edge to edge with smooth, rounded stones in deep brown, slate blue and warm tan. Swirling ripples run across them as if the water over a streambed were moving. Then a small amber pebble catches your eye, and you start looking around the frame.

At first glance this is a bed of pebbles, packed tight, with no gaps except thin dark brown seams between them. Then the ripples take over. Concentric rings and spiral eddies pass across the stones and bend their outlines, so the pebbles look like they are seen through water that never stops moving.

The surface looks like thick, brushed oil paint. The colour inside each stone is marbled and glossy, and fine curling ridges run through the whole image. I like that a subject as ordinary as a pebble can turn liquid.

There is no horizon and no single place to rest. Your eye follows one eddy, lands on a burnt orange stone among the cooler blues, then drifts to a patch of teal or lavender you hadn't noticed. Each pass shows you something different.

The listing calls it Digital Art, a painterly stone abstract. The rest is for you to meet in front of the piece and make your own.

The format is horizontal and runs full-bleed with no focal point, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk, or along a hallway where you pass it and see it from different distances.

The palette is earthy brown and slate blue with warm accents, so it sits comfortably in warm neutral and earth-toned rooms. It is also a quiet piece to look at slowly. You might want it simply because it's beautiful, and that is reason enough.

Measure the wall, or the width of the sofa or bed beneath it, before you choose. The listing lets you pick a size, and the print is made to order in the size you choose. If it doesn't feel right when it arrives, the 30-day money-back guarantee applies.

Screens vary, so the blues and ambers may look a little different in your room, and paper, canvas and metal each show colour in their own way. The deep browns, slate blues and warm tan are the core of the piece, with amber and orange accents glinting among them. The money-back guarantee exists for exactly this worry.

I believe art is felt, not seen, and that your own response is the only test that counts. If this image stops you and you find yourself following the swirls, that is reason enough. There's no right answer to pass.

Imagine that wide wall finished: a field of rippled stones in blue, brown and amber, with a few bright pebbles catching your eye as you walk in. Guests may ask where you found it.
