Every Wing Is Packed With Navy Swirls. Look Closer.

Butterflies, Birds & Wildlife · horizontal

Filigree Wings is a digital illustration of one ornate golden butterfly, drawn in flowing navy line over glowing gold, green and blue.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and the space above it has looked unfinished for months. Everything you've found so far looks like everything else.

Picture something different there: a single butterfly seen from above, wings spread wide across a pale ground. Its wings glow golden orange, olive and lime green, and deep navy. At first glance it is bright and cheerful. Then you step closer.

I can't give you a made-up story about this butterfly, and I don't think it needs one. What it has is on the surface, waiting for you to find it. I'd rather you stand in front of it than read about it.

Start with the wings. Every one is filled with fine navy swirls, scrolls and leaf-like shapes laid over mottled, stained-glass-like color that moves from gold to green to blue. There is hardly a bare patch anywhere.

Then follow the curls. They spiral through the wing scrolls, and the antennae and tail tips end in tiny spirals of their own. Behind the butterfly, a pale web of cell-shaped outlines in soft white, pink and lavender repeats the same line language. The insect and its ground read as one drawing.

The left and right wings mirror each other, which gives the piece a balanced order. The density of pattern is what keeps you looking.

This is a horizontal, symmetrical piece with one centered focal point, so it suits a statement spot above a console, sofa or headboard, or over a desk or reading nook that needs a lively accent. The shape follows a wide wall.

The pale ground is evenly lit and looks backlit, which helps the colors stay bright in a light room. The mood is playful and ornate, and the appeal is visual: rich color and intricate pattern.

You don't need to be. I believe art is felt, not seen, and your own response is the only test that counts. If this butterfly stops you and you keep wanting to look at it, that is reason enough.

Imagine it centered over your sofa or bed: a butterfly with spread wings, navy swirls glowing over gold and green, and a pale lace-like web reaching out across the wall. Come closer and the curls keep going.
