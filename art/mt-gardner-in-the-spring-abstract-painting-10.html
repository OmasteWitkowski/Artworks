Real-looking peaks, drawn in heavy black line and cobalt blue

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Cobalt Summit is a digital mountain landscape in flat color steps, wobbly black outlines and a stepped blue border, made for the wall that needs one bold cool accent.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and the space above it still looks unfinished. Maybe you nearly bought a soft, realistic mountain print and then saw the same one in someone else's house.

This is the mountain picture I would hang instead. It looks like a mountain view you could stand in, but it is drawn in heavy black lines and flat blocks of blue. It does not look like a standard photograph.

The first thing you see is the summit. White snow sits in the center of the picture, bright against a sky that is cobalt at the top and lightens toward the horizon. The sky is not smooth. It is built from curved bands, like arcs stacked on each other.

Then the lines show up. Thick, slightly wobbly black outlines trace the rocks, the snow patches and the ridges. The color is reduced to flat steps, so the mountains look cut out and layered rather than blended. Small white and pink flecks are scattered over the slopes.

Look lower and the blue ridges step down into forested slopes and a pale, tan hillside in shadow. The ridges slope in from the lower left and lower right and make a V, and your eye follows it up to the peak. Then the sky arcs carry it up again.

The sky bands come back in the wide border, in stepped blue and black. The picture sits in a frame that seems to be made of the same sky. I'm not going to tell you what to feel in front of it. Stand there for a minute and you'll know.

This is a horizontal piece, so it suits the long, low spaces: above a sofa, a console or a desk, or along an entry wall. The strong blues and the clear central peak give the wall a focal point. It pairs well with neutral walls, dark wood and other cool-toned decor.

The mood is crisp, cool and open, with clear air and a sense of distance. It suits a living room, office, entry, den or cabin where you want a graphic landscape rather than a gentle one.
