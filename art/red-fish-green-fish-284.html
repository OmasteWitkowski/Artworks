Rust red and teal, side by side, and one bright seam

Abstract Glass Macro · horizontal

Ember and Tide is a glossy, luminous macro abstract: a rust-red diamond on the left, a teal drop in the centre, and tiny bubbles caught in the surface.

You know the wall. It's the one over the sofa, or the bed, or the sideboard, and it has looked a little bare since the room was finished. Everything else is settled. The wall is not.

This is the piece I would hold up to it. The left half is burnt orange-red. The right half is teal and aqua. Where they meet there is a bright, narrow seam, and the whole image glows as if the light is coming from behind it.

The first thing I look at is the contrast. The rust-red diamond and the teal drop sit side by side, and neither one gives way. Each keeps its own temperature. Soft pink, peach and pale yellow-green sit between them, and the tones shift smoothly from warm to cool.

Then the teal form pulls you in. It hangs down the centre and ends in a rounded point, with a pale blue curve running along the right edge. Come closer and the surface has more to show: single drops, small clusters of bubbles, thin bright reflections curving along the lower left and right, and faint fine ripples low on the left.

The edges are cropped tight, so there is no horizon and no object to name. The listing calls it a macro photograph, and beyond that I'm not going to tell you what to see in it. What you see is yours to decide.

I don't think a piece has to be explained to be enjoyed. If the colour stops you, that is reason enough.

This is a wide horizontal frame, so it suits a long, specific wall: over a sofa, a bed, a sideboard or a console, or at the end of a hallway. The bold colour blocks and soft gradients are simple enough to read from across the room.

The two halves also give you something to echo. The warm side can pick up a rust or terracotta cushion, throw or rug. The cool side can pick up teal, blue or green nearby. If your room has an accent on only one side, the other half adds the contrast.

Measure the wall, or the width of the sofa or bed beneath it, before you open the listing. The frame is wide and horizontal, so width is the number that matters most. The listing shows the sizes and formats on offer. I'm not stating a largest size here, so check what the listing offers for the wall you have.
