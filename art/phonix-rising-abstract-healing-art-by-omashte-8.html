Phoenix, firebird or peacock? One orange bird, your call

Butterflies, Birds & Wildlife · vertical

Ember Wings is a symmetrical, flame-orange bird with a peacock-eye chest, set in cream swirls and framed in deep navy.

You know the wall. It might be the narrow stretch in the entryway, the space above a console, or the blank spot behind a desk. The furniture is settled and the paint is done, and that wall still looks unfinished every time you walk past.

This is the piece I'd hold up to it. A bright orange bird stands head-on in a tall frame, wings raised high and tail pointing straight down. Against the pale yellow ground it glows like a flame, and you can read it from across the room.

From a distance, it's a silhouette: one bold orange shape, perfectly mirrored left to right, with a solid navy border holding it in place. It looks like an emblem.

Step closer and the chest opens up. Rows of small teardrop feather eyes, like peacock feathers, cover it, with a small heart-shaped outline near the center. The orange has a faint mottling in it, so the figure isn't quite as flat as it first looks.

Closer still, the background turns out to be a field of fine cream-white lines, swirls and concentric ripples that spiral out on both sides. They look like marbling or zebra stripes, and they mirror the bird's own symmetry.

I call it a phoenix, but you could just as easily see a firebird or a stylized peacock. The raised wings and fiery color feel upward and celebratory to me. What it means to you is yours to decide.

The vertical format and strict symmetry suit a centered spot: an entryway, a narrow wall, above a console, a bed or a desk. The bird fills the frame from top to bottom, so it reads as a statement piece, not a small accent.

The orange gives a neutral or cool-toned room a strong warm accent, and the navy border ties in easily with blues. If you want a warm, celebratory, emblem-like bird rather than a realistic wildlife portrait or a generic abstract, this is that.
