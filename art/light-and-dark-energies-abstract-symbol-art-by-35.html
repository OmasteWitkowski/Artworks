A Yin Yang That Glows Like a Jewel Set in the Dark

Spiritual, Mandala & Symbolic · vertical

Indigo Portal is a tall digital abstract: a pale, milky yin-yang beside a glassy, swirling half of plum, indigo, crimson and peach.

Picture the narrow wall beside a doorway, or the stretch above a desk, or the corner of a room where you sit and read. The furniture is settled and the paint is chosen. That one wall still looks unfinished, and a busy picture would be too much for it.

This is the piece I would put there. It is a single, centered image on a dark ground, with one bright point of focus that pulls your eye in. It is a yin yang, but not the flat black-and-white one you know.

From across the room, you see a pale shape and a deep, colored shape held together inside a tall oval. The white half is soft and milky, almost glowing, with faint cloudy variations in it. The other half looks glassy and backlit. Deep plum and navy open into warm peach and cream.

Step closer and the details appear. The white half holds one small navy dot, and the colored half holds one small white dot, stacked along the center line. The swirls inside the colored half are smooth and smoky, like liquid or glass, in purple, deep blue, crimson and cream.

Around the symbol runs a ring of marbled color in violet, peach, teal and gold. It is mirrored left to right and top to bottom, so it repeats from the center outward like a slow pulse. A thin dark outline separates it from the symbol. Beyond that is the dark, finely flecked ground, which looks a little like woven fabric.

The yin yang carries its traditional meaning of light and dark held together. You do not need that meaning to want this piece. The color and the glow are reason enough, and your own response is the only test I trust.

The vertical format and centered oval suit a wall that wants one glowing, symmetrical image. Think above a desk, beside a doorway, or in a reading or meditation corner. The dark ground sits well in a bedroom or living room that needs one deep, jewel-toned accent.

It would also pair with other dark-ground abstracts in a small group. The oval and the symmetry give the piece a quiet, steady presence rather than a busy one.
