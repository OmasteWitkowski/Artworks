A White-Hot Flame Wrapped in Swirls of Amber and Gold

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · vertical

Ember Heart is a tall, painterly flame on a deep brown-black field, available as a print on Fine Art America.

You know the wall. It might be the narrow one beside the fireplace, or the stairwell you pass twenty times a day. The furniture is settled and the paint is done, and that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished.

This is the piece I'd hold up to it. A single flame rises almost the full height of the frame. The core is nearly white, and golden-orange swirls wrap around it before fading into rust, burnt umber and near-black.

I can't tell you where this flame was lit, because that isn't something I'm going to make up. What I can tell you is what's there, and it rewards a longer look.

Start at the brightest point, just right of center. The white-hot core moves outward through creamy yellow and amber into shadow. The surface looks like thick, swirled paint, with fine curling grooves across the bright areas and small spirals rolling through the flame.

Then look at the dark. Soft round shapes drift through the brown-black, and a small spiral near the top center catches the eye before it follows the flame up to its curled tip at the upper right.

The piece is listed as digital art. It is a stylized flame with a painterly texture, not a literal record of logs in a fire. What it means is up to you.

Ember Heart is vertical, with high contrast and one dominant form, so it works as a single statement. Think of a narrow wall, the space beside a fireplace, a stairwell, or the wall above a reading chair.

The dark ground suits moody, warm-toned rooms: a living room, den, study or reading corner where deep browns and warm light are already part of the feel.

Measure the wall first, then choose from the sizes shown on the listing. Because the piece is vertical, think about the height you have as well as the width. Fine Art America also offers a 30-day money-back guarantee.

Screens vary, so I can't promise an exact match. The piece is built on glowing amber and orange against deep brown-black, and you can expect that warm, high-contrast look. If it isn't right in your room, the 30-day guarantee applies.

Your own response is the only test that counts. If the glow stopped you, that is enough. There is no good or bad art, and you don't need a reason beyond liking it.

Picture the narrow wall again, with a tall flame glowing against the dark, white-hot at the center and curling toward the corner. It would be the first thing you see when you walk in.
