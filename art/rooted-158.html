Who Is the Gold and Violet Face Looking Back at You?

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Gilded Totem is a horizontal digital abstract, mirrored left to right, with a mask-like face drawn in purple lines on glowing yellow.

You know the wall. It's the long one above the sofa, or the bed, or the console, and it has looked bare for months. You have probably nearly bought something generic just to fill it.

Picture something else there: a field of gold, violet and indigo ripples, and in the middle a rounded, mask-like face that seems to look back at you. That is Gilded Totem.

From across the room, you see color first. Golden yellow glows against deep violet and indigo, with bands of burnt orange, magenta and olive running between them. The pattern fills the frame edge to edge. There is no horizon and no empty corner.

Come closer and the center takes over. A rounded, shield-shaped outline holds dark violet curves that read as eyes and a mouth, with a pointed yellow shape above. It is drawn entirely in thin purple contour lines on glowing yellow. Below it sits a second mirrored shape, like a vase or a torso.

Then the details start to show. Fine dark lines, often doubled, trace every fold. Small white-blue glints sit on the curves, so the surface looks like liquid glass or molten metal. Translucent layers overlap, and the ripples echo across both halves.

I'm not going to tell you what the face is. Some people see a mask, some a guardian, some a totem. Whatever you see is the right answer.

Gilded Totem is wide and horizontal, so it suits a long wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The dense pattern and the gold-and-violet contrast let it work as the main color anchor of a room.

It does best in a space that can carry bold pattern. If your room is quiet and neutral, it will be the one thing everyone looks at. If you already love color, it will keep company with it.
