The Amber Horn Rising Out of a Field of Glowing Glass

Abstract Glass Macro · horizontal

Amber Horn is a wide, glowing abstract in amber, olive and cream, made for the long wall that still looks bare.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that long stretch above it still looks unfinished. You have probably been walking past it for months, picturing something you can't quite name.

Picture this instead: deep olive and maroon on the left, opening into cream, yellow and orange on the right. Between them a curling amber horn rises, and the whole image glows as if light were passing through it from behind.

I'll tell you what is in the frame, because that is the honest place to start. A dark maroon dome sits at upper left. A large olive and amber mass sweeps across the lower left. A pale cream cell sits near the center, and a pointed amber-brown form curls up just to its right.

A thin teal-blue line wanders through the upper right, a little like the leading in stained glass. Your eye travels from the heavy lower left, up along the curves, toward the bright open space on the right.

Then it gets quieter. Tiny bubbles and specks float in the pale areas, and bright rims trace the edges of the shapes. You don't see them from across the room, but they reward a closer look.

It looks like glass to me. I'll leave what the shapes are, and what they mean, to you. Everyone sees something different here, and your own reading is the only one that counts.

This is a horizontal piece with no horizon, filled edge to edge, so it reads as a wide statement over a sofa, a bed, a console or a long dining wall. The dark weight at left and the bright space at right let it anchor a wall off-center without feeling lopsided.

The amber, olive and cream sit easily with wood, leather, linen and other earthy tones. The cream and orange at right keep the heavier left side from feeling dark.

That is a fair worry. Measure the wall first, and mark the space with tape or paper before you choose a size. Screens also vary, so the glow you see may look a little different in person. Fine Art America's 30-day money-back guarantee is there for exactly this.

You don't need to know anything. Look at it and notice whether you keep looking. That response is the only test I trust. There is no right or wrong reading of these shapes.

It looks like a close-up of glossy, translucent glass lit from behind, and that is what I'd invite you to see. Beyond that, I'd rather leave the shapes open to your own reading.

Picture that long wall with the olive and maroon settling in at one end and the cream and orange opening up at the other, and the amber horn right where your eye lands. Then picture a guest asking where you found it.
