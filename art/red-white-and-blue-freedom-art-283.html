A Hot Orange Glow That Looks Lit From Behind

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Core is a vivid abstract in which a glowing orange-yellow center sits in deep cobalt blue beside a glossy red oval.

You know the wall. It is wide and bare, over the sofa or the bed or the console, and the room around it is already settled. Everything else is done, and the wall is the part that still looks unfinished.

Picture a single hot point of light on it. A bright yellow-orange glow, just left of center, with a band of orange-red running down toward it from the upper left. All of it sits in deep cobalt blue. That is Ember Core.

What holds me is the light. It seems to come from inside the shapes, the way a window glows when the sun is behind the colored glass. The center is hot and bright, and the blues around it are darker and cooler.

The diagonal band pulls your eye down into that glow. Then the glossy red oval on the right and a pale curved shape at the upper right answer it, so the eye does not stop in one spot. The blurred blue areas give the whole piece a slow, drifting feel.

Look closer and the smooth surfaces open up. There are fine hairline cracks, tiny speckles that look like bubbles or dust, and a few thin curved lines, mostly in the lower yellow-green form and the pale shape at upper right.

I would let your own eye decide what it is. Some people will see glass, some will see fire or water, and some will just enjoy the color. All of those are fair responses.

Ember Core is a horizontal piece, so it suits a wide wall: over a sofa, a bed, a console or a dining wall. It works best in a room that can hold a bold statement. The deep blues give the oranges and reds a dark ground to sit against, and the single glowing center keeps the color from feeling scattered.

It is vivid and energetic, not quiet. If you want a soft, restful picture, this is not it. If you want a wall with some fire in it, it might be.
