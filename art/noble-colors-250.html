A violet pool ringed in glowing seams: look closer

Abstract Glass Macro · horizontal

Violet Chamber is a wide abstract of glossy, backlit-looking colour, with cobalt blue on one side, red and orange on the other, and a bubble-flecked violet pool in the middle.

You have a wall that has bothered you for a while. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that wide stretch of wall still looks unfinished. Everything you've found for it looks like everything else.

This is what I would put there. Deep cobalt blue fills the left side of the frame. A red form sweeps into orange on the right. In the middle sits a pool of violet, outlined by a thin, bright, wavy edge, and the whole thing looks lit from behind.

Your eye lands in the middle first. The violet and purple pool is flecked with tiny bubble-like specks and held by a rounded pink shape at the bottom. Pink, yellow and magenta shapes press in around it, and they crop off at the edges, so the image feels larger than the frame.

Then it starts to wander. The seams between the colour fields are thin and glowing, and they read like glass or liquid surfaces. In the violet centre there are fine scratch-like marks as well as the specks. This is where the piece slows down and rewards a longer look.

The movement is gentle. The shapes seem to drift against each other rather than collide, so the colour is vivid and a little playful, but not restless.

What it makes you think of is yours to decide. Art is felt, not seen.

This is a horizontal piece, made for a wide, settled wall: over a sofa, over a bed, or above a long console. The blue and red masses at the edges give it presence from a distance, and the violet chamber in the middle rewards standing close.

It does well in a room with neutral walls or furniture, where the colour has space to glow and becomes the one confident colour in the room.
