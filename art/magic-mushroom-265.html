The glowing peach shape that holds a wide, bare wall

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Peach Ember is a glossy red and blue abstract: a luminous peach form in nested panels, with two small spirals, pearly beads and dark drips to find.

Maybe the sofa is new and the wall above it now looks bare. Or the wall has bothered you for months, and everything you have found looks like everything else.

Here is a piece that is not a pattern. A large peach and orange form sits low in the frame and seems to glow from within. Deep red surrounds it, and a cool blue-violet panel sits to the left.

The peach form is the part people stop on. To me it looks like a soft creature or a smooth stone, and it is neither for certain. I have left it open on purpose.

Red is warm and heavy. Blue is cool and quiet. Here they sit right against each other, and that contrast carries the whole picture.

Then the small things start to appear. There are two tiny spirals turning in the blue panel and pale pearly circles along the edge of the peach shape. A chain of beaded knobs steps down the right side. Dark drips hang from the top left corner, and I can read them as roots or as antlers.

What the shapes are is up to you. Art is felt before it is explained, and your own response is the only test that counts.

The format is horizontal, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a bed, a sideboard or a console, or at the end of a hallway.

The red is strong enough to be the main colour in a room of neutral or dark furnishings. The blue gives you a cooler note to pick up in cushions, glass or a rug.
