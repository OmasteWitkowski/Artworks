Turquoise Whirlpools That Look Lit From Behind

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Electric Tides is a wide, horizontal digital abstract: glowing turquoise spirals above a grainy blue band and streams of molten red, orange and green.

You have a wide wall and a room where everything else is settled. The sofa is in, the paint is done, and the wall still looks bare every time you walk past it. A small, quiet piece won't fix that. It would look lost.

This is the piece I picture there. Turquoise whirlpools, outlined in dark lines, sit across the top and again at the lower right. Below them, red, orange, yellow and green ripple toward the center. It's a lot of color on purpose.

I can't give you a tidy backstory for this picture, and I won't make one up. What I can tell you is what happens when I look at it for a while.

It's the way two surfaces meet. A speckled, grainy band runs across the middle like a horizon, almost pebbled. Everything around it is smooth and glossy, like liquid. The bright colors sit against dark outlines, so the spirals look lit from behind, even though there is no single light source in the picture.

Then the movement takes over. The spirals seem to spin in place while the lower half is pulled sideways, and the upper right folds into jagged, flame-like bends. Your eye travels from one whirlpool to the next along the curving lines.

Some people will think of marbling or water, and some will see something else entirely. That part is yours.

This is a horizontal piece, so it suits a wide wall: over a sofa, a bed or a sideboard, or at the end of a hallway. The color is saturated and the contrast is strong. In a neutral or dark-toned room, it becomes the first place the eye goes.

The mood is energetic and a little dreamlike. It's lively rather than quiet, so it works best where you want the wall to do some talking.
