Every Swirl Is a Tiny Maze. Look Closer.

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Labyrinth Unfurling is a symmetrical digital abstract in cobalt, chartreuse, orange and red, with a mirrored teal-and-blue oval at its heart.

You bought the sofa. The paint is settled. And the wall above it still looks bare, a little too quiet for the room you've built.

What that wall may need is one strong spot of colour, not a pile of small frames. That is why I want you to see this piece.

From across the room, you see one shape. Curling, paisley-shaped lobes fan out from a central egg-shaped oval, and two ribbon-like tails reach along the bottom corners. The whole form is outlined in white and floats on a plain white ground.

Step closer and each lobe opens up. Thin white lines run in concentric spirals, like a maze or a fingerprint, laid over soft, blended patches of cobalt blue, chartreuse, orange and red. In the centre, the oval holds a mirrored pattern in teal and blue.

The lines pull your eye inward and around, then back out along the curls, so the form seems to pulse or unfurl. I think of it as flower-like or mask-like, but that is only a comparison. You may see something else entirely, and that is part of the fun.

This is a horizontal piece, so it suits the wide spots: over a sofa, a bed, a console or a sideboard. The white ground sits quietly in a light, neutral room, and the colour carries the space.

The mood is playful, bright and a little whimsical, with a folk-art, psychedelic feel. It isn't a quiet piece, and it doesn't need to explain itself.

Screens vary, so the cobalt, chartreuse, orange and red may look a little different from one device to the next. The white ground and the strong colour contrast are what to look for. If the print isn't what you expected, the 30-day money-back guarantee applies.

I believe your own response is the only test that counts. If this image stopped you, that is a good reason to want it. You don't need to know anything about art, and you don't need to see what I see in it.

Picture the wall again, with the cobalt, orange and chartreuse above the sofa and the white ground keeping everything else calm. Then picture a guest walking in, stopping, and stepping closer to follow the lines.
