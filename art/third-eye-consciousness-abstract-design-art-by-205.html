Cobalt blue with a grain like soft static

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Cobalt Hush is a wide, edge-to-edge field of deep blue, and its fine vertical grain is what keeps it from going flat.

You know the wall. It might be over the sofa, the bed or the console. The furniture is settled and the paint is done, but that one wide stretch still looks unfinished.

What it may need isn't a scene. It may need one strong colour with enough surface to hold your eye. That is what I see in Cobalt Hush: a field of deep cobalt and royal blue from edge to edge, with no object, no horizon and no focal point.

From across the room, Cobalt Hush reads as a single saturated blue. Step closer and it breaks into fine vertical flecks and streaks, some lighter, some darker, some leaning greener toward teal. A few touches lean violet.

The grain looks soft and fibrous, a little like felt, suede or fine static. The short vertical strokes give the surface a faint downward shimmer, like light rain, but nothing pulls you toward one spot. The tone shifts only in small flickers, so the field changes a little as you move from close to far.

The light is even and flat, with no shadow and no gradient. The colour and the surface are all there is. I think that's enough, and your own response is the only test of whether it is for you.

Cobalt Hush is built for a broad wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. Because the texture is uniform and has no focal point, it gives you a strong blue presence without telling the room where to look.

It pairs easily with neutral rooms, where the blue becomes the one deliberate colour. It also sits well beside other quiet, textured work. Its even surface means it can hang alone or as part of a set of several.
