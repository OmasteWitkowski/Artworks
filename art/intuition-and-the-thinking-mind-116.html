A Bright Point Where Blue, Green and Violet Meet

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

"Where to Look Next" is a horizontal digital abstract of glowing colour bands, with a Jonas Salk line on intuition printed beneath it.

You know the wall. It's over the sofa, the console or the desk, and the room around it is finished. The paint is chosen and the furniture is where you want it, but that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished.

A wide, horizontal piece with colour that glows could be what it's waiting for. This is the one I'd ask you to look at: royal blue, violet and forest green sweeping toward a single bright point.

The first thing you see is colour. Royal blue sits at the left, and a forest green band runs in from the upper left toward the middle. Violet fills the right and the lower centre, all over a deep navy base. Touches of magenta and yellow-green warm up an otherwise cool palette.

Then the light. Thin white and pale lavender filaments gather just right of centre, where the green, blue and violet meet, and then spread out to the right in curving lines. The white seems to come from behind the colour, so the bands look almost translucent. Faint smoky wisps drift over the top, and a fine, canvas-like texture covers the whole surface.

The last thing you find is the strip along the bottom. Two centred lines of light serif type read: "Intuition will tell the thinking mind where to look next," attributed to Jonas Salk. The whole image moves toward one bright point, and the line asks you to slow down and look at it.

I'm not going to tell you what it means. The colour and the line are both there, and what you feel in front of it is yours.

The horizontal format is made for the long, low wall above a sofa, console or bed. It also suits a desk in a study or studio, where you can step close enough to read the quote.

The palette is cool and deep, and the glow holds attention from across a room. It reads as a luminous focal point rather than a loud statement.
