The blue diamond hiding in a mirrored stand of bare trees

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · vertical

Blue Diamond Seam is a tall, mirror-symmetrical digital artwork of bare branches around a glowing blue center, and you can order it as a print on Fine Art America.

You know the wall. It might be the stretch beside the front door, the hallway, or the stairwell. The room around it is finished, the paint and furniture are settled, and that one narrow space still looks like nobody decided anything.

This is the piece I would hold up to it. It is tall, dark and calm at the edges, with one saturated blue diamond glowing in the middle. Your eye goes straight to it.

First you see trees. Bare trunks and branches against the sky, still recognisable as trees.

Then you notice the thin dark line down the center and realise the left side and the right side match. The limbs fork and cross into X-shapes, and in the middle they frame a bright blue diamond. Smaller blue diamonds repeat above and below.

Come closer and the blue starts to move. A swirled, liquid-looking pattern runs through it and gives the symmetry a gentle turning motion. The twigs are fine and dense, dark brown and purple over pale lavender and muted olive.

It is a digital piece, a mirrored photographic composite. The pattern is exact, but the trees stay trees. That is the look-closer moment I care about: you start with a pattern and then see what it is made of.

The portrait format suits a hallway, entry, stairwell, the space above a console, or the narrow wall beside a doorway. These are places where a wide landscape piece would not fit.

The deep blue and aubergine tones suit a room that wants one bold, graphic focal point. The mood is striking and a little otherworldly. It comes from pattern and color contrast, and I make no larger claim for it.
