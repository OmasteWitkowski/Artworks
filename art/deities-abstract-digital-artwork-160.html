Two Glowing Eyes Face Each Other Across One Seam

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Facing Embers is a mirrored digital abstract in burnt orange, deep red and dark maroon, where two flowing, mask-like shapes meet inside one large red-orange circle.

You know the wall. It's the long one over the sofa, or the bed, or the console, and the room is finished except for that space. You've held up a few ideas and none of them felt like yours.

I made Facing Embers for a wall like that. From across the room you see a big red-orange circle, a dark seam down the middle and two bright orange ovals. Then the shapes around the ovals start to look like two masks, or two creatures, facing each other.

I'll tell you what I see in it. The image is mirrored exactly across a narrow vertical strip. On each side, marbled shapes curve in toward the center and loop around a glowing orange oval. The ovals read as eyes, and the swirling forms around them read as a muzzle or a mask.

The light looks like embers. Bands of bright orange and golden yellow seem to glow against dark maroon and near-black brown, a bit like a stained-glass window with a lamp behind it. Small touches of teal and steel blue cool it down, and pale cream highlights sit where the light is strongest.

Then there's the surface. It's smooth and glossy, and fine contour lines ripple through the shapes like wood grain or a topographic map. The faces are an impression, not a stated subject. I won't tell you what they are. What you find in them is yours.

This is a horizontal composition, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a console or a bed, or behind a bar. A den, office or lounge works too. The strong center seam and the contrast give it presence in a room, and the dark maroon border frames it.

The palette is deep and warm, so it fits best where rich colors are welcome. Think moody, warm-toned rooms, or a room that needs one bold piece to anchor it. If your space is pale and quiet, this will be the loudest thing in it.

You don't need to know anything about art. I believe there's no good or bad art, and your own response is the only test that counts. If you look at the two glowing ovals and want to keep looking, that's reason enough. If you see something different in the shapes than I do, that's fine too.
