Five Glowing Notes That Turn From Gold to Lime Green

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Molten Melody is a horizontal digital art piece: five translucent music notes standing in a row over dark, liquid marbling.

You know the wall. It's the long one above the console, the desk or the shelf in the room where the music happens. Everything else in the room is settled, and that stretch of wall still looks unfinished.

This is the piece I'd want you to picture there. Five music notes stand in a row, glowing against a dark, swirling field. The first is gold and the last is lime green.

I don't have a backstory to hand you for this piece, and I won't make one up. What I can tell you is what happens when you stand in front of it. You read it as music right away: five flagged notes, evenly spaced, each with an oval head at the bottom and a thin stem rising up.

Then you look closer. The notes are translucent, so the marbled background shows through every stem, flag and head. There are ripples and ring-like eddies in deep reddish brown, navy and near-black, and they seem to keep moving behind the glass.

Follow the row from left to right. The note heads go from gold to blue, then teal, then bright green and lime. The light is warmest on the left and cooler and greener on the right. Some people will hear a melody travelling along. Others will just enjoy a rich, dark picture of glowing shapes. Either response is a good one.

The format is horizontal, so it suits a long wall: above a console, a desk or a shelf, in a music room, studio, den or listening corner. The dark ground and built-in frames give it a gallery-like presence. The notes stay clear from across the room.

The palette is deep reddish brown, navy and near-black, lit by gold, teal and green. It sits comfortably with warm wood and deep jewel tones. The mood is lively and rhythmic, like sound turned into color.
