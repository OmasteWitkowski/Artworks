Wings that look like glowing, marbled liquid

Butterflies, Birds & Wildlife · horizontal

Ember Wings is a mirrored red and gold butterfly, its wings filled with glossy swirls and lit from within against deep maroon.

A new sofa can make the wall above it look bare. Or the old picture suddenly looks small in the room. You want something with warmth and weight, and you don't want the butterfly print you've seen in everyone else's house.

Picture a large butterfly with its wings spread across the middle of a deep red and maroon ground. You get the butterfly in the first second. Then the wings start to look like something else: glowing, marbled liquid in gold, orange and lavender.

The butterfly is easy to read. It has a red body, a cream head and two thin antennae curving above it. The wings fill most of the frame, so there is nothing to puzzle over. It is a butterfly, and it is large.

Then look at the wing surfaces. Glossy swirls of gold, burnt orange, blush pink and lavender overlap like translucent layers, with small touches of lime green and pale aqua. The surfaces are smooth and fluid, with no visible brushwork. It looks like marbling caught mid-flow.

The whole image is mirrored exactly across the center. The swirls ripple outward from the body in a pulsing, kaleidoscope-like rhythm, and faint ornamental curls repeat in the background around the wings. It reads closer to stained glass or a kaleidoscope than to a field study of a butterfly.

A dark maroon border and a thin glowing orange inner edge frame it like a mat. That, and the warm light at the center, gives it the look of something kept in a jewel box. I'd rather you stand in front of it than read about it. Whatever you feel is the only test that matters.

The format is horizontal and the butterfly is centered, so it works as the focal piece over a sofa, a bed, a console or a dining buffet. The saturated reds and golds act as a warm accent in a room that already has its furniture and paint settled.

It suits a room where you want color to lead. The deep maroon surround is dark and rich, so it can anchor a wall instead of fading into it.
