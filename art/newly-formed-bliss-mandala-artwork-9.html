Two Tilted Squares, One Glowing Orange Core

Spiritual, Mandala & Symbolic · square

Ember Window is a square kaleidoscope mandala in electric blue, orange and magenta, set in a thick black frame.

You have a wall that has bothered you for a while. The sofa is in, the paint is done, and the wall still looks unfinished. Maybe the old piece looks small now, or the room is calm enough to take one strong color.

This is the piece I would put in front of that wall. Two tilted squares sit inside a thick black frame. At the very center, a small upright square holds a core of glowing orange and red.

Start at the middle. The orange-and-red core sits against cool blues, so it looks lit from behind, like a window with a lamp on inside. There is no single light source and no cast shadow. The color itself does the glowing.

Then follow the swirls outward. Marbled, flame-like ribbons of electric blue, magenta, violet and orange curl and mirror left to right and top to bottom. They repeat in each corner and inside the diamonds. Because the two squares are rotated, the whole design seems to turn slowly like a pinwheel, even though the symmetry is strict.

The black does a lot of the work. The thick frame and the corner blocks make every color look brighter and more gem-like. Some people see stained glass here, and others see a quilt block. I think both are fair, and what you see is yours to decide.

It is a digital piece, and the mirrored symmetry is part of its character. Each corner and diamond gives you a new turn of pattern the longer you look.

This piece suits a wall that can take strong color. The black frame lets it stand alone as a single statement over a sofa, a bed or a stairwell. It can also be the vivid anchor next to quieter neutrals, or the center of a mixed arrangement.

The square shape sits comfortably on a wall where a wide landscape would feel too horizontal. The mood is energetic and bold, with the order of symmetry underneath.
