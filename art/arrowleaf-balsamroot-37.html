Yellow Blooms That Glow Against Liquid-Marble Bark

Botanical & Flowers · horizontal

Ember Blooms is a painterly wildflower image: golden-orange petals that look lit from within, set against dark, swirling bark.

You have the sofa, the paint and the rug, and one wall still looks bare. Everything else in the room is settled, and the wall hasn't been. If the room leans neutral or dark, it may be waiting for one thing that carries all the warmth by itself.

Picture a cluster of bright yellow-orange flowers with orange-red centers, opening toward you. Behind them is a dark, rough tree trunk, and the petals seem to glow against it. That is Ember Blooms.

The glow comes first. The petals are saturated golden yellow and orange, and the dark trunk and shaded undergrowth around them make them look almost lit from within. There are no harsh shadows to interrupt it, only color against dark.

Then the bark. It isn't rendered as plain bark. It swirls in marbled patterns that look nearly liquid, and once you notice that, you start to see the swirls echo the way the petals radiate and curl outward. The long stems sway between them, and the upright trunk holds it all steady.

This is a heightened, painterly version of a wildflower scene, not a literal record of one. The color is turned up and the leaves have crisp, slightly glossy edges. I would rather you stand in front of it than read about it, so I'll leave the rest to you.

Ember Blooms is a horizontal piece with strong warm color, so it suits a wide wall: over a sofa, along an entryway, in a kitchen or a sunroom. It works best where the surroundings are neutral or dark, because the yellows and oranges can then carry the room.

The mood is cheerful, warm and energetic, like wildflowers in full bloom. It is bright to look at, and that is reason enough to want it.
