A Butterfly That Looks Lit From Behind, Like Stained Glass

Butterflies, Birds & Wildlife · horizontal

Ember Wings is a single symmetrical butterfly whose wings are filled with glowing orange and red spirals, teardrops and tiny cells, on a calm cream ground.

The sofa is in. The paint is settled. And the wide wall above it still looks like it is waiting for something. You know it needs one warm piece of colour, but not a busy one, and not the same print you have seen in every other home.

This is the one I would put there. From across the room it is a single butterfly, perfectly balanced, in burnt orange and deep red on soft cream. Walk closer and the wings open into something else entirely.

Stand back and you see a butterfly: wings spread wide, antennae lifted, a slim body running down the middle. The shape is clean and easy to read, and the left and right sides mirror each other, so the whole image feels steady.

Then the wings start to unfold. Dark red-brown lines curl into rose-like spirals, teardrops and clusters of small bubble-like cells. Each cell has its own smooth gradient in burnt orange, deep red or golden yellow, with a few flecks of olive green and blue-violet.

What holds me is the glow. The colour inside the lines has a sheen, so the wings look lit from behind, the way a stained-glass window does. The swirls keep moving even though the shape is so still.

Whether you find roses, flames or something else in there is up to you. I would rather you stand in front of it and decide than have me tell you.

The wide, horizontal shape suits a wall that is longer than it is tall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a dining sideboard. The strong left-right symmetry looks settled and balanced over a centred piece of furniture.

The palette is warm: orange, red and cream, with small touches of green and blue-violet. It should sit comfortably against neutral walls, greens and warm woods, and be the one colourful note in the room.
