The One Orange Note on a Quiet Green Dock

Travel / Specialty / Projects · horizontal

"Off Duty Quiet" is a horizontal, painterly picture of a lone figure in orange at the end of a floating dock, under a sign that says no one is watching.

You know the wall. It's over the sofa, or the bed, or the desk. The furniture is settled and the paint is chosen, but the wall still looks unfinished, and you've been looking at it for months.

Now picture a wide field of moss and olive green with a single bright orange point near the top center. A person sits at the far end of a dock with their back to you, looking out at the water. That is "Off Duty Quiet."

The orange comes first. It is the brightest, most saturated color in the picture, and everything else is muted: moss, olive, dark green, pale gray-white, soft cream. Your eye lands on the figure before you decide to look.

Then the dock pulls you in. It runs on a diagonal from the lower left up toward the seated figure, and a small pale boat is tied along its side. Open green water fills the right side and the background, so the figure has a lot of room around them.

Then you read the sign. In the lower left foreground, a brown wooden sign says NO LIFEGUARD ON DUTY. Below it is rippled water, and out at the end of the dock sits someone who looks entirely at ease. I like how dry that is. The scene is very still, and the sign gives it a slight smile.

The light is soft and diffused, with no hard shadows. The finish is smooth, painterly and slightly glossy, like oil paint, and a dark border frames the whole thing. Look closer and you'll find fine horizontal ripples in the water and a speckled surface on the dock.

This is a horizontal piece, so it suits the wide spaces: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The green field is calm and the orange gives it one warm focus, so it can be the main point of a room without crowding it.
