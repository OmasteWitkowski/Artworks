Look Closer: The Blue Rim Is Cut Into Tiny Frills

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Cobalt Crown is a horizontal blue abstract: a glowing cobalt mass with a serrated, frilled edge curling around a pale, crumpled-looking center.

You have the sofa. The paint is settled. And the wall above it is still bare, or still holding something you stopped noticing a long time ago.

This is the piece I'd put in front of that wall. A big form of saturated cobalt blue fills the left and center of a horizontal frame, and the first thing it does is stop you.

From across a room, you see the blue. It is deep and bright, and it glows as if there were light behind it.

Step closer and the edge changes. The rim of the blue form isn't smooth. It breaks into fine frills and sharp serrated points, and the color stays crisp where you might expect it to go soft.

Inside the blue sits a paler, crumpled-looking center in light blues and white. Some people see a glowing eye there. Others see a blue flower from above. It is purely an abstract, so you can see what you like in it, or nothing at all.

Then your eye starts to travel. It goes from the blue rim to the bright center and back out along the feathery ripples. A curved band of cream, pale yellow and olive green wraps the right side and bottom and keeps the blue from feeling cold.

It is a horizontal, color-led piece, so it suits the wide wall: over a sofa, a console or a bed, or at the end of a hallway. The corners fall into darker, quieter tones, which holds the eye in the middle. Give it some room to breathe and it will carry the space.

The cool blue pairs well with neutral walls and warm woods, and the cream and yellow keep it friendly. It suits a living room, studio, office or entry wall where you want one confident piece.
