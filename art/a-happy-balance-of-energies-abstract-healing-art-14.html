The Yin-Yang That Turns Out to Be a Window

Spiritual, Mandala & Symbolic · vertical

A solid black yin-yang whose two small circles open onto glowing marbled gold and blue: a digital abstract, printed to order.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished. Maybe it's above a console, at the end of a hallway, or on a dark wall that swallows anything timid.

This is the kind of piece that wall can take. A tall oval holds a large black yin-yang on a black mat, and color glows around it and through it. It reads from across the room, then pulls you closer.

Most yin-yang art paints the symbol in two flat tones. This one is solid black, and the color lives behind it. You see that color as a crescent around the symbol and through its two small circles, which are cut out like windows.

Each window frames a different slice of the swirl. The color moves from golden yellow and amber orange on the left to cobalt blue and violet at the upper right, with small touches of teal, magenta and burnt red. The curls run in S-shaped currents that echo the curve of the symbol, so your eye keeps travelling between the dark shape and the light behind it.

The yin-yang is a familiar symbol of duality, and here the warm side and the cool side show it without a word. You can read it as balance, or simply enjoy the black against the color. Either is a fine reason to want it on your wall.

It is a vertical, symmetrical piece, so it suits a single centered placement above a sofa, bed, console or desk, or at the end of an entry or hallway. The deep black mat and saturated color read well from a distance.

It is especially at home on a dark or neutral wall, where the gold, orange and blue can glow. It also suits a living room, office or bedroom that needs one confident piece rather than a crowd of small ones.
