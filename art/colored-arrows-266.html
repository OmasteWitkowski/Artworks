One Purple Sliver That Glows Like a Lantern

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Lantern Drift is a close-up abstract of glossy, translucent shapes, with a tall purple-magenta teardrop standing between warm peach and green-yellow.

You know the wall. It's the long one over the sofa, or the bed, or the sideboard, and it has looked a little bare since the furniture settled in. Everything else in the room is finished. That wall is the one thing still waiting.

Picture a single tall sliver of purple and magenta standing in the middle of it, with warm peach and red on one side and leaf green fading to yellow on the other. That's Lantern Drift.

The first thing you see is the purple. It's a narrow, pointed teardrop right at the center, dotted with tiny round bubbles. Soft light seems to pass through it from behind, so it reads like a lantern lit from within.

Then your eye starts to travel. Curves sweep from the upper left down to the lower right, and the sliver pulls your gaze downward and across the frame in a slow, liquid drift. Thin gold lines trace every place where one shape meets the next. They keep each color zone clean, so it never turns into a muddy wash.

There's no horizon here and no single object to name. The surfaces look smooth and glass-like, and up close the color is faintly grainy. I'd rather you look for a while and decide for yourself what the shapes are. If the color draws you in, that is reason enough to hang it.

Lantern Drift is a wide, horizontal image with a strong vertical accent in the middle. That makes it a natural fit for a long wall, such as over a sofa, a bed, a sideboard or a dining area. The warm peach and red, the leaf green and the central purple are strong enough to read from across the room.

It's saturated and warm without being busy. Against calm, neutral walls it brings the color, and the purple gives the eye one place to settle.
