A glowing orange center, and no one agrees what it is

Abstract Glass Macro · horizontal

Ember Window is a glossy, glass-like abstract: an orange center that looks lit from behind, a dark purple rim and one golden arc on royal blue.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch above it still looks unfinished. Whatever hangs there now either looks small or says nothing.

Picture an orange that looks lit from behind, sitting in a dark purple-maroon ring, with a thin golden arc curving over it against deep blue. That is Ember Window. It reads from across the room, and it still gives you something to find when you step up close.

The glow comes first. The orange center looks as if light is passing through layers behind it, and darker streaks fan outward from the middle. Bright highlights run along the curved edges.

Then the arc. A thin golden-yellow band starts at the left, climbs over the top and comes down the right side. Behind it the top of the picture is royal blue, and warm red-orange swirls sweep across the lower right. Cool above, warm below, and the gold ties the two together.

Then the edges. The surface looks smooth and glossy, like glass or liquid. Small round bubbles and tiny specks gather on the right, and fine rippled lines run through the left third. Stay a while and the edges give you as much to look at as the center.

Some people see an eye, some a flower, some a lit opening. It is none of these literally, and I would rather you decide than have me tell you. Your own response is the only test that counts.

Ember Window is horizontal, so it suits a wall with room to breathe: over a sofa, a bed, a long console or a stairwell landing. It has one strong warm presence, and a quiet room gets a place to look.

The amber, gold and burnt red sit well against neutral walls and furniture, and the royal blue and indigo give them something cool to push against. It is a bold picture, so give it space.
