Empty chairs, a glowing valley, and a view left open for you

Travel / Specialty / Projects · horizontal

A wide, horizontal photograph with a soft painterly finish: a wooden deck, a hazy mountain valley, and a seat waiting for whoever hangs it.

You know the wall. It's over the sofa, the bed or the dining table. The furniture is settled and the paint is right, but the wall still looks unfinished. Nothing you've found for it has felt like a view you'd want to stay in.

This is the picture I'd hang there. A wooden deck runs on a diagonal toward a wide mountain valley. The tables and chairs are empty, and the low sun spreads a golden haze across the sky. Nobody is in the frame, so the seat is yours.

Most mountain pictures put you on a trail or a summit. This one puts you in a seat. The weathered boards and the white railing sit in the foreground, and the metal tables and chairs sit under a broad roof. You take the view from there, not from the peak.

What holds me here is the empty seats. They leave the view open, so what it feels like to sit there is yours to decide. I'd rather you stand in front of it than have me tell you.

Then the layers start to show. The deck gives way to a tree-covered slope, then the flat valley floor, then ridgelines fading to dusty violet-blue. The railing and the deck boards pull your eye toward the bright horizon. The sweep of the roof pulls it back again.

A soft, canvas-like painterly surface covers the whole image. The wood grain, the mesh tabletops and the edges of the trees still read clearly beneath it.

This is a wide horizontal image, so it suits a long wall: over a sofa, a bed, a dining table or a console. The pale sky and open valley give the wall breathing room, and the warm browns and cream sit well in rooms with warm, quiet tones.

It reads as a window you didn't have before. The scene is quiet, with an early or late-day light, and it asks nothing of the room except space.
