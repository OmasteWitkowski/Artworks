A Red Crab That Glows Like Stained Glass

Butterflies, Birds & Wildlife · square

Ember Claw is a symmetrical crimson crab silhouette, filled with a mirrored pattern of red, orange and amber, on a clean white ground.

You know the wall. It's the one by the kitchen table, or in the dining area, or in the hallway you walk down every day. The room is finished and the wall still looks bare.

Picture a single deep red crab there, centered on white, claws raised. It is bold enough to read from across the room, and it isn't the realistic seafood picture or the cartoon you've seen a hundred times.

From across the room, you see a crab. It's a clean silhouette from above, with two claws sweeping up and outward, four legs fanning out on each side, and two folded front legs pointing down. Thin white lines divide the shell and claws into plates and joints, in a tribal-tattoo style.

Step closer and the red stops being flat. The body is filled with swirls, teardrops, diamonds and small mirrored motifs in crimson, burnt orange, amber and dark maroon, with a few touches of purple-pink. Glowing amber and orange spots sit inside the red, so the crab looks faintly backlit, like a stained-glass panel.

Two ideas sit together in this piece. The outline is a clear, bold crab. The pattern inside is its own abstract design. The near-perfect symmetry keeps it steady, while the lifted claws and spread legs make the crab look ready to move.

I'd rather you stand in front of it than read about it. There is no cast light and no shadow, just crisp edges, clean white lines and a lot of color.

The square format and centered crab make Ember Claw work as a single statement on a plain wall. It suits a kitchen, a dining area, a beach house, or a restaurant or seafood-themed space. Red and white is a confident palette, and the amber and orange glints keep it warm.

The coastal subject is plain to see, but the stained-glass fill and tattoo-style linework mean it reads as graphic decoration, not a literal seafood picture. It also works as one of a pair, thanks to the white space around the crab.

Measure the wall space first, then pick from the sizes offered on the listing. The piece is square, so it suits a spot that is roughly as tall as it is wide. The generous white space around the crab also gives it some breathing room on the wall.

Screens differ, so I can't promise an exact match. What I can tell you is that the red is deep crimson, with burnt orange and amber glints and a little dark maroon. If it isn't what you hoped for, Fine Art America's 30-day money-back guarantee applies.
