One bright seam runs through a field of deep green

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Green Seam is a horizontal abstract of glassy forest and moss green shapes, a thin glowing line down the middle and a dusty rose edge.

You know the wall. It's the wide one over the sofa or the bed, and every time you walk past it, it looks a little bare. The room is finished. The wall isn't.

Now picture a field of deep green there, with the greens swelling like smooth glass. One thin, bright line crosses the middle, and a band of dusty rose runs down the right edge. That is Green Seam.

The greens hold you first: deep forest, moss and olive, with yellow-green showing at the edges of the forms. The surfaces are smooth and glassy, and they seem to glow softly, as if lit from within.

Then the seam. Two large green masses meet along a diagonal, and a thin bright line traces where they touch. Just below it sit a few tiny bubbles, small enough to miss on a first pass.

Then the pink. Without that band of dusty rose and mauve, the image would be all green. With it, the greens look deeper.

I leave what it is, or what it might remind you of, to you. Art is felt, and your own response is the only test that counts.

Green Seam is horizontal, so it suits a wide wall: over a sofa, a bed, a console or a sideboard. The deep greens anchor a room, and the rose edge picks up blush, mauve or warm neutrals you already have.

The look is minimal and fluid, with no busy detail to compete with your furniture. It is a quiet piece, but not a plain one.
