Black ribbons, and a glow that looks lit from behind

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Windows is a horizontal abstract: heavy black ribbon forms, edged in fine colored lines, framing a glowing orange, violet and blue field.

You know the wall. The sofa is new, the paint is settled, and that wide stretch of wall above it has looked bare for months. You've pictured something there, but nothing you've found looks like more than a placeholder.

This is a piece for that wall. Thick black shapes curl and fold across the frame like twisted ribbons, or a maze you could follow with your finger. Behind them, orange, violet and cobalt blue glow through the gaps as if lit from behind.

The first thing you see is the contrast. The black is heavy and absorbs the light. Because of that, the color behind it looks brighter, as if it were lit from within.

Then you step closer. The edges of the black shapes are lined with many fine threads of olive, blue and orange, a little like topographic lines or wood grain. Against them, the background is smooth and liquid, like glossy flowing glass or paint.

The openings in the black begin to read like windows. Small white highlights sit in the blue swirls. Your eye follows the looping outlines around the frame and keeps finding another bend.

I don't tell you what it is. You may see ribbons, a maze, or something else. Whatever you see is yours to trust.

Ember Windows is a horizontal piece, so it suits a wide wall: over a sofa, a bed, a long console or a dining wall. The dense black lets it anchor a large space without being overwhelmed by it.

It works well in rooms with neutral, dark or wood tones. There the orange, violet and blue can echo accents you already have, or bring in a color the room has been missing.
