Pools of Color That Look Lit From Behind

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember and Lilac Pools is a horizontal abstract of amber, crimson, lilac and green shapes whose edges meet in thin, glowing lines.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one wide stretch above it still looks unfinished. You have probably carried a few ideas past it in your head and none of them fit.

This is the image I would put there. Amber-orange, crimson, lilac-pink and lime green sit edge to edge like pools of liquid color, and they look lit from behind. There is no object in it and no landscape. It is color you can look into.

What holds me here is the light. The colors look as if a glow is passing through them, and thin bright lines trace the curved edges where one shape meets the next. Each hue slips into its neighbor gently, so the whole spectrum sits side by side without getting loud.

A red wedge hangs from the top right. An orange mound rises from the bottom. Green fills the left, a brown arc curves across the upper left, and pink-violet takes up the right. In the middle, a pale, cool area sits between the warm and cool sides and ties them together.

Nothing in it tells you what to think. Some people will see cells, some molten color, some a place that does not exist. Look closer and you will find tiny bubble-like specks and fine lines along the edges. Art is felt, not seen, and whatever you feel in front of it is the right response.

The horizontal frame suits a wide wall, such as over a sofa, a bed, a console or a dining buffet. The amber and terracotta tones sit well with wood, leather and neutral walls, and they carry the eye across the room.

The cool, pale center gives the eye somewhere to rest, so a piece this colorful does not feel heavy. It is a warm, playful image, and its pull is purely visual.
