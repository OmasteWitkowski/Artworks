Are they birds? Two rust forms meet across turquoise

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

"Face to Face" is a horizontal digital abstract in which two mirrored, spiral-built forms lean toward each other across a swirling turquoise field.

You know the wall. It's the one over the sofa, or the bed, or the console, and the room around it is finished. The paint is chosen and the furniture is where you want it. The wall is still empty, and it has bothered you for months.

This is the piece I'd hold up to it. Two rust-brown shapes face each other across a field of bright turquoise, and nearly every inch of the picture is wound into small spirals. It reads from across the room as a bold colour statement, and the closer you walk, the more spirals you find.

I'll tell you what I see when I look at it, because the picture does the talking better than I can.

The first thing is the colour. Deep rust brown and burnt sienna sit against turquoise and cerulean, warm against cool, and the two make each other louder. Then the shapes come forward. Each brown form carries a pale cream wedge and a small cream spot. Put those in the right places and you get a beak and an eye, so you may see two birds or two creatures. Some people see only two shapes, and that reading is just as good.

Look longer and the surface keeps going. The brown shapes are packed with tight, dark, concentric curls. The blue between them opens into looser rings. The cream areas relax into smooth, buttery strokes. Glossy highlights on the swirl edges make it look lit from within, like enamel. The two cream wedges point inward, so the forms seem to lean toward each other, as if they are meeting or squaring off.

I like that it stays playful and a little curious. It doesn't explain itself, and I'd rather leave it that way.

The format is horizontal and the arrangement is roughly symmetrical, which suits a focal spot above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The saturated colour reads clearly from across a room.

The turquoise holds up well against neutral walls. The rust brown picks up wood, leather and terracotta you may already own. In a more eclectic room, the swirl pattern can sit comfortably with other bold things.
