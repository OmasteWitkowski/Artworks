Is that a mouth, a mask or a butterfly?

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · vertical

Lit From Within is a tall, mirrored abstract in peach, cobalt blue, red and purple, with a glossy red shape at its glowing center.

You know the wall. It might be the narrow stretch at the top of the stairs, the entry, or the space beside the bed. The paint and furniture are settled, and that one wall still looks unfinished.

A tall, quiet landscape or a pale print will not do much there. This piece is a different kind of answer: a glowing peach oval, framed by deep blue and red, running the full height of the wall.

First comes the glow. The peach and cream oval at the center looks lit from within, and small pale-yellow and white highlights sit in the spirals on either side of it. The corners fall away into dark navy and red, and that contrast keeps your eye in the middle.

Then the red shape. At the very center is something glossy and red. I see a mouth. You may see a mask, a face, or a butterfly with its wings open. None of these is the right answer, and the piece does not ask you to pick one.

Then you notice the mirror. Everything is reflected left to right and top to bottom. A purple spine runs through the top and bottom of the image, and forms like petals, flames and wings curl away from it in matching pairs.

Stay a little longer and the small things appear: thin, sharp tendrils, and tiny spirals near the edges that you missed the first time. This is digital art, and the surface is smooth, polished and liquid-looking, with no visible brushwork.

The tall vertical shape suits a narrow wall: a stairwell, an entry, the space beside a bed, or the wall above a console. The oval and the mirrored pairs can run the full height instead of being squeezed into a wide frame.

The peach and blush center sits well with warm neutrals, and the dark navy and red corners give it presence against lighter walls. The mood is vibrant and warm, a little dreamlike, calm and energetic at once.

Picture it on that wall: the peach center glowing at eye level, the blue and red corners holding the edges, and the mirrored shapes running up the wall toward the ceiling. Then picture a guest stopping to ask what the red shape is, and you asking what they see.
