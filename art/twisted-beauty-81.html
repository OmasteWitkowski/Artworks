A Hot Pink Arch Wrapped Around Two Glowing Green Forms

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · square

Pink Embrace is a square, painterly macro abstraction in magenta, lime and teal, and you can choose your print size on Fine Art America.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished. Anything quiet would disappear there. Anything generic would look like it came with the room.

This is the piece I'd put in front of that wall. A thick, glossy magenta stem sweeps up from the lower left, over the top, and down around two plump lime-green forms. Behind it, pale aqua deepens to teal, forest green and near-black. It is bold, a little strange, and hard to forget.

The colour stops you first. Hot magenta and acid green are about as opposite as colours get. Teal and deep shadow sit between them and keep them from fighting, and a little pale aqua at the lower left gives your eye somewhere to rest.

Then the shapes arrive. The pink arch moves your eye up, over and down to the pair beneath it. The two green forms sit side by side with a narrow dark seam between them, and vertical ribs show most clearly on the right one. It looks like a close-up of something growing, though the image doesn't say what. It reads as a loop, or an embrace. That reading comes from the shapes alone, and you may see something different.

Then the surface. A fine, rippled, oil-paint-like texture covers everything. It looks grainy and brushed across the green forms and stays smooth and glossy along the pink stem. A close-up subject ends up looking painted and dreamlike. The edges soften into a shallow depth of field, so the focus settles on the pair.

I'd rather you stand in front of it than read about it. If it makes you smile, that is reason enough.

The square format and dark, saturated edges suit a single statement piece. Think of the wall over a sofa, an entry that needs a first impression, or a studio or creative workspace that wants some energy. The pink arch leads the eye in a calm loop, so the colour is vivid without feeling restless.

It also sits well beside other bright, colour-forward work if you like to build a small group. Measure your wall first. A square suits a spot that is roughly as tall as it is wide.
