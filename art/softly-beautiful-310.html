One glossy maroon shape that looks lit from within

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Drop is an abstract of a dark maroon teardrop on a cream field, framed by warm bands of plum, peach and orange.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch above it still looks unfinished. You have probably been looking at it for months.

What that wall wants is not a pattern or a pale wash. It wants one thing for the eye to land on. This is the piece I would put there: a heavy, glossy maroon shape with a bright white edge, sitting in a quiet cream field.

I did not paint you a scene, and I won't tell you what this is. Here is what I see.

The eye starts at the red form in the top right corner. It narrows like a stem or a pour. Follow that line down and you arrive at the large maroon shape below center, tapering to a point at the bottom. It looks smooth and wet. A sharp white highlight runs along its lower edge, and a few tiny bubble-like dots sit near it.

Then you notice the rest. A smaller burgundy shape sits at the upper left. At the lower left there is one small pale aqua droplet, the only cool note in all that warmth. Behind them, broad vertical bands of plum, peach, orange and olive-yellow frame the cream center.

The colors look lit from within, soft where they blend and crisp where the dark shapes meet the cream. What you feel in front of it is yours to interpret.

This is a horizontal piece. It suits a wall that needs one confident focal point, such as above a sofa, a bed or a sideboard. The single central shape and the wide bands hold up from the other side of the room.

The warm cream ground sits easily in rooms that already have cream, tan, rust or deep red. If you want color that feels warm and settled rather than busy, this is that kind of color.
