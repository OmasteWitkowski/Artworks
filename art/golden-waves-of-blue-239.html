Liquid Fire Held in Shadow: A Glowing Gold Swirl

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Dome is a horizontal digital abstract in which a molten-looking golden mass glows at the center, cradled by a sweeping dark slate-blue arc.

You know the wall. It might be above the sofa, the bed or the console. The furniture is settled and the paint is right, but that stretch of wall still looks unfinished, and it has for months.

Now picture a rounded mass of golden swirls in the middle of it, glowing as if lit from behind. A dark arc curves over it from the left to the upper right, and the gold looks brighter for being held in that shadow.

This is the piece I'd ask you to stand in front of. From a few steps back, it reads as one bold shape. A golden dome sits at the center and lower center, framed by dark slate blue and charcoal purple. The cool shapes recede, so the warm ones come forward and seem to burn.

Come closer and the surface changes. It looks smooth and glossy, almost like molten glass or oil. It is made of fine flowing contour lines and small marbled eddies. Thin bright yellow edges trace the curls, and faint circular arcs cross the image like ripples.

Then the eye starts to travel. Curls flow outward from the central glow, and smaller whorls spiral through the upper left corner and along the right edge. Burnt orange fills the upper left and the lower edge, so warm and cool areas alternate across the frame. Your eye keeps finding its way back to the glow.

It is listed as digital art. I won't tell you what it means. That part is yours to feel.

Ember Dome is a horizontal piece, so it suits wide spaces such as above a sofa, a bed, a console or a mantel. The central glow can anchor the room, and the warm and cool areas can pick up colors already around it.

The dark blue-gray areas sit well with neutral or cool surroundings, and the burnt orange and gold bring in one strong warm note. It is energetic and dramatic, a piece that holds attention rather than fading into the background.
