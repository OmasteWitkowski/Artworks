One leaf-or-flame at the center of hundreds of tiny cells

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · square

Ember Leaf is a square abstract of fine white linework, glowing rust, gold and teal, and a zigzag starburst around a tall central shape.

You bought the sofa. The paint is done. And the wall above it is still bare, or still holding something you chose years ago that now looks small in the room.

Picture a square of burnt orange and rust, crossed by crisp white lines, with a tall shape rising through the middle. From the doorway it reads as a warm burst of color. Step closer and it opens into hundreds of small striped cells.

First, the center. A tall shape rises to a point, ringed by a starburst of white zigzags. I see a leaf or a flame there. You may see something else, and that is fine.

Then the cells. Fine white lines divide the whole square into small curved, pointed and zigzag sections, and each one is filled with thin parallel or concentric stripes. Follow the lines and you can watch them fan out from the top center and swirl into spirals in the corners.

Then the color. Burnt orange and rust red carry the piece, with navy, golden yellow, sage and teal showing between the lines. The white outlines have a faint glow, so the colors look lit from behind, like stained glass. Look for the paler, translucent shapes sitting beneath the linework.

What I keep coming back to is the contrast: the sharp zigzags set against the soft, flowing curves. A small border of oval beads holds the whole thing in place.

Ember Leaf is square, centered and symmetrical, so it sits comfortably in a statement spot: over a sofa, above a bed, on the wall over a console, or on a stairwell landing that needs color.

The palette is warm, with burnt orange and rust leading and teal and navy as counterpoints. It suits someone who wants real pattern and warm color on the wall without a landscape or a flower.
