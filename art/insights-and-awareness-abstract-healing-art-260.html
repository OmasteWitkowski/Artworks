Two Glowing Windows, or Two Faces? Look a Little Longer

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · square

Ember Windows is a symmetrical digital abstract of two golden-rimmed ovals filled with molten red, orange and blue, set on deep cobalt.

You bring in the new sofa, step back, and the wall above it looks bare. Or the old picture looks small in the bigger room. You have probably known for a while that the wall needs one confident piece, and nothing you have found so far looks like it belongs to you.

This is the piece I would put in front of you. Two tall ovals sit side by side in the middle of a deep blue square. Each has a golden-yellow rim, as if a light were burning behind it. Inside, red, orange and yellow swirls flow down into bands of turquoise and blue.

The first thing you notice is the heat against the cold. Saturated orange and bright red sit inside a field of deep cobalt, and neither one backs down.

Then the symmetry settles in. The left oval mirrors the right exactly, so all that swirling movement is held in a balanced, still arrangement. Your eye keeps going to the narrow gap between the two ovals. Two darker ovals, maroon above and blue-brown below, sit behind them and complete a shape like a clover. The glow is strongest at the center and fades toward the edges, which is why the two central ovals look like lit windows in a dark wall.

Look a little longer and the mirrored swirls may start to look like two faces or profiles turned toward each other. That resemblance is chance, and you may not see it at all. Some people see two faces and some see only two windows. I think art is felt, and what you find here is yours to decide.

The square, centred, symmetrical format suits a wall that needs one strong anchor, such as above a sofa, a bed, a console or a stairwell landing. The strong colour can hold a room with plain or neutral walls and furniture.

The deep blue sits comfortably with warm wood, cream and neutral walls. The orange and red give a plain room something to hold onto.

You don't need a qualification. I believe there is no good or bad art, and your own response is the only test. If these two glowing ovals stop you, that is reason enough. If they don't, nothing I write here should change your mind.

Picture the wall that has bothered you for months, and two golden-rimmed ovals glowing above the sofa, with all that orange and red held against deep blue. A visitor may see two faces, or just two lit windows, and either way they will probably ask where you found it.
