Every Shape Has an Exact Twin: Ember Mirror

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · vertical

Ember Mirror is a tall, four-way mirrored abstract in orange, violet and cobalt, with a soft rose-mauve center.

You know the wall. It's the tall, narrow one in the entry, the hallway, the stairwell, or beside a doorway. Everything else in the room is settled, and that stretch of wall is still bare. Most art made for it comes out too small, too wide, or too polite.

Ember Mirror is built for that shape. It's a vertical abstract in fiery orange, deep violet, cobalt blue and olive green, and it looks lit from within. Your eye lands on the quiet center first, then follows the spirals back out.

Start in the middle. A soft, hazy rose-mauve field sits at the center, and it's the calmest part of the image. Everything around it is moving.

Follow the curling ribbons outward. Their orange and gold edges glow like embers against the darker blues, purples and reds. Thin looping lines trace each shape and fold back on themselves.

Then look at the top and bottom edges. A blue-and-purple oval holds an olive-green band that ends in a glowing orange point, and the same oval appears again at the opposite end. The whole image is mirrored top to bottom and left to right, so every motif has an exact twin.

Once you see the symmetry, you may see a kaleidoscope. You may see an ornate mask or a face. Both readings are there, and which one you get is up to you.

The tall format is made for an entry, a hallway, a stairwell, or the wall beside a doorway. It can also anchor the wall above a console or a bed, in a room that can carry saturated color.

The orange and violet will stand out against neutral or quiet furnishings. If your room is already full of strong color, give this one plenty of wall around it.
