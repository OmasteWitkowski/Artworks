A Seat Under the Tree, Looking Out at a Cobalt Sea

Travel / Specialty / Projects · horizontal

"Quiet Seat Beneath the Windswept Tree" is a coastal landscape photograph: a huge arching limb, a deep blue sea, and a tiny figure tucked among red flowers.

You've got the sofa, the paint and the lamps. The wall above it is still bare, and you've been looking past it for months. Most coastal pictures would only give you sea and sky. You don't need another one of those.

Picture this instead. A thick, reddish-brown trunk arches in from the right, and its feathery branches spread across the top of the frame like a roof. Under it, a dark rocky shoreline meets a deep blue sea, and near the edge a small figure sits half hidden by red flowers, looking out at the water.

I can't give you a made-up memory of this spot, so I'll tell you what the picture does. It starts with the blue. The sea and the sky are both a deep cobalt, and the clouds have bright edges. Then the warm, low light finds the golden grass and the trunk, and the whole scene turns blue and gold.

Then you notice the limb. It sweeps from right to left across the top and gives you a sheltered place to stand, as if you were sitting under the tree. The line of dark rocks leads your eye across the picture toward the horizon, which sits about halfway down.

The last thing you find is the small seated figure beside the red flowers, looking out toward the sea. It's easy to miss at first. Once you've seen it, you can't unsee it, and it gives the whole scene its scale. I leave the rest to you.

This is a horizontal piece with strong contrast and one clear arc, the sweep of the tree. That suits a wide wall above a sofa, a bed or a console, where the blue and gold can be seen from across the room. It also works in an office where you want a broad, colorful view of the coast.

The mood is open-air and unhurried: calm sea, drifting clouds, a quiet spot to sit and watch the water. The blue pairs easily with neutral walls, and the gold and brown pick up warm woods and textiles.
