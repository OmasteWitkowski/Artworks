A surfer, a sunset, and a porthole of jewel-toned color

Travel / Specialty / Projects · horizontal

Catch Another One is a digital surf-poster illustration: a black surfer silhouette inside a deep navy circle, ringed by a glowing rainbow kaleidoscope border.

You know the wall. Maybe it's above the sofa in the den, or behind the bar, or over the bed in a room that's meant to feel like a day at the beach. The furniture is settled and the paint is done, but the wall still looks like nobody has decided what goes there.

This is the piece I'd hold up to that wall. A small black figure stands with arms outstretched against a warm orange-and-gold sunset, sealed inside a deep navy circle. A ring of glowing color surrounds the circle. You can read it from across the room before you've walked close enough to see any detail.

Start with the surfer. The silhouette is solid black, arms wide, standing on an angled board among black and white palm trees. Behind the figure sits a block of sunset that runs from orange to yellow. Tropical leaves, curling black flourishes and a splash of blue water add movement around it.

Then look at the circle. It's deep navy with a glowing yellow ring, and the scene sits in its upper half. Small white sparkle stars catch the light. Because the circle is centered and framed by a mirrored border, it feels like looking through a porthole or a lens.

The border is the second surprise. Glassy, bubble-like lobes in red, green, blue and violet fan out from the circle into the corners, perfectly symmetrical. So the piece reads as a retro surf poster and a kaleidoscope at the same time.

Last, the gold script at the lower right: "Waves Come. Let the other guys go. Catch another one." It is attributed in the image to Duke Kahanamoku. With the surfer's open arms above it, the words sit easily in the picture. It's a message about patience and taking the next chance, said in a sunny, carefree way.

The horizontal format suits the wide spots: above a sofa, a bar, a desk or a bed. The navy, gold and orange work well in a casual, beach-minded room, such as a living room, den or game room, or anywhere you want a cheerful hit of color.

It's a good match for surf culture, but you don't need a reason beyond liking it. If it stops you, that's reason enough.

Picture it on that bare wall: the navy circle glowing above the sofa, the sunset orange picking up a cushion, the gold words catching the light. Guests will ask where it came from, and the answer is a surf poster with a kaleidoscope around it.
