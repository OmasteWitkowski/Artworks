One cool patch makes the hot red glow

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Drift is a red, orange and gold fluid abstract that looks lit from behind, and it's ready to be printed in the size your wall needs.

You bought the sofa. The paint is dry. And the wall above it is still bare, the one you look at every evening and think, not yet.

Picture a horizontal piece there that fills the space with heat and light: deep crimson, burnt orange and amber gold, melting into each other inside a thin glowing edge of gold. That's Ember Drift.

I'll tell you what I see when I look at this piece. First it's the heat: big masses of red and orange that seem to flow and push into one another. Then the gold, which brightens toward the top center, where the yellow is strongest.

Then the one thing that doesn't belong, and makes the whole piece work: a pale grey-white patch on the right. It's cool and soft, and the hot colors have something to push against. Without it, this could read as one block of red.

Stay longer and the edges start to move. They're jagged and feathery, pointing diagonally across the frame, and the colors seem to drift toward the lower right. The surface looks liquid, with fine rippling lines running through it. The wide dark maroon border carries a faint pattern of circles and curls, which you only notice up close.

To me it feels like embers, or autumn color. That's my reading, and yours may be different. Art is felt, and what you feel in front of it is the only test that counts.

This is a horizontal piece, so it suits a wall with room to spare: over a sofa, a bed, a fireplace or a console. The dark border and warm glow suit a moody or richly colored room, and the colors sit well with deep neutrals and wood tones.

It's a statement piece rather than a quiet one. If your room already has some richness in it, this will meet it.
