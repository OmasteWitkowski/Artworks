Three white spirals, and a red that glows behind them

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · square

Ember Spirals is a square digital abstract: three white-outlined spirals curling to tiny centres over deep brick red, with mustard, teal and navy showing through like lit glass.

You know the wall. It might be over the sofa, the bed or the console. The furniture is settled and the paint is done, and that one stretch of wall has bothered you for months. Nothing you have found for it looks like it belongs to you.

This is the piece I would hold up to it. Three large spirals fill a square of deep brick red and oxblood maroon. Bright white lines glow against the dark ground, and each spiral pulls your eye inward to a tiny centre.

From across a room, you see the graphic part first: white spirals, a rich red, a lacy border of oval cells. Two spirals sit stacked on the left and one tall oval spiral stands on the right. Curving bands link them and carry your eye around the square.

Step closer and the surface turns dense. Fine parallel hatching, feather-like strokes and rows of small pebble-shaped cells shrink toward each centre. Under the lines, soft blended colour gives the red a glassy, marbled depth.

Patches of mustard yellow, teal, sage, navy and lavender grey show through that red like coloured glass lit from behind. The light is even, with no shadows, so the lines stay crisp everywhere you look.

I like a piece that gives you something new on a second and third look. What this one means is yours to decide.

Ember Spirals is a square, high-contrast piece that works as a single focal point. It suits the wall over a sofa, a bed or a console, or a stairwell wall where people pass slowly and get a second look.
