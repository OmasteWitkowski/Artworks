A Blue Star Blooms in a Field of Molten Gold

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Cobalt Starbloom is a digital abstract: a deep blue star-or-flower form at the center, glowing gold and orange around it, and ripples along the bottom edge.

You know the wall. It's above the sofa, or the bed, or the console. The furniture is settled and the paint is chosen, and the wall still looks unfinished every time you walk past it.

Picture a horizontal piece there with one dark blue form in the middle, glowing gold and orange around it, and green curls reaching in from both sides. This is Cobalt Starbloom.

I'll tell you what is in the image and leave the rest to you. The blue form at the center has angular points that radiate outward. It could be a star or the petals of a flower, or neither. I don't need you to settle it.

Look at the edges of the blue and you'll see pale highlights along its inner rim. Around it the gold and orange seem to glow without any single light source. Small spiral swirls sit in the yellow and green like eddies in moving water.

The surface is smooth and glossy, with no visible brushwork. The shapes look poured or melted, stretching and dripping outward from the middle. Along the bottom, rippled blue lines run side to side and could be water or a reflection.

The green tendrils and spiral coils mirror each other on the left and right. That gives the whole thing a steady rhythm while the curling shapes keep your eye moving.

The format is horizontal, so it suits a wall that runs wide: above a sofa or bed, over a console, or behind a desk. The warm edges and cool blue center hold attention from across the room.

In a neutral room, the blue and gold will be the color everything else answers to. In a room that already has warm and cool accents, the piece can pick them up. The mood is lively and playful, not quiet.
