The mountain outlines that spill out of their own frame

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Drawn Summit is a stylized digital landscape of two slate blue peaks, traced in thick, wobbly black line that runs past the picture onto a dark border.

You bought the new sofa, and now the wall above it looks bare. Or the old picture looks small in the bigger room. Or you have seen the generic mountain print you nearly bought hanging in someone else's house.

This is a different kind of mountain. It is not soft or hazy, and it does not look like a photograph. Two craggy peaks are outlined in thick black line, like a comic-book drawing, in cool blue against pale cream sky. You can read the shape from across the room.

I have no travel story to tell you about this mountain, and I won't make one up. What I can do is point to what is in the picture.

First you see the silhouette: a tall summit right of center and a lower, rounded peak on the left, with a ridge dipping between them. Your eye climbs the angled slopes to the top. Then you notice the line. It wobbles like a hand-drawn contour and traces the ridges, the rock and the clouds.

Then you see that the line does not stop. It runs past the thin inner frame onto the wide, dark border, so the drawing seems to push out of its picture. Loose black strokes drift through the sky as well, a gentle, restless rhythm over calm, even light.

Up close, the color is flattened into posterized patches with a faint canvas-like grain. The rock face is mottled with small blotches of blue, gray and brown. It is a rugged mountain, drawn with confidence.

This is a horizontal piece with strong contrast, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a console or a desk. The dark border and charcoal lines anchor the space, and the cool blues keep it calm.

It works where you want a graphic, cool-toned landscape instead of a realistic view. Think living room, home office or study. It is a mountain picture and a line drawing at once.

I can't promise an exact match, because every screen shows color a little differently. The palette is slate blue, charcoal and pale cream with small tan and green patches. The 30-day money-back guarantee covers you if it isn't right in your room.

I believe art is felt, not seen, and that your own response is the only test that counts. If the outlined peaks stopped you, that is reason enough. You don't need to know anything else about art.

Picture the wall above the sofa with two blue peaks across it, the black line running out to the edge of the dark border, and the cream sky lifting the room. When a guest asks where it came from, you can tell them it is a mountain drawn like a comic book, and that you liked it the moment you saw it.
