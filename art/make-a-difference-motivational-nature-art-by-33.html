A bumblebee in sharp focus, and the words beside it

Botanical & Flowers · horizontal

A macro photograph of a fuzzy bumblebee on violet lupine, with a Jane Goodall quote in navy script, printed to order in the size and surface you choose.

You know the wall. It's the one over your desk, or beside the reading chair, or by the front door. Everything else in the room is settled, and that wall has stayed bare, or it holds something you stopped noticing a long time ago.

Picture a small bumblebee clinging to a tall spike of violet-blue lupine, so close you can see the fur on its back. Beside it, in open sage green, a few lines of navy script ask what kind of difference you want to make. That's the piece.

The bee comes first. It's the sharpest thing in the frame: black, golden yellow and a touch of orange, with fuzzy fur you can almost feel. It sits right where the flowers meet the open space.

Then the lupine. The petals are velvety and slightly translucent, with fine veins, in violet-blue and periwinkle. The spike rises on the left and carries your eye upward.

Then the eye drifts right, across a smooth, creamy blur of sage green and faint violet, to the words. They're Jane Goodall's: "What you do makes a difference, and you have to decide what kind of difference you want to make." The quote is set inside the photograph, in the same soft, even daylight, so the image and the message arrive together.

The bee is small and paused on a single blossom, which gives those words a gentle, concrete picture. I leave the rest to you.

This is a horizontal piece with two clear zones: the flower and bee on the left, open space and words on the right. It suits a wall you stand or sit fairly near, where the quote can be read, such as a desk wall, home office, reading nook, sunroom, entry or classroom. It sits comfortably above a desk, console or shelf.

The palette is soft: violet-blue, sage green and deep navy, with small golden accents from the bee. It works with a lot of settled rooms without taking them over. I'd think of it as a small-to-medium statement piece rather than a giant one.

Measure first. Tape a paper or painter's tape rectangle on the wall and live with it for a day. This piece is built for close-to-moderate viewing, where you can read the quote, so I'd lean small to medium. The listing shows the sizes available.
