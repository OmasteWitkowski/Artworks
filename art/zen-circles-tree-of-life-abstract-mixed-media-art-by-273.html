White Lines That Glow Like Stained Glass Lit From Behind

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · square

Ember Window is a square of glowing white line work over burnt orange and rust, with six spiral roses and a tree-like trunk.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the walls are neutral, the wood tones are settled, and one wall still looks bare. It has looked that way for months, and nothing you've found has been right for it.

This is the piece I'd put in front of that wall. It is a square of fine white lines so dense that they form a web of triangles and fanning rays, laid over warm orange and rust. The lines glow, and from across the room the whole thing looks lit from behind.

From a distance you get colour and glow. Burnt orange and rust red fill the square, and a thin dark red border holds them in. The white lines have a soft halo, so the piece reads like stained glass with a lamp behind it.

Come closer and it changes. Crisp white lines sit over softer, mottled colour, and the mesh of triangles starts to look like cracked mosaic. Golden yellow, cobalt blue, teal and sage show through the gaps. Six spirals, each inside an oval, sit in a loose zigzag among the lines. A trunk-like shape rises from the bottom middle and branches upward.

The spirals draw your eye inward and the trunk carries it up. You may see a rose bush, a tree, or simply pattern. All three are fair, and what you see is yours to decide. I'd rather you stand in front of it than have me tell you.

Think of the wall over a sofa or bed, the end of a hallway, or a stairwell. The room is neutral or wood-toned and can take one rich, warm accent. The orange and rust carry the colour, and the white line work gives you detail without clutter.

The pattern is dense and fills the whole surface, so it works as a statement piece rather than a quiet one. It is warm, intricate and energetic, and it doesn't ask for any more reason to love it than that you like looking at it.
