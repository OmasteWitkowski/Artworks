Pink and orange flowers that look lit from within

Botanical & Flowers · horizontal

Lanterns in the Leaves is a painterly flower study: magenta and tangerine blooms with hair-fine stamens, glowing against deep green.

You have the sofa. The paint is settled. And the wide wall above it has bothered you for months, because everything you find looks like everything else.

Picture something different there: pink and orange flowers hanging in long, curved tubes, so saturated against the dark green that they seem to glow.

I won't give you a made-up story about this piece. What I can tell you is what happens when you look at it for a while.

The first thing is the glow. The light is soft and even, with no hard shadows, yet the magenta and tangerine petals look lit from within against all that deep, muted green.

Then you follow the stamens. They trail out of each bloom like fine threads, fan out and droop, and give the image a drifting, airy quality. The tubes sweep from the upper right down to the lower left cluster, and your eye travels with them.

The surface is swirled and painterly, close to oil paint, so the flowers sit somewhere between a photograph and a painting. Flowers can be a pure pleasure of color and shape. I think that is reason enough to want one on a wall.

This is a horizontal piece with open dark space at the edges. It suits a wide wall, such as over a sofa, a bed, a sideboard or a console.

The dark green ground lets the pink and orange carry the room. If your room is already green, or you want one confident color on a settled wall, this is the kind of piece that can do it.
