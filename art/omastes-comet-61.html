A Comet, an Eye or a Glowing Drop? Look Closer

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Amber Comet is a glowing abstract in cobalt blue and gold, with one bright cream starburst that holds your eye.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall above it still looks unfinished. Nothing you've scrolled past has been right. It was all pleasant and none of it was yours.

This is the piece I'd put in front of that wall. A glowing amber shape sweeps across deep cobalt blue and ends in a dark-ringed oval with a pale starburst at its centre. You see it from across the room, and then you walk closer to see what else is in it.

First, the diagonal. The amber form starts cropped at the upper left, so it seems to come from beyond the frame. It runs down to the lower right, and your eye goes with it.

Then the oval at the end. It has a dark brown rim and a cream burst in the middle, which is the brightest point in the whole image. Some people see a comet. Some see an eye. Some see a glowing drop. I'm happy with any of those. What you see is yours to decide.

Then the detail. Fine, curved, hair-like lines run through the green halo and wrap around the oval like a wake. Tiny bubbles and specks are scattered through the blue, and a few larger ones carry bright highlights. The surface looks smooth, glassy and backlit, as if light is passing through it.

The pleasure here is mostly visual: blue against gold, energy and motion, and a single point to land on. I think that is reason enough to want it on a wall.

Amber Comet is a horizontal piece, so it suits a wide wall: over a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The diagonal gives a long wall some movement, and the deep blue corners frame the glowing centre.

It is a confident piece with fully saturated colour. It works best where you want one bold image to anchor the room rather than a quiet accent.
