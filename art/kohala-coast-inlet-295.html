Gold rocks against a cobalt sea, all swirling in the wind

Travel / Specialty / Projects · horizontal

Gold Light Cove is a painterly rocky coast where warm sun-struck rock and leaning trees meet deep blue water and heavy clouds.

You know the wall. It might be over the sofa, over the bed, or down a long hallway. The furniture is settled and the paint is chosen, but that wall still looks unfinished.

Most seascapes you'll find for it are soft blue and sand. This one has weight: dark, chunky rocks, tangled branches, and low sun that turns the shore to amber while the sea stays cool and deep.

I'll let the picture tell it, because it does so in a particular order. First you see the contrast. Gold and amber on the right, cobalt and pale blue on the left. The warm half and the cool half pull against each other, and that tension is what stops most people.

Then the surf pulls you in. White-capped waves roll in from the lower left along a diagonal and wash into a small cove. From there your eye climbs the faceted rocks to the tree on the right, with its bare, looping branches leaning into the wind, and out into layered clouds drifting toward the horizon.

The surface looks thickly worked, like oil paint. The clouds are heavy, billowing strokes, the rocks are blocky and angular, and the branches are fine tangles. Sky, water and trees all swirl in the same wind-shaped rhythm, so the whole scene seems to move.

What you feel standing in front of it is yours to decide. I believe art is felt, not seen.

The shape is horizontal, with an almost level horizon in the upper third. That suits a wide wall: over a sofa, a bed, a console, or along a long hallway. The large sky on the left gives the picture room to breathe, and the rocks and trees anchor the right.

The ochre and cobalt can anchor a room's palette, so it can sit comfortably in a room that is already settled. It would also suit a lake or beach house, or any room where you want warm coastal colour with some weight to it.
