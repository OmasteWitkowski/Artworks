The red and white swirl that turns into a face

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Crimson Totem is a mirrored red and white abstract that glows as if lit from within, with a face in it for you to find or ignore.

You have the sofa you wanted and the paint is done. The wall above it still looks unfinished, and it has for months. Nothing you have tried has felt like yours.

So picture this wall holding Crimson Totem. Deep red and crimson swirl in glossy bands, and bright white ribbons cut through them so the colour looks lit from within. It is the kind of image that stops you mid-scroll, and I think it would stop you mid-room too.

I will not hand you a story about this piece, because the image tells it better. Colour comes first: saturated red, deep crimson, white and blush pink, with small touches of orange, magenta, violet and royal blue low in the frame.

Then the symmetry arrives. The left and right sides mirror each other exactly. A dark crimson oval sits at the centre inside a white arch. Round whorls sit in the upper corners, and a wide white curve runs along the bottom.

Somewhere in that second look, many people see a face, a mask or a totem. Some see it at once and others take a while. Nothing is depicted. The mirroring does that on its own, and what you see in it is up to you.

I would rather you stand in front of a piece than read about it. Art is felt, not seen, and your own response is the only test that counts.

Crimson Totem is horizontal, with a strong central axis. That suits a centred spot over a sofa, a bed or a sideboard, or at the end of a hallway where the red and white can be seen from a distance. It can be the single bold thing on a wall.

The mood is energetic and warm, with something a little playful and mysterious in the face-like symmetry. It is not a quiet piece, so choose it if you want the wall to have a presence.

You don't need to know anything. If the colour stopped you and you want to look again, that is reason enough. I believe there is no good or bad art, only what you feel in front of it. You may see a face in this one, or you may not, and either is fine.

Picture the wall that has bothered you for months, with red and white glowing over the sofa and the dark oval at the centre drawing the eye to the middle of the room. When a guest asks where you got it, you can say you chose it because it stopped you.
