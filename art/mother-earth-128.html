A Blue That Looks Like Light on Wet Glass

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Cobalt Tide is a horizontal digital abstract: a glassy blue center ringed by tiny spirals, with red-orange and green ribbons curving around it on deep navy.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall above it still feels unfinished. Whatever hung there before looks small now, or it never felt like yours.

Picture a large, rounded mass of cobalt and royal blue in the middle of that wall, with white highlights rippling through it like light on wet glass. Red-orange and green ribbons curve around it in the dark. That is Cobalt Tide.

I'll tell you what I see when I stand in front of this one. The blue center comes first. It is big and rounded, and the highlights in it, white and pale lavender, look wet. Small spiral whorls gather around its edge like eddies.

Then the ribbons pull your eye outward. A red-orange sweep rises from the lower left. Golden orange, emerald and forest green, and a pale rose-beige lobe wrap around the blue. A few small yellow dots sit near the right. Fine contour lines mark where each band meets the next, so the colors stay distinct instead of blurring into one soft blend.

The longer you look, the more small ripples and reflections turn up along the ribbons. Some people see a face or head in the blue center. Others see only liquid and light. Either reading is fine. The colors carry the piece.

The horizontal format suits the wall over a sofa or a bed, or the space above a console or desk. The dark navy edges frame the colors and give it presence from across the room. The blue center and the contrast mean it reads as a focal point rather than background.

The mood is lively and a little dreamlike. If your room is calm and neutral, the saturated color will be the first thing anyone sees.
