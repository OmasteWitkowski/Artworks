Glowing white stars drifting through melting red and green

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Ember Constellation is a horizontal abstract of liquid crimson, forest green and cream, with thin white antler-like lines and small glowing stars floating across it.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall still looks unfinished every time you walk past. Maybe the old piece looks small now, or maybe you've been waiting months for something that fits.

This is the piece I'd put there if the room could use some colour. Deep crimson sits on the upper left and forest green gathers on the right. Between them, thin white lines curl like antlers, and small stars glow as if lit from behind.

From across the room you see colour first. The red is saturated and warm, the green is deep, and the cream opens up in the lower center. They don't meet in hard edges. They melt into one another like liquid currents, with touches of dusty blue-grey, violet and peach in the transitions.

Step closer and the white shows up. Thin, crisp lines branch and curl across the surface. They look like antlers or tendrils, and they sweep from the lower left toward the upper right. Small star shapes are scattered through the upper and lower areas, some almost like starfish. They carry the brightest highlights in the image, white edged with pale gold, so they stand out against the darker red and green.

Those stars lead your eye from one to the next around the frame. I like that the smooth, glossy colour sits right beside the sharp white lines. It's easy to find the red and green festive, and you're welcome to. It also works as rich colour with no meaning attached. How you read it is up to you.

The image is horizontal and full-bleed, with colour across the whole surface. That suits the wide wall over a sofa, a bed or a console, or above a mantel. The red band at the upper left anchors the image, and the cool green on the right balances it.

Because the colour is so strong, the piece can set the palette for the room around it. It's a lively, dreamlike abstract for a wall that needs a warm focal point.

I don't think there's a right or wrong answer. Your own response is the only test. If the image stopped you, that's a good reason to want it.
