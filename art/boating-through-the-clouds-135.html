One tiny boat, one thin wake, a whole wall of silver water

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Quiet Crossing is a wide, textured image of a small green boat on open silver-blue water, where the empty space is the point.

The new sofa is in, and the wall above it looks bare. Or the old picture, which was fine in the old room, now looks small. You have probably already scrolled past a dozen busy images that all look alike.

Now picture a wall with room in it: pale silver-grey water across the whole width, and one small dark green boat just below the centre. A single thin wake runs behind it. That is Quiet Crossing.

The boat is the first thing you see, and it is tiny. One person sits in it. Under the hull is a faint reflection, and behind it a narrow dark wake trails off to the right.

Then the water takes over. Pale, reflective patches alternate with darker blue bands, and the deepest navy gathers along the top edge. The wake slowly fades into the ripples around it, so the movement is quiet and sideways against an almost still field.

Look closer and the whole surface carries a fine, wavy texture with a pressed, oil-paint-like grain. It gives the water a tactile finish that most pictures of boats and water don't have.

I'm not going to tell you where this is or what the person is doing. The image doesn't say, and I'd rather leave that to you.

This is a horizontal piece, so it suits the wide wall spaces: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The muted slate, silver and navy sit easily with neutral rooms and with other blues and greys, and nothing in the image competes with the room around it.

It is a picture for someone who wants restraint on the wall instead of a busy scene. The mood it supports is solitude, stillness and quiet scale.
