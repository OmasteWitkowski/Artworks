An eye-like pink oval, glowing in orange and navy

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · square

Molten Eye is a square abstract in burnt orange, salmon pink and near-black navy, built around one ringed oval that gives your eye a place to rest.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is done, and the space above the console, the bed or the entry still looks unfinished. Whatever you hang there has to hold its own, and the safe, pale option you keep almost buying would just disappear.

This is the piece I would put there. It is a square of burnt orange, deep red and salmon pink, with dark navy pressing in from the right. It looks glossy and lit from behind, and it holds up from across the room.

From across the room you see warm color and dark shapes. Come closer and the left side resolves into a pale pink oval with nested outlines in dark red-brown. Each ring sits inside the last and draws your eye inward in a slow spiral.

Some people see a stretched eye. Others see a slice through a tree trunk. Both are only resemblances, a play of form, and you are free to find your own.

Then your eye travels. A band of orange and red runs down the middle, smooth and liquid-looking. On the right, a navy-black shape sits over a dark olive-brown mass scattered with tiny white specks that catch the light. Warm and glowing meets dark and absorbing, and the picture stays in balance.

The image fills the square edge to edge, with no horizon and no background, so it works as a single focal piece. It suits a spot where you want one confident, warm accent: above a console, over a bed, or in a stairwell or entry.

The strong contrast between warm and dark carries across a room. It sits comfortably against deep blue, charcoal or neutral walls and furniture, and it picks up warm wood and rust tones.

Screens differ, so I can't promise an exact match. The piece is built on burnt orange, deep red and salmon pink against near-black navy, and those relationships should carry through. If it isn't right in your room, Fine Art America offers a 30-day money-back guarantee.

Your own response is the only test that counts. I believe there is no good or bad art, only what you feel in front of it. If this image stops you and you can picture it on your wall, that is reason enough. Nothing about it needs explaining.

Picture the wall again, with a glowing pink oval ringed in fine lines, a channel of orange running down through it and the navy deepening the room around it. When a guest asks about it, you will have something to point to, and something they will want to stand closer to.
