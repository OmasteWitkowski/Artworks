Green Ovals Glowing Against Molten Orange, Framed in Maroon

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Emerald Ember Windows is a mirrored digital abstract: one green form repeated three times, burning against orange and red inside stacked maroon frames.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall above it still looks unfinished. You have probably held a few ideas up to it in your head and none of them held.

This is the piece I would hold up next. A deep green oval sits at the center, with two mirrored panels on either side. Around them, orange and red swirls glow against a dark maroon frame.

At first it reads as one bold, ordered shape: a central oval, two matching panels and a frame that steps inward like a row of windows. Then the details begin to move.

The same glossy green form appears three times, once in the center and once in each side panel. Small spiral curls sit on the panel forms, and mirrored swirls of orange, red and golden yellow turn outward around them in a slow, blooming rotation.

The orange and yellow look lit from behind, like light passing through colored glass. The green shapes hold softer highlights inside them. Faint touches of peach, lavender and olive sit in the details, and a fine canvas-like weave runs across the surface.

I think the pull here is simple. It is rich color, held steady by symmetry. You do not need a story to want it. If it stops you, that is reason enough.

The format is horizontal, with a strong central axis, so it suits a main wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a fireplace. The deep maroon frame anchors it, and the orange and green bring energy to a room that needs warmth.

The mood is warm, rich and celebratory, with an ordered calm underneath. It looks best in a room that can carry saturated color, and it can be the first bold thing in one that cannot yet.
