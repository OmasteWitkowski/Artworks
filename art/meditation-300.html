A blue abstract with a centre that looks back at you

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Cobalt Echo is a mirrored blue abstract with two dark oval eyes under a glowing pale heart, made for one wide wall.

You bought the sofa, and now the wall above it looks bare. Or the old picture has started to look small in the room. You know the wall needs something with a centre, something that holds the space without fighting the furniture.

Cobalt Echo is a wide, horizontal piece with a strict mirror line down the middle. Two dark navy ovals sit under a glowing pale pink-white shape, with glossy blue swirls all around them. Most people see a face or a mask. Many people look twice.

The first thing you see is the blue: electric cobalt, bright azure and deep navy. Then the centre pulls you in. Two dark openings sit side by side beneath a pale, heart-like glow. The whole image is mirrored exactly left to right, so it feels balanced and a little watchful.

I'm happy to leave the face reading open. It comes from the symmetry, and nothing in the image says what it must be. You might see a mask, a creature, a flower or only a pattern. Whatever you see is the right reading.

Then the swirls take over. Large spirals curl in the lower corners, ribbons of colour ripple outward from the centre, and scrolling forms run along the bottom edge. The layered shapes are translucent, so the whole piece seems lit from behind. Look closer and you'll find fine lines inside the swirls, and a few small, mottled patches on the left and right that are easy to miss at first.

This is a horizontal piece with a centre line, so it suits a wide, low wall: over a sofa, a bed or a long console. The symmetry lines up with the furniture below, so the piece looks settled and placed on purpose.

The cool blues look strong against white or warm neutrals, and the deep navy gives the piece weight. It is bold and a little mysterious, and it is also simply striking as a pattern. You don't need a reason beyond liking it.
