Blue so deep it makes the amber look lit from behind

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Cobalt Ember is an abstract of velvety cobalt blue beside a glowing band of amber, gold and rust, with fine veins, tiny bubbles and ripples to find up close.

The sofa is in. The paint is done. And the wall above it still looks like it is waiting for something. Most of what you find to fill it looks like what hangs in everyone else's house.

This is not that. A deep cobalt blue fills the right side of the frame. Beside it, a band of amber, gold and rust seems to glow from behind, as if light were passing through glass or clear resin. You do not need to know what it is to feel it from across the room.

From across the room, it is a contrast. The blue absorbs the light and reads as dark and velvety. That makes the amber beside it seem to burn brighter. White glints run along the edges of the warm area and the green ring, and they sharpen the glow.

Step closer and the surface changes from place to place. Small clear bubbles cluster in the upper left. In the orange and violet center there is a fine, cracked, net-like pattern. Long veined streaks in the amber look like wood grain or agate banding, and ribbed ripples run across the blue.

Horizontal smears sweep through the middle and bend outward to the right, as if the surface were stretching. Rounded arches and rings in blue, green and yellow curve below and above, giving the whole picture a rolling rhythm.

I will not tell you what to see in it. No object is hiding here, just color, light and texture. It is a piece to enjoy simply for how it looks.

The horizontal format suits the long, open wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The calm blue side and the busy, glowing side give the eye somewhere to land and somewhere to rest, so the piece holds a wall without crowding it.

It is a saturated piece, best in a room that can take a bold accent. If your space is mostly neutral, the blue and amber will be the thing people notice.
