Is It a Microscope Slide or a River Delta from Above?

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Electric Delta is an abstract of one vivid blue channel branching through pitted, brown-rimmed ovals, and it can be read both ways.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one stretch of wall still looks bare, or the old picture hanging there has started to look small.

Now picture a long horizontal piece there. A bright electric-blue channel runs diagonally through the middle and splits like a river delta. Around it sit rounded, brown-rimmed shapes, some with a pale yellow-green glow inside.

From across the room you see color first. Electric blue and cobalt cut through deep brown, with rust and amber at the edges. The cool palette has warmth in it.

Step closer and the surface changes. It is finely pitted, scratched and speckled, with tiny bubbles. The raised, ridged rims look crusted, almost crystalline, and the edges catch the light with a metallic, oil-slick shine.

Then comes the question. It could be a slide under a microscope, or a frozen landscape seen from far above. I haven't named a subject, and the picture doesn't settle it. That is yours to decide, and it is a big part of why the eye keeps going back.

This is a horizontal piece, so it suits the walls that run long: over a sofa, above a console, behind a desk, or at an entry. The saturated blue gives the room a cool, confident statement, and the brown and rust keep it grounded.

It is dense and detailed rather than soft or washed, so it works best where you want the wall to have presence. It also gives you something to look at up close.

You don't need to know anything about art. I believe there is no good or bad art, and that your own response is the only test that counts. If the blue stopped you, that is reason enough.

I've left that open on purpose. It can look like a microscope slide or a landscape seen from above, and the picture doesn't choose between them. What you see in it is yours.

Picture that blue channel running across your wall, and the little yellow-green glows picking up the light as the day changes.
