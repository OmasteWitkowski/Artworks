The Tall Wall Piece That Glows Like Backlit Stained Glass

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · vertical

Jewel Totem is a tall, mirrored digital abstract: thick black outlines, marbled jewel-toned cells, and a glowing green and turquoise center.

You know the wall. It might be the stretch beside a doorway, the hallway you walk down every day, or the run beside the stairs. The room around it is finished, and that one strip still looks empty. Most art is too wide for it or too quiet to hold it.

This piece is tall, and it is built for that kind of wall. Deep maroon and navy cells stack down the middle. In the center is a glowing column of green and orange with a turquoise burst at its heart. It looks lit from behind, like light coming through colored glass.

The first thing I notice is the color. Burgundy, navy and cobalt carry most of the surface, so the bright parts glow all the more: golden yellow, orange, lime, emerald and turquoise. Violet and magenta close off the corners.

Then the black lines. They are thick, flat and smooth, and they run around every rounded, pebble-like cell the way leading holds the pieces of a stained-glass window. That is why the piece reads as glass, though it is listed as digital art.

Look closer and each cell is not a flat color. Inside the lines there are soft, marbled swirls that look like liquid or smoke. The outlines are fixed and ordered, and the color inside them moves.

Step back and the mirror symmetry takes over. The pattern becomes a figure, something like a totem or a mask, with the turquoise burst as its center. The yellow bands at the sides pulse outward in a ribbed rhythm. I leave what the figure means to you.

The vertical format and the dark maroon and navy base suit a hallway, a stairwell, or the space beside a doorway. The dark cells keep it grounded, and the bright center keeps it lively in a room of quieter colors.

It is a bold piece, and it is playful as well as rich. If you want color with order to it, rather than a loose, all-over wash, this is that.
