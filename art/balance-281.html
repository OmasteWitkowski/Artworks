A Blue Dome Pressed Against Ember Orange, Lit From Within

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Cobalt Dome and Ember Seam is a glossy abstract close-up of blue, orange, amber and magenta shapes meeting along thin bright edges.

You bought the sofa. The paint is right, the rug is down, and the wall above it still looks unfinished. Or the old picture that used to fit now looks small in a room that has grown around it.

Now picture a wide, deep cobalt blue shape filling the top of that wall. Below it, pools of burnt orange, amber and magenta glow along thin bright edges. That is Cobalt Dome and Ember Seam.

The blue comes first. It is a large rounded form, saturated and deep, with a thin dark line around its edge. It carries the whole upper half of the picture and holds its own from across a room.

Then the eye drops. The curves bend and pinch toward a narrow seam near the center, and that seam pulls your gaze down from the cool blue into the warm shapes below: burnt orange, amber, magenta, pale yellow-green and bright green.

Stay a little longer and the surfaces start to look smooth, glossy and see-through, as if light passes through them. Tiny round bubbles and fine specks sit in the orange, amber and magenta areas. From across the room you get color. Up close you get detail.

There is no horizon and no subject to name. The crop is tight, so it is only color and curve. I think that is enough reason to want it on a wall. If you feel something calm or something playful in it, that is yours to decide.

This is a horizontal piece, so it suits a wide wall: above a sofa, a bed, a console or a desk. The big blue and orange color fields are bold enough to anchor a living room, studio, office or entry.

The color carries a lot of energy, and the rounded shapes soften it. If your room is mostly neutral, this can be the one thing in it that has color. If your room already has blues or warm tones, the orange and blue may pick them up.
