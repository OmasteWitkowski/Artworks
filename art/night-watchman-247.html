A red curve wraps a cool teal form, and the edges glow

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · square

Ember and Tide is a square, glossy abstract in which deep red meets teal and blue, with bands of yellow, green and violet where they touch.

You know the wall. The sofa is in, the paint is settled, and that one space above it still looks unfinished. You've held up samples in your head and nothing has felt right, because so much of what's out there looks like everything else.

This is the image I'd put there. A large red curve sweeps across the right side and the top, wrapping around a cool shape of blue, teal and green. Nothing else is competing with it, so the colour does the work.

The first thing is the contrast. The red is deep and warm, almost ember-like. The blue-green is cool and layered, with fine streaks running through it. Where the two meet, thin bands of yellow, green and violet shimmer like light through a prism.

Then the glow. The surfaces look glossy and translucent, as if light were passing through them. Thin bright highlights trace the edges. Near the lower center a pale, leaf-like point glows, and that's where your eye lands and rests.

Nothing in the image tells you what it is, so you go by what you see. It looks like glass or liquid, but I'll leave that to you. Round, cell-like shapes are cut off at the corners, as if this were one small piece of something bigger. What it means is up to you.

The square format and strong colour suit a wall that needs a focal point, such as over a sofa, a bed or a console. With no horizon and no obvious subject, the image works as a block of colour and light, and it's easy to place.

The red and teal sit well against neutral walls and wood tones. The colours are rich enough that one piece can anchor the room, and the warm and cool halves balance each other, so it won't feel lopsided.
