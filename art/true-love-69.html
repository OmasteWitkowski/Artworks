A Warm Glow Held Between Two Deep Blues

Contemporary Abstract & Mixed Media · horizontal

Cobalt Ember is a rippled abstract of blue, gold and pink bands, with a glowing center that holds the eye.

You've got a wide wall that has looked bare since the sofa arrived, or old art that looks small in a bigger room. You've probably tried plenty of colorful abstracts. Most are one flat wash of color or a busy scatter with nothing to rest on.

This one is different at first glance. Deep cobalt blue holds both sides of the frame, and between them a warm band of golden yellow, amber and pink glows like light coming through stained glass.

I'll tell you what I see. Tall bands of color run down a horizontal frame, and they ripple as they fall, like melting paint or flame. Thin, dark, jagged lines separate one color field from the next.

Step closer and a fine, wavy, swirled texture covers the whole surface. Small curling squiggles trace the edges of the bands. In the upper right, the blue gathers into a gentle spiral.

There's no single focal point. Your eye travels from cool to warm and back to cool, and a dark maroon curve along the bottom holds the movement in place.

I won't tell you what it means. Art is felt, and what you take from it is yours.

The wide, horizontal format suits a sofa wall, a wall above a bed, a sideboard or console, or the end of a hallway. The saturated color carries across a room, so it reads from the doorway as well as up close.

If your walls and furniture lean neutral, the warm center gives the room a bright point of color. The blues give it a steady frame. The mood is energetic and lively.
