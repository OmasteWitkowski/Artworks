A Flower Drawn in Gold Line, Lit From Within

Botanical & Flowers · horizontal

Ember Rosette is a digital abstract: a thin golden outline of a spiraling flower glowing inside an oval, over molten orange, amber and brown swirls.

You have a wall that has bothered you for a while. Maybe the sofa is new and the old picture now looks small above it. Maybe the room is warm and settled, with wood, leather and brass, and the wall is the one part that still feels unfinished.

This is the piece I would hold up to that wall. It is a horizontal image with a glowing oval at the center and a flower drawn in a single thin line of gold. The line is only an outline, but the flower looks lit from within.

From across the room you see a glowing oval in a dark brown frame. It reads like a medallion or a cameo, with a soft golden halo around its edge.

Come closer and the flower sorts itself out. Layered, pointed petals spiral outward from a tight little bud, set slightly above the middle of the oval. It could be a rose or a succulent rosette. The image does not insist on either, and you do not have to choose.

Behind the line, glossy ribbons of burnt orange, amber, rust and chocolate brown curl and swirl like molten color. The brightest yellow-orange sits at the lower right and deepens to brown toward the upper left. The swirls outside the frame echo the spiral of the petals, so the flower and the color seem to move together.

The gold line is crisp and flat. The color behind it is smooth and liquid. That contrast is what holds me, and I think it is why the picture keeps drawing the eye back.

The image is horizontal, symmetrical and strongly centered. That makes it a natural focal piece over a sofa, a console, a bed or a desk, wherever you want one point for the eye to land.

The deep brown, amber and burnt orange will feel at home in rooms with warm woods, leather, brass or earthy textiles. It is a fiery picture but a settled one, and the dark frame keeps it from overwhelming a room.
